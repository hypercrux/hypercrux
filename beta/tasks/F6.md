# F6: Following other processes

Status: done, 8 October 2026.

## The contract

From BETA.md: following other processes: `stat`, reading on, applying
marked batches, the check when the writer is gone. Done when T4 shows every
reader seeing every commit in order, with writers killed at random.

## What was built

All of it is in `beta/internal/logfile` and `beta/internal/procs`.

- **`logfile/follow.go`,** new. The comment at its top sets out the rules.
  - `Follow`, the log's new call. It stats the path once, and when the path
    names the Log's file and the file ends where the log this process has
    applied ends (`seen`, which `publish` keeps), it returns without a lock.
    Otherwise it takes `fol`, tries the mutex without waiting, stats again,
    reads on (`followOn`), and when that stops short of the end of the file,
    tries the lock (`tryCheck`). When another holds the lock, it waits for
    it (`confirm`) only when the cheap test found something.
  - `followOn`, the follower's order: the head and the marker's place
    probed, the batch read through the window only once the marker is
    whole, and a batch that fails its checks under a whole marker read once
    more from its head before the cheap test counts it.
  - `ErrReplaced`, exported, in place of F4's `errReplaced`.
  - `waiting`, what Lock does between its tries at flock: it reads on.
  - `unmarked`, where a follower stops, and `shorter`, the damage of a
    shrunk file.
- **`log.go`:** `fol`, `seen` and `file` in `Log`. `letGo` publishes how far
  the log has been applied as the mutex goes, at every place that let it
  go. Lock publishes once it has caught up, and `flock` takes a function to
  call between its tries, which is `waiting` for Lock and nil for
  `confirm`. The comments on `Target`, `Options.Wait`, `Log` and `Lock` say
  so, and F6's note at the top gives way to a sentence on `follow.go`.
- **`read.go`:** `probe`, which reads only the bytes asked for when the
  window doesn't hold them, and `load` keeps the file's identity, as
  `fresh` does in `create.go`. **`check.go`**, **`damage.go`** and
  **`doc.go`:** `ErrReplaced`, and comments.
- **Ten planted bugs,** in `plant_on.go` and `beta/plants.txt`:
  `logfile/follow-before-marker`, `logfile/follow-keeps-unmarked`,
  `logfile/follow-without-mutex`, `logfile/follow-never-tries`,
  `logfile/follow-shrink-unseen`, `logfile/follow-path-unchecked`,
  `logfile/follow-no-confirm`, `logfile/follow-read-once`,
  `logfile/follow-stuck-checks` and `logfile/lock-waits-blind`. Two of them

- **The harness,** added to, with nothing taken away:
  - `Options.WriterGap`: a writer's slot stays empty for a while after its

  - `Writer.Count` and `Reader.Count`, a report of the kind `count`,

- **Tests:**
  - `logfile/follow_test.go`, new: the follower's cases one by one, listed

## The result

- **T4 shows every reader seeing every commit in order, with writers killed
  at random.** `TestFollowersSeeEveryCommitInOrder` runs two writers on the
  real log and two followers that open it once and follow it, for 2
  seconds. Each is killed with SIGKILL at a random moment and a new one
  started in its place, a writer after a gap of 20 to 150 milliseconds.
  Writers live 10 to 200 milliseconds, followers 50 milliseconds to a
  second, and every sync of a writer's takes a millisecond more, as on a
  slow disk. The run goes on until followers have marked 3 batches that
  killed writers left.

  | Run | Writers started and killed | Followers started and killed | Commits begun | Seen to succeed | Under way at a kill | Of those, in the file | In the file at the end | Commits the followers saw | Checks of the end by followers | Batches they marked |
  |---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
  | 1 | 22 | 7 | 634 | 617 | 17 | 11 | 628 | 2,959 | 10 | 10 |
  | 2 | 21 | 7 | 624 | 608 | 16 | 10 | 618 | 3,191 | 12 | 10 |
  | 3 | 23 | 8 | 595 | 580 | 15 | 10 | 590 | 3,132 | 11 | 9 |

  No follower saw a commit out of order, twice, or one that later went,
  the followers agreed with each other and with the file, and every commit
  a writer saw succeed was in the file. Of the 31 commits under way at a
  kill that were in the file at the end, followers marked 29, in the gaps
  with no writer: they found the batch the killed writer left synced and
  unmarked, got the lock when they tried, and checked the end of the log.
  No follower cut anything. A kill can't leave half a batch, since a batch
  goes in one write, so the cut is shown in `logfile`'s own tests.
  With `-short` a run takes 0.8 to 1.1 seconds. Without the slow disk, on
  this machine's ext4, kills land between a batch and its marker less
  often: six runs of 2 seconds made 1,197 to 1,357 commits and 3 to 6
  marks each.
- **`TestTheRealLogWithProcessesKilled` still passes:** 41 writers and 33
  readers killed, 1,343 commits begun, 1,320 seen to succeed, 23 under way

## Decisions settled

**The follower's API** is `(*Log).Follow() error`, with `ErrReplaced`.

- It makes one stat of the path, and when the path names the Log's file
  and its size is where the log this process has applied ends, it returns
  without a lock, at the cost of that stat and an atomic load. The Log
  publishes how far it has applied each time the mutex goes, and when Lock
  has caught up.
- With something to read, it stats again holding the mutex, since a stat
  made before the locks can be older than the read position: another
  follower, or a commit in this process, may have read further since.
- It never resets the Target. Another file at the path gives an error that
  wraps `ErrReplaced`, with the path, and reads nothing, for F9's reload.
  So does an empty file that something wrote into in place, which
  HyperCrux never does. Lock still moves to a new file by itself.
- With nothing at the path, it reads on in the file it holds, which a
  writer that locked it before it went can still commit to, and it checks
  nothing.
- A file shorter than the end of the log it has applied is damage at once:
  no writer cuts below a marker, so no read under the lock is needed.

**Its place beside the writer.** The read position belongs to the holder of
the write lock's mutex.

- The lock order is `fol`, then the mutex, then flock, then the Target's
  locks, which for the store are its writer's lock and then the copy's lock.
- Follow tries the mutex without waiting. When a follower holds it, a
  later Follow waits for it through `fol`, so a read never comes up short
  of one that began before it. When anything else holds it, Follow returns
  nil at once and reads nothing, since that would be a wait for an
  Update's function. A follower waits for the Target's locks only while it
  holds the mutex, when no Update is under way, and nobody holding the
  Target's locks waits for the mutex. So a read through the database
  inside an Update's function, which S2 allows before the first change,
  waits at most for a follower that finds the mutex held and lets go of
  `fol` at once, and can't deadlock. That holds as long as Follow is never
  called holding one of the Target's own locks, such as inside the
  store's `Read` (F9's note below).
- The holder reads on instead. Lock reads on between its tries at flock
  (`waiting`), which goes past the brief. Without it, while an Update's
  Lock waited behind other processes' commits, the reads in its process
  would have missed every one of them until the Lock got flock. Now a read
  misses at most what was committed since the Lock's last try, 16
  milliseconds at most before. Once a writer holds flock, it has read on to
  the end of the log and no other process can commit, so a read misses
  nothing.
- Append moves the read position inside the store's Commit, holding the
  mutex, so a follower never applies this process's own commit. S2.md's
  check after Begin isn't needed, since Follow applies only holding the
  mutex, and so never while an Update's transaction is open.

**Reading on.** For each batch, the head and the marker's place are read
first, by themselves unless the window holds them, and the batch only once
the marker is whole, through the window, so a catch-up over many batches
takes few reads, and a long commit under way costs 48 bytes a read at
most. Bytes read through the window together can hold a batch caught half
written and the marker written a moment later, so a batch that fails its
checks under a whole marker is read once more from its head, with fresh
reads, before it counts as suspect. Nothing of a batch without its marker
is kept.

**The writer gone, and damage.** When Follow stops short of the end of the
file, it tries the lock without waiting, and when it gets it, checks the
end of the log as Open does (`tryCheck`). When another holds the lock, the
cheap test looks at the batch it stopped at alone: a whole marker after it
naming it while the batch fails its checks, or naming another batch, or the
same batch with another checksum, while the batch counts. Only then does it
wait for the lock and read again (`confirm`). It lets go of `fol` first, so
other followers find the mutex held and read the copy as it is, instead of
waiting with it. A marked batch whose changes are malformed, or that the
Target refuses, is damage at once, as `readOn` has it.

**A stuck handle follows** marked batches as any follower does, and stops
there. It never tries the lock, which it holds already, and so never checks
the end of the log, which would write, or lets go of flock. No other process
can commit while it's stuck, so there's nothing of theirs to follow. Its
own failed batch is applied only if its marker reached the file whole,
which is what every other process reads too.

**The harness's additions.** `WriterGap` gives each writer's slot a gap after
its writer ends, so with gaps long beside the lives there are moments with
no writer. `Count` and `Least` let a workload count events of its own and
make a run go on until it has enough of them, which turns "followers found
the writer gone" into something a run has to show. The followed workload's
writers sync on a slow disk, as T4's toy log pauses, so the test means the
same on a disk with fast syncs.

## Notes for later tasks

- **F9, the public package's reads and reloading:**
  - Call `db.log.Follow()` before each read through `DB`: `Get`, `Scan`,
    `Neighbours`, `Walk`, `Nearest`, `Check`, and the SQL reads once G4
    has them. Reads through `Tx` needn't, since the Update caught up under
    the lock. Inside an Update's function, Follow returns nil at once.
  - Call it before the store's `Read`, and outside its callback. Follow's
    batches go in through `ApplyBatch`, which takes the copy's lock alone,
    and inside the callback it would wait for the read's own shared hold
    on it.
  - An error that wraps `logfile.ErrReplaced` asks for a reload, then a
    Follow again. Return damage, `ErrLockTimeout` and other errors from the
    read, as Open fails with them; the copy is as the last applied batch
    left it.
  - The reload can be a Log method holding the mutex that does what Open's
    loop does after `ErrReplaced`: close the old file, `Reset`, the garbage
    collection, `load`, then `openCheck`. `reopen` is most of it, and Lock
    already uses it. Publish when it lets go of the mutex (`letGo`).
  - `glue.go`'s `target.Apply` takes `ApplyBatch` once Open is done, which
    is right for Follow's batches, and for those Lock reads on while it
    waits for flock, which come before the Update begins its transaction.
  - G1's note: `TestTwoHandlesOnOneFile` can drop its empty Updates once
    reads follow. `betax`'s skip for ProcessesShareAFile, which says it
