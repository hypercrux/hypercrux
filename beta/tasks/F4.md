# F4: Damage

Status: done, 8 October 2026.

## The contract

From BETA.md: damage: reported in the middle with nothing cut, a look past
zeros before cutting, a bad marked batch read again under the lock. Done
when every damaged fixture is reported and nothing is cut.

## What was built

All of it is in `beta/internal/logfile`, apart from one function added to
`beta/internal/format`.

- **`format.FindMarker`,** in `marker.go`: the first whole marker in some
  bytes, at any offset, found with `bytes.Index` on the magic number.
  `TestFindMarker` tests it, and `FuzzDecodeMarker` now holds it to
  `fixtures_test.go`'s reader too. Nothing else in `format` changed.
- **`damage.go`,** new:
  - `pastEnd`, step 1 of the check: `inCompacted`, the rule for a
    compacted file, then `markerPast`, the search from the end of the log
    to the end of the file for a whole marker naming the next batch or a
    later one.
  - `openCheck`, Open's check: `tryCheck`, and when another holds the
    lock, `suspect`, the look without it, then `confirm`, the wait for the
    lock and the read holding it.
  - `endBatch` and `otherMarker`, the first batch after the end of the log
    and a whole marker naming another batch after it, which `markLeft` and
    `suspect` share. And `errReplaced`.
- **`check.go`:** `checkEnd` makes step 1 at its top through `pastEnd`, and
  the compacted-part rule moved there. `markLeft` uses `endBatch` and
  `otherMarker`, and does what it did. `tryCheck` says whether it got the
  lock, and `checkLocked` returns `errReplaced` when another file has taken
  the path.
- **`log.go`:** Open's loop calls `openCheck`, and after `errReplaced`
  resets the Target and reads the file at the path from its start.
  `Options.Wait` covers Open's wait. The comment at the top, and Open's and
  Lock's, say where F4 is.
- **`read.go`:** `load` returns the size it read with. `readOn` is as it
  was; its F4 comment gave way to one saying who looks past the end, and a
  note for F6.
- **`doc.go`** mentions damage.
- **The damaged fixtures,** in `testdata/damaged`: 29 files, `damage.txt`
  and a README. `damaged_test.go` makes them and checks them.
- **Five planted bugs,** in `plant_on.go` and `beta/plants.txt`:
  `logfile/cut-at-zeros`, `logfile/damage-unconfirmed`,
  `logfile/window-edge`, `logfile/any-sequence-number` and
  `logfile/compacted-held-only`.
- **Tests:**
  - `damaged_test.go`, new: `TestTheDamagedFixtures`, the closing test.
  - `damage_test.go`, new: `TestAReaderReadsAgainUnderTheLock` and
    `TestTheLookPastFindsAMarkerAnywhere`.
  - `check_test.go`: the four `f4` cases in `endCases` are damage, and the
    field is gone. Three cases are new: a torn batch holding a copy of the
    database, which is cut, and two that are damage, a batch's head of
    zeros with its marker and another batch after it, and a batch that
    counts without its marker with a marked batch after it. The Lock half
    of `TestTheCheckOfTheEndOfTheLog` opens the file as far as the end of
    its log, then writes the rest in place. `holdLock`'s release runs once,
    and `releaseAfter` lets go on a timer.
  - `log_test.go`: `TestReadingStopsAtTheEndOfTheLog` holds the lock
    throughout for the cases with no damage, and lets go after 50 ms for
    the damage cases, which Open reports once it holds the lock.
  - `crash_test.go`: the closing test's comment says what it catches now.

## The result

- **Every damaged fixture is reported, and nothing is cut.**
  `TestTheDamagedFixtures` reads each of the 29 fixtures in four ways:
  Open, which gets the lock at once; Open while another holds the lock and
  lets go 30 ms later; Lock, once the fixture is moved into place over the
  Log's file; and Lock on a Log that opened the file when it held only its
  header, then found the rest written in place. The last can't be done for
  the two with damaged headers and the two compacted ones. All 112 reads
  report the batch and offset `damage.txt` gives, and every file's bytes
  are as they were after each. For the 20 fixtures whose damage a writer
  at work could explain, Open while another held the lock reported it only
  after the lock came free.

  | Damage | Fixtures | Found by |
  |---|---|---|
  | The header: its checksum, a compacted part past the end of the file | 2 | the header's checks |
  | A batch that counts, its change malformed or breaking the rules for a change on its own, marked or not | 5 | the codec |
  | A marked batch putting into a table that doesn't exist | 2 | the Target |
  | A batch that counts, then a whole marker naming the batch before | 2 | step 2 at the end, step 1 in the middle |
  | A marked batch failing a check: checksum, magic, generation, length, sequence number | 10 | step 1 |
  | A head of zeros, a batch of zeros, a batch missing, a marker damaged with batches after it | 6 | step 1 |
  | In a compacted file, a log that ends inside the compacted part | 2 | the compacted-part rule |

  Opened through the public package and the real store, in a test written
  for the purpose and then removed, every fixture gave the same batch and
  offset, and stayed as it was.
- **The cases waiting for F4 are damage.** The four `f4` cases, through Open
  and Lock. In `TestReadingStopsAtTheEndOfTheLog`, the nine damage cases of
  `endCases`, among them the two F2.md named, are reported once Open has
  waited for the lock; the other 17 return with the lock held throughout.
- **The crash tests.** With step 1 in, `TestTheRealLogPassesACutAtEveryWrite`
  catches `logfile/marker-before-sync` by cuts, as F3.md's trial said: a
  new database at `cut WriteAt 6 seed 22`, and four of the other five
  workloads within their first seven seeds. "A batch cut short" passes its
  32 seeds. Its line in `plants.txt` names `./beta/internal/logfile`, which
  holds the test, so it needed nothing more. Without the bug, every cut
  opens as before: the six workloads give F3's table exactly, with one more
  call a run in the four where a check has a tail to look through.
  `TestTheRealLogAfterAFailedCall` makes 1,824 and 1,224 failures, and
  `TestCopiesOfTheRealLog` 180 copies on the plain workload, all allowed.
- **Reading again under the lock.** `TestAReaderReadsAgainUnderTheLock`,
  with the writer's moves made at Open's tries at the lock, so nothing hangs
  on timing: a batch read half written with its marker after it, which the
  writer finishes before letting go, is read whole and applied; a torn end
  cut, or a batch committed, between Open's read and its look is no reason
  to wait; a torn end that turns into bad changes is reported once the lock
  comes free; a wait that runs out gives `errs.ErrLockTimeout`, naming

  wait is read instead.
- **The look past finds a marker anywhere.**
  `TestTheLookPastFindsAMarkerAnywhere`, on a file held in memory: a marker
  naming the next batch at 27 offsets, just past the end of the log, on

  and ending the file, past zeros and past magic numbers whose check values
  don't fit. Markers naming batch 1, of another database or of a later

- **The planted bugs are caught,** all 13 in `logfile`, each run alone with

  - `any-sequence-number`: by the torn batch holding a copy of the

  - `compacted-held-only`: by `compacted-part-end`, read while another held

- **Bugs put in by hand,** one at a time: of 19, the tests caught all 19.
  Among them: the sequence-number rule off by one either way, the search
  starting a marker late, an overlap a byte short, a single window, a reader

## Decisions settled

**Where the look past the end of the log is made.**

- **Holding the lock,** at the top of `checkEnd`, before `markLeft` writes
  anything: the compacted-part rule, then the search for a marker. Every
  Lock and every check Open makes holding the lock goes through it. A whole
  log reads nothing there.
- **Open, whether or not it gets the lock:** through the check when it gets
  it, and without it (`suspect`) when another holds it.
- **Where `readOn` stops,** its callers look: Open after `load`, and
  `checkEnd` after `catchUp`. `readOn` doesn't know whether the lock is
  held, and its other callers, `reopen` and `fill`, are in `lockFile`,
  which takes the lock and checks straight after, so the look comes once,
  there.

**Finding the magic number quickly.** `format.FindMarker` runs `bytes.Index`
on `HCRM` and tries `DecodeMarker` at each hit. `markerPast` reads the file
through the log's window, a megabyte at a time, each read starting 19 bytes
before the one before it ended, so a marker across the edge is whole in the
second. Memory stays at one window, and a run of zeros goes by at the speed
of memory.

**What the damage names.** The batch that should come next, at the offset
where the log ends: the first batch that can't be read. The reason gives
the marker's offset and the batch it names, such as "batch 2 at offset 159:
a whole marker at offset 229 names batch 2, so a commit was made past the
end of the log". The compacted-part damage was named that way already.
Step 2's keeps F3's naming, the batch that counts at its marker's offset.

**A marker naming an earlier batch** is passed over, so a copy of the
database stored in a torn batch never passes for a commit. One naming the
next batch or a later one is damage.

**A marked batch that fails its checks** is found by the search, since its
marker names the next batch. Holding the lock, that's damage. `readOn` still
stops there, as it stops at any batch that doesn't count.

**Open when another holds the lock.**

- It looks for what may be damage, up to the size its read used, so a
  commit that lands after the read shows nothing: step 1's two rules; a
  batch that counts followed by a whole marker naming another, which is
  step 2's damage; and a batch at the end that now counts with bad
  changes, though it didn't when it was read.
- On finding one, it waits for flock until `Options.Wait` is over, through
  `flock`, as Lock waits. The deadline is kept if Open starts again on
  another file.
- **Holding the lock,** it does what it does when it gets the lock at once
  (`checkLocked`): the inode check, the check that what was read is still
  there, the read on, and the check of the end, all of it. So the read is
  final, and when the damage is gone, the end is marked or cut as a writer
  would leave it.
- **When the wait runs out,** Open fails with an error that wraps
  `errs.ErrLockTimeout` and names the batch, the offset and the reason. It
  doesn't wrap `errs.ErrDamaged`, since nothing confirmed the damage.
- **When another file has taken the path** by the time Open holds the lock,
  at once or after the wait, Open resets the Target and reads the file at
  the path from its start. This changes F3's choice for the try without
  waiting, so that every file Open returns has been looked past. With
  nothing at the path, Open returns what it read, as F3 had it.
- Damage no writer at work can explain is reported at once, as before: a
  damaged header, and a batch that counts whose changes are malformed or
  break the rules, the codec's or the Target's.

**The compacted-part rule for readers** is in `suspect` too, so a reader
never takes part of a damaged compacted part for a whole database.

**The fixtures** are binary `.hcx` files, so `hypercrux check` can read them
as they are, with `damage.txt` saying, one a line, the file, the batch, the
offset and what's damaged. `damagedFixtures` builds them from two small
databases with the codec, and the offsets in its table come from the layout,
apart from the log's code. `HYPERCRUX_LOGFILE_RECORD=1` writes them, as the
repository's other golden files are written, and the test fails when the
folder differs from what the table makes. The two that break the rules for
the state need a Target that knows its tables: `tables` in the test refuses
a change to a table no create change made, as the store does.

## Notes for later tasks

- **F5, failed commits:** a failed commit's batch, cut back and written
  again by the next commit, is the race `confirm` is for. A case for it can
  join `TestAReaderReadsAgainUnderTheLock`, with the writer's moves at
  Open's tries. `failed` is as F3 left it.
- **F6, following:** a follower that stops short of the end makes the cheap
  check FORMAT.md gives it, a whole marker after the batch it stopped at
  that names it while the batch fails its checks, then `confirm`; `readOn`
  has a comment there. `suspect` searches the whole tail, which suits
  opening; a follower would pay for that at every commit under way.
  `tryCheck` returns `errReplaced` now, which a follower takes for a
  reload. A follower's `confirm` holds the mutex for up to `Options.Wait`.
- **F8, compaction:** `confirm` waits through `flock`, so the wait past the
  deadline while `NAME.compact` is locked applies to it too. A reader
  waiting there when the compacted file is renamed in reads that one.
- **F9, reloading:** Open's start again after `errReplaced` is a small
  reload, a `Reset` then `load`, and the garbage collection could go
  between them.
- **G1 and the public package:** `Open` can now wait up to 10 seconds when
  another process holds the lock and the file looks damaged past the end
  of its log, then fail with `ErrLockTimeout`. `hypercrux.Open`'s comment
  could say so. No test in `beta/hypercrux` or `beta/conformance/betax`
  needed a change.
- **G7, `check`:** the fixtures are in
  `beta/internal/logfile/testdata/damaged`, which a test in `beta/hypercrux`
  reaches as `../internal/logfile/testdata/damaged`. `check` can hold each
  file's report to the batch and offset in `damage.txt`. The README there
  says how to change them.
- **T4 and I1:** a reader that opens the file while a writer works finds no
  damage unless there is some. A false suspicion, which a failed commit's
  replaced batch could give, costs a wait for the lock, and never a report.
- **R1, long runs:** with more seeds, "a batch cut short" may catch
  `logfile/marker-before-sync` by cuts too.
