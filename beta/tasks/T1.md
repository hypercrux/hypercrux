# T1: Fault layer for data

Status: done, 8 October 2026.

## The contract

From BETA.md: the fault layer for data: unsynced writes lost or torn at a
simulated power cut, and any call made to fail at its nth use. Done when its
own tests show synced data surviving every cut.

## What was built

All of it is in `beta/internal/fault`, a new package that imports `fsys`
alone from the Beta.

- **`disk.go`:** `Disk`, a drive held in memory with a page cache in front
  of it, and `New(seed)`. `FS()` gives the file system as one process sees
  it until the next cut. `Cut` cuts the power between calls, `Add` sets a
  rule and `Clear` removes them, `Mkdir` makes folders for a test's setup,
  and `Calls`, `Cuts` and `Failed` count. `SectorSize` (512), `MaxSize`
  (1 GiB) and `ErrCut`. `Disk`'s comment sets out the whole model.
- **`inode.go`:** a file's two copies of its data, the cache and the drive,
  with its pending sectors and its sizes, and what a sync, a failed sync and
  a cut do to them.
- **`fs.go`:** the 18 calls of `fsys.FS` and `fsys.File` on the disk.
- **`op.go`:** `Op`, a kind for each of the 18 calls, and `Any`; `Rule`,
  which picks a call by kind, path and number, then fails it or cuts the
  power in it; the counting.
- **`sectors.go`:** a set of sector numbers held as sorted runs, so a file
  written from its start to its end is one run.
- **Five planted bugs,** in `plant_on.go` and `beta/plants.txt`, each making
  the disk kinder than its model or breaking what the crash tests lean on:
  `fault/cut-keeps-everything`, `fault/torn-with-zeros`,
  `fault/clean-pages-written`, `fault/second-open-locks` and
  `fault/nth-plus-one`.
- **Tests:**
  - `cut_test.go`: the closing test, with an oracle written from `Disk`'s
    comment alone that knows, byte by byte, which bytes a cut must keep;
    long random runs; every sector after a cut held to the model; a cut in a
    write and in a truncate; what a cut does to the processes running.
  - `fail_test.go`: every call failing at its nth use; `Times` and `Clear`;
    what failed calls leave behind; the failed-sync case.
  - `lock_test.go`: flock across opens, processes, renames, removals and a
    cut, and goroutines contending for it under the race detector.
  - `seed_test.go`: the same seed giving the same disk, cut by cut.
  - `names_test.go`: the calls on names, the file calls the crash tests
    don't make, and the counts.
  - `sectors_internal_test.go`: the sector set against a plain map.

## The result

- **Synced data survives every cut.** `TestSyncedDataSurvivesEveryCut` runs
  8 fixed workloads of 90 calls on three files: writes at the end, inside
  and past the end, truncates both ways, syncs that work, syncs a rule
  fails, and reads. Each runs once with the power cut in each of its calls,
  and once with it cut after the last, with 6 seeds each time: 4,368 cuts
  (546 with `-short`). After every cut each file has a size it had since
  its last good sync, and every byte that sync put on the drive, with
  nothing written over it since, reads back as it was. The oracle counts a
  byte that went through a failed sync as promised by no later sync, which
  is the model's own rule.
- `TestSyncedDataSurvivesLongRuns` takes 6 disks through 6,000 random steps
  each, with the power cut between calls or in one: 1,787 cuts and 5,171
  failed writes and syncs, each run carrying on from what its earlier cuts
  left. Synced data survived every cut there too.
- **Unsynced data is lost or torn as the model says.** In 600 seeds of a
  synced file with writes over it and past its end, every sector after the
  cut is as the drive held it when nothing touched it, and otherwise kept,
  lost, or torn at one point. All of them came up: 1,407 sectors kept,
  1,387 lost, 161 torn with the new bytes first and 157 with them second;
  sizes as synced 194 times, as reads saw them 212 times and between 194
  times; files kept whole 215 times, lost whole 217 and mixed 165. A cut in
  a write and a cut in a truncate give the same spread.
- **Every call fails at its nth use.** The script makes calls of all 18
  kinds in three rounds. Run once per call with a rule failing it, by kind
  across every path, by kind on one path, by place among all calls, and by
  path across every kind, it fails at exactly the call picked, 132 times,
  with the rule's error wrapped with that call's path, and every call before
  it succeeds.
- **The failed-sync case:** of 400 seeds with a failed sync and then a good
  one, 90 lost every unsynced sector at the cut, 213 kept them all and 97
  mixed the two. Written again after the failure, the bytes survived in
  every seed. Data synced before the failure survived in every seed, and
  the size stayed pending, as synced 131 times and as written 147 times.
- **flock across opens** follows Linux's rules, and four goroutines with an
  open each never held it at once.
- **The same seed gives the same disk:** 8 seeds through 3,000 steps each,
  run twice, gave the same contents after every cut, and no two seeds gave
  the same.
- **The tests catch a wrong disk.** The five planted bugs are caught, and
  `sh scripts/planted.sh` reports 11 of 11. Of 25 bugs put in by hand one
  at a time, such as a sync that writes no sectors, a cut that keeps the
  flocks, a write whose gap isn't pending, a cut that settles files in map
  order, or a rename counted for one path, the tests caught 24. The other
  turned a failed sync's "left pending" sectors into "marked clean", which
  nothing outside the disk can tell apart, since a pending sector can be
  kept, lost or torn at a cut as well.
- `go vet ./...`, `go test -short ./...`, `go test -race` on
  `beta/internal/fault` and `sh scripts/check-beta.sh` pass. The package's
  tests take about 1.5 seconds, 5.5 under `-race -short` and 34 under
  `-race`.

## Decisions settled

**What sits underneath.** A `Disk` holds every file twice: the page cache,
which reads see, and the drive, which holds what the last sync put there.
A `WriteAt`, and the change of size a `Truncate` makes, mark the sectors
they touch as pending, gaps included. A good `Sync` puts every pending
sector on the drive with the size. Files live in memory, so `MaxSize`
refuses anything past 1 GiB with EFBIG.

**What a cut does,** to each file in the order of inode numbers:

- **The unit is the sector, 512 bytes,** the smallest drives write. Each
  pending sector is kept, lost or torn on its own, since a drive can write
  the sectors in its cache in any order. Kept, it holds what reads saw;
  lost, what the drive held.
- **A torn sector** holds one version up to a point and the other after it,
  either way round. The point falls between the first and the last bytes
  where the two differ, so a torn sector always matches neither; with fewer
  than two such bytes it's kept or lost.
- **How often:** a third of the time every pending sector of a file is
  kept, a third of the time every one is lost, and otherwise each is kept,
  lost or torn on its own, a third of the time each. Whole outcomes come up
  often that way, and mixed ones too.
- **Bytes no pending change touched** keep what the drive held, even inside
  a torn sector, so synced data always survives. SQLite calls this
  powersafe overwrite and assumes it by default, and BETA.md assumes drives
  that honour cache flushes.
- **A cut in a call:** in a `WriteAt` or a `Truncate` it comes once the call
  has reached the cache, so the change is pending at the cut; that covers a
  write half done, since any of its sectors can be lost. In any other call
  the cut comes first. The call returns `ErrCut`.

**Sizes.** At a cut a file's size becomes the one its last good sync left,
the one reads saw, or any size between the smallest and the largest it has
had since that sync, a third of the time each. That covers a growing file
whose size reached the drive with only some of its data, as file systems
can leave it, and a truncate that never reached it. Past what the drive
held, a file reads as zeros.

**A failed sync** settles the pending sectors as Linux can after an error
writing them back: each is written to the drive, torn there, marked clean
without reaching it, or left pending for the next sync. A quarter of the
time every sector is marked clean, a quarter left pending, a quarter
written, and otherwise each gets one of the four on its own. A sector torn
or marked clean stays as the drive holds it through later good syncs, while
reads go on seeing what was written, until something writes to the sector
again. That's FORMAT.md's reason to write a batch again. The size stays
pending.

**Other failed calls change nothing,** apart from three. A failed `WriteAt`
writes a prefix of what it was given, anything from none to all but one
byte, and returns its length; a failed `ReadAt` reads a prefix the same
way, as `io.WriterAt` and `io.ReaderAt` allow. A failed `Close` closes the
file and lets go of its flock all the same, as Linux's close does.

**Picking a call.** A `Rule` names a kind (`Any` for every kind), a path or
none, the nth matching call, and an error (EIO when none is given), or
`Cut` in place of the error. It counts matching calls from when it's set,
and `Times` fails several calls in a row from the nth, or every one after
it, as a dead or full disk keeps failing. A cut wins over a failure on the
same call. The error is wrapped as the os package wraps its own: an
`*fs.PathError` with the system call's name and the path, or an
`*os.LinkError` for a rename, so `errors.Is` finds the rule's error.

**Counting calls.** `Calls(op, path)` counts every call that reached the
disk, failed or not, since `New`. A call on an open file counts on the path
it was opened or created with, even after a rename, so a handle's counts
don't move under it; a rename counts on both its paths. A call on a closed
file doesn't reach the disk and isn't counted, and nor is one from a
process a cut killed. So a test runs its work once, reads `Calls`, and runs
again on a fresh `New(seed)` with a rule for each point.

**Processes and flocks.** `FS()` is a process; two of them between the same
cuts share the page cache. After a cut every call through an FS from before
it, or on a file opened through one, fails with `ErrCut`, so a dead
process's code can't touch the disk. A flock belongs to an open file and
lives on its inode, as on Linux: two opens contend, in one process or two;
the holder can take it again; it follows the file through a rename or a
removal; Unlock from an open that doesn't hold it does nothing; Close and a
cut let go of it.

**Names stay as they are through a cut,** and so do permissions and owners.
Paths are absolute, a relative one is taken from "/", and there are no
symbolic links, so `RealPath` cleans. A new disk holds "/" alone. `Rename`
moves files only, and gives EISDIR for a folder.

**Determinism.** `New`'s seed feeds one PCG generator, drawn from only under
the disk's lock, in call order, so the same seed and the same calls in the
same order give the same disk, cut by cut.

**Where the model is kinder than some disks.** These are left out, for a
later task to add if the engine needs them:

- a torn sector that reads back as an error, as on a drive whose
  interrupted write fails its own check; a rule failing `ReadAt` can stand
  in for one;
- bytes that are neither version, from a drive that writes garbage or
  writes to the wrong place, which BETA.md's premise of a drive that
  honours flushes leaves out;
- pages dropped from the cache after a failed sync, so reads would see the
  drive's bytes before any cut;
- the 4 KiB page as the unit that writing back works in, which would put
  the rest of a page on the drive when part of it changes;
- a failed `Truncate` or name call that took effect all the same.

## Notes for later tasks

- **T2, names:** `Disk.names` is the namespace, one map from path to inode,
  and `cut()` is where names would be settled; it settles data file by file
  in inode order, then drops files without a name. A durable copy per
  folder, updated by `SyncDir`, fits there. A cut on a call on names comes
  before the call for now. Owners and permissions are durable too; whether
  a cut can lose an unsynced `Chown` is T2's to decide.
- **T3, the driver:** count with a run on `New(seed)` and `Calls(fault.Any,
  "")` or `Calls(fault.WriteAt, "")`, then for each `i` run again on a fresh
  `New(seed)` after `Add(fault.Rule{Op: fault.WriteAt, N: i, Cut: true})`,
  or `Rule{Op: fault.Any, N: i}` for a failure. `Cuts()` and `Failed()` say
  whether the rule fired. Call `Clear()` before checking, so a rule still
  waiting doesn't fire in the check, and check through a new `FS()`. A copy
  taken mid-commit is a read of the file through another open, which sees
  the page cache as `cp` would; a thin `fsys.FS` of T3's own over the
  disk's can take it at the call T3 picks. A process killed without a cut
  is its files closed: the cache keeps their writes and their flocks go.
- **T3's planted "marker before sync":** a cut before the sync can keep the
  marker's sector and lose or tear the batch's. With a batch and its marker
  in one sector, a tear with the new bytes second does the same. Either way
  a reader finds a whole marker after a batch that fails its checksum.
- **Determinism and goroutines:** the nth call is the same from run to run
  only when the calls come in the same order. A test with goroutines making
  file calls side by side, such as a reader beside a writer, gets a
  different order each run, and a failure may not replay.
- **F3, F5 and F8** pass their closing tests through T3 on this disk. An
  injected failure matches `syscall.EIO` by default, and `ErrCut` means the
  process died with the power, so the engine's code after it changes
  nothing.
- **R1, long runs:** `TestSyncedDataSurvivesLongRuns` and
  `TestSyncedDataSurvivesEveryCut` take their sizes from numbers at their
  top, which a long run can raise.
- **Shared files:** `beta/README.md` needs an entry for the package (the
  report gives wording). No interface changed.
