# Q2: Dates

Status: done, 8 October 2026.

## The contract

From BETA.md: dates: `date` and `datetime` on `'now'`, plus or minus days,
months and years. Done when A4's date cases, month ends included, match
exactly.

## What was built

All of it is in `beta/internal/query`, beside Q1's evaluator and Q3's
parser.

- **`date.go`,** the dates: SQLite 3.53.4's date code, ported step for step
  from go-sqlite3's `sqlite3-binding.c`. It has `setDateTimeToCurrent` for
  'now', the case of `parseModifier` that reads "NNN units", `computeJD`,
  `computeYMD` and `computeHMS`, the end of `isDate`, and `dateFunc` and
  `datetimeFunc` for the text. A `dateTime` is SQLite's `DateTime` without
  the zone, the raw number and `nFloor`, which the subset never uses.
  - `compileDate` works out `date()` and `datetime()` from the statement's
    moment, `Frame.Now`, with the modifiers read once as the call compiles.
  - `dateModifiers` refuses a first argument other than 'now' and a modifier
    outside SQL.md's pattern. `checkDates` runs it over a whole expression,
    subqueries included, before `Compile` and `CompileCondition` compile it.
  - Below the refusal, `parseModifier` reads a modifier as SQLite reads
    "NNN units", with fractions, hours and smaller units, and no sign
    needed. `dateValue` applies the modifiers in turn to any start, a date
    of its own included, which lets the closing test run the corpus's dates
    on fixed days.
- **The files that were there,** each changed a little:
  - `eval.go`: `Frame` has a new field, `Now time.Time`, the statement's
    moment. `Compile` and `CompileCondition` call `checkDates` first. The
    comments on the evaluator and on `Scope.Call` say the dates are the
    evaluator's now.
  - `funcs.go`: `call` hands `date()` and `datetime()` to `compileDate`, and
    they're off the list of calls left to `Scope.Call`.
  - `plant_on.go`: the seven planted bugs' names, in a `case` of their own at
    the top of the switch, and their descriptions in a section between Q3's
    and Q1's, away from the ends of both lists, where Q4 adds its own.
  - `scope_test.go`: `TestWithoutAScope` loses its row for `date('now')`,
    which compiles without a Scope now. `TestCallsGoToTheScope` gives its
    Frame a moment and expects the dates worked out. `TestWhatASubqueryWaitsFor`
    uses `walk('docs:1', 2)` where it used `date('now')`, as a call the fake
    Scope still logs, so its rows still show the order.
- **Seven planted bugs,** in `plant_on.go` and `beta/plants.txt`:
  `query/date-month-end-clamps`, `query/date-modifiers-reversed`,
  `query/date-now-per-call`, `query/date-now-in-its-zone`,
  `query/date-seconds-rounded`, `query/date-limit-inclusive` and
  `query/date-checked-as-compiled`.
- **Tests:**
  - `date_test.go`: `TestTheCorpusDatesGive0xsAnswers`, the closing test.
    `TestDateCases`, 88 dates on 'now' at set moments, at month ends, leap

    `TestDatesOutsideTheSubsetAreRefused`, 33 refusals with their messages

## The result

- **Every date case in the corpus gives 0.x's answer exactly.** Of the 608,
  607 parse, and each call is refused as it compiles, since its first
  argument isn't 'now'. Below the refusal, the arithmetic on its fixed date
  gives 0.x's answer, with the column's name, for all 607: 563 dates and 44
  NULLs. Of the dates, 254 move a month or a year from the 29th, 30th or
  31st, and 141 more start on 28 or 29 February. The odd cases come out as
  in 0.x too: '+1.5 days' and '1 day' move the date, '+ 1 day' and
  '+1 fortnight' give NULL, and so do 'not a date' and a 25th hour, and
  2026-02-30 is 2026-03-02. `date(NULL, '+1 day')`, the 608th, is refused by
  the parser, as Q3.md says.
- **Random dates agree with 0.x far beyond the corpus:** 40,000 cases of one
  to three modifiers, three in four on 'now' with the moment in `Frame.Now`,
  and one in four below the refusal on a date of its own. They gave 28,349
  dates, 8,475 of them a month or a year moved from the 29th to the 31st,
  and 11,651 NULLs. The moments cover SQLite's whole range, its ends, the
  last days of months and the days around leap days, with numbers up to the
  units' limits and past 64 bits, in any case, and with SQLite's six spaces.
  The dates of their own often have a day their month hasn't got, and some
  of their modifiers are outside the pattern: fractions in eighths, hours,
  minutes and seconds, no sign, units SQLite hasn't got, and modifiers
  written wrong. While the tests were being written, six more seeds ran
  200,000 cases each, and none differed.
- **The table of 88 cases is 0.x's:** `TestTheDateCasesAre0xs` runs the 81
  whose moment can be written as a date of its own on 0.x, and gets the
  table's answers. The other 7 are moments only a clock gives: at or before
  the start of SQLite's range, where its clock gives NULL, and in 10000.
- **The cases on 'now' match 0.x at the time:** the corpus's 14 and 13 more,
  with several dates in a row and dates in WHERE. Each runs on 0.x between
  two readings of the clock, and 0.x's answer is the Beta's at one of them.
- **The calendar is Go's:** at 200,000 random moments, and at four moments
  a year every few years across the whole range, `computeYMD` and
  `computeHMS` give Go's date and time, and `computeJD` gives the moment
  back.
- **The planted bugs are caught,** all seven, one at a time with `-failfast`
  as `scripts/planted.sh` runs them, by the tests without cgo as well as
  with it. Q1's fifteen and Q3's eleven still are.
- **A bug found on the way,** by the random test: 0001-01-01 00:00:00 UTC is
  Go's zero time, which a Frame takes for no moment. No clock gives it, so
  it stays that way. `Frame`'s comment says so, and the random test leaves
  that millisecond out.
- `sh scripts/check-beta.sh`, `go vet ./...`, `go test -short ./...`,
  `go test -race -short` on the package, the package's tests with
  `CGO_ENABLED=0`, and the date tests with `TZ` set to four other zones
  pass. The date tests take about a second, and a quarter of one with
  `-short`.

## Decisions settled

**The arithmetic,** SQLite's routines as they run for the subset, checked
against 0.x:

- Days add whole days to the moment, which is in milliseconds of the Julian
  day. Months and years move the month or the year and keep the day and the
  time of day. The moment is then worked out again from the date, so a day
  past the end of its month runs on into the next: 31 January and
  '+1 month' is 31 February, which is 3 March, or 2 March in a leap year.
- The modifiers apply in turn, so on 31 January '+1 month', '+1 day' gives 4
  March, and '+1 day', '+1 month' gives 1 March.
- Leap years are the Gregorian calendar's, back to the start of SQLite's
  range: 1900 and 2100 aren't leap years, and 2000 and year 0 are.
- SQLite gives NULL, and so does the Beta, for a number of 5,373,485 days or
  more, 176,546 months or more, or 14,713 years or more, whatever the date;
  for a month or a year that moves the year outside -4713 to 9999, or that
  comes while the moment is outside SQLite's range; and for a moment outside
  that range at the end. The range runs from noon on 24 November 4714 BC to
  9999-12-31 23:59:59.999. A run of days may leave the range and come back:
  on 31 December 9999, '+1 day', '-1 day' gives the same day.
- A year before year 1 is written with a minus sign and four digits, after
  a year 0, as SQLite counts them: 1,000,000 days before 7 October 2026 is
  -0712-11-09.
- `datetime()` cuts off the fraction of the seconds without rounding it.

**'now':**

- It's `Frame.Now`, a `time.Time` that whoever runs the statement reads once
  with `time.Now`, before the first row. Every row sees the same moment, as
  SQL.md says, where 0.x reads its clock again for each row.
- It's read as SQLite reads its clock, in whole milliseconds since 1970, so
  its zone doesn't count and every date is in UTC. SQLite gives NULL for a
  clock at or before the start of its range, and so does the Beta.
- A zero `Frame.Now` is an error, "date('now') has no moment to read: the
  statement's Frame.Now isn't set", so a statement run without a moment
  fails at its first date, where it would otherwise give dates in year 1.
- Tests set it. `momentText` in `date_test.go` writes a moment as a date of
  its own, for 0.x.

**Refusals:**

- `Compile` and `CompileCondition` refuse a date the subset doesn't take
  anywhere in the expression they're given: inside the one-record
  subquery's key and a walk's arguments, and in the parts the compiler
  leaves out, such as x in `0 AND x` or in `x IN ()`. So whether a statement
  is refused doesn't depend on what SQLite would work out.
- The refusal is an `*Error` of kind "error", placed at the argument it
  can't take: "date() on '2026-10-07' is outside the SQL subset: it takes
  'now' and modifiers such as '+1 day'", and "the modifier '+1 week' in
  date() is outside the SQL subset: a modifier is a sign, a whole number, a
  space and days, months or years, such as '-3 months'".
- The fixed-date cases still parse, as Q3 left them, and Q3's corpus test
  still counts them as dates Q2 refuses.

**Where SQL.md is silent,** settled as 0.x's SQLite behaves and checked
against 0.x. The wording for SQL.md's "Dates":

- After the bullet on modifiers: "The number may have any count of digits,
  zeros in front included. A space is any of the six bytes SQLite reads as
  one there: the space, tab, line feed, vertical tab, form feed and carriage
  return. Nothing comes before the sign or after the unit, so ' +1 day' and
  '+1 day ' are outside the subset."
- In place of "Where SQLite gives NULL, the Beta does too.": "Where SQLite
  gives NULL, the Beta does too: for a number of 5,373,485 days or more,
  176,546 months or more, or 14,713 years or more; for a month or a year
  that moves the year outside -4713 to 9999, or that comes while the moment
  is outside SQLite's range; and for a moment outside that range at the end.
  The range runs from noon on 24 November 4714 BC to the end of 9999. A run
  of days may leave it and come back, so on 31 December 9999
  `date('now', '+1 day', '-1 day')` is that day."
- A new bullet: "A year before year 1 is written with a minus sign and four
  digits, after a year 0, as SQLite counts them:
  `date('now', '-1000000 days')` gave `-0712-11-09` on 7 October 2026."
- After the bullet on 'now': "'now' counts whole milliseconds, in UTC, as
  SQLite's clock does, and `datetime()` cuts off the fraction of the
  seconds."
- A new bullet: "A date outside the subset is refused before anything runs,
  wherever it is in the statement, even where SQLite wouldn't work it out,
  as in `0 AND date('2026-10-07')`."

## Notes for later tasks

- **Q5, the planner:**
  - Set `Frame.Now` once for each statement, with `time.Now()`, before the
    first row, and give every Frame of the statement the same moment: those
    on other goroutines, and those for WHERE, ORDER BY, LIMIT and the
    subqueries. `Nearest`'s filter takes one moment for each call.
  - Compile every expression of a statement before running any row.
    `Compile` and `CompileCondition` refuse a date outside the subset
    anywhere in what they're given, so once every expression has compiled
    the statement has none, and the refusal has come before anything runs.
  - Dates need no Scope, and `date()` and `datetime()` don't reach
    `Scope.Call` any more.
- **G4, slice 3:** the driver takes the moment once for each `Query`,
  `QueryRow` or `Exec`, inside `Update` too, where each statement has a
  moment of its own, as in 0.x. The 14 cases on 'now' in `dates.jsonl` can
  then go through `sqlcorpus.Replay` against 0.x at the time, which tries
  again when a second ticks over. A Frame without a moment is an error, so a
  path that forgets it fails at its first date.
- **Q4, operators:** an operator that makes Frames of its own copies `Now`
  from the statement's Frame.
- **Q6, writes:** an INSERT's values and an UPDATE's SET may use
  `date('now')`, on the statement's moment. A date never raises an error, so
  it adds nothing to the kinds of a write's errors.
