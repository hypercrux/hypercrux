# Q5: Planner

Status: done, 9 October 2026.

## The contract

From BETA.md: the planner: a nearest search for `ORDER BY distance` with
`LIMIT`, rows without a vector first otherwise, walk joins, `IN` and
`NOT IN` over walks, the one-record subquery. Done when plan-shape tests
pass, and the crux query matches 0.x on fixed random data.

## What was built

All of it is in `beta/internal/query`, in files of its own, and it reads
the store through `store.Reader` as it is. No file that was there changed
apart from this one, `doc.go`, whose package comment has a clause on
`Query` now, and `beta/plants.txt`, which has thirteen new lines.

- **`plan.go`,** the planner:
  - `Query(r, s, f)` plans the SELECT `s` over the `store.Reader` `r`, runs
    the plan with the statement's `Frame` `f`, and gives the result
    columns' names and the `Rows`. It's the one call the driver makes for a
    SELECT.
  - `Prepare(r, s)` gives a `*Plan`, with every name found and every
    expression compiled, so all the errors a statement's text and tables
    can give come before its first row. `Plan.Run(f)` works out LIMIT and
    OFFSET and puts the operators together. `Columns`, `Shape` and `String`
    give the names, the plan's shape and the plan itself, an operator a
    line in the order rows go through them.
  - The shapes are `ShapeRow` without a FROM, `ShapeScan`, `ShapeLookup`
    for the records a term on the key names, `ShapeNearest`, `ShapeWalk`
    and `ShapeWalkJoin`.
  - Before it compiles, the planner writes out `*`, folds `x AND 0` as
    SQLite's parser does, puts the result columns' aliases into ORDER BY's
    expressions and settles each ORDER BY term. It splits the WHERE at its
    ANDs and sorts the terms out: those worked out once before any row, the
    term a key lookup uses, a join's terms on the walk alone, and the rest.
- **`plan_run.go`,** the plan's own operators beside Q4's: a gate that
  works out the terms worked out once, the one row, a scan backwards, the
  key lookup, the walk, the join, and the nearest search with its filter
  or the sort that stands in for it.
- **`plan_scope.go`,** the planner's `Scope`: the sources' fields, the
  one-record subquery and IN over a walk, each worked out once for the
  statement, and `walk()` as a function. It sets up the key lookup's term
  too.
- **`plan_walk.go`:** walk()'s checks of its arguments, in 0.x's order and
  with 0.x's messages; the walk and the one-record subquery, kept for the
  statement; and the planner's own condition for `IN` and `NOT IN` over a
  walk, which looks x up in a set of the walk's keys.
- **`plan_tree.go`:** `rewrite` over the tree, `foldAnds`, the split of a
  WHERE, ORDER BY's column numbers, and two questions about an expression:
  whether it's worked out once (`onceOnly`), and whether it can raise an
  error at all (`mayFail`).
- **`plan_filter.go`:** `NearestFilter(r, table, where, params, f)`, the
  `store.Filter` for the public package's `Nearest(table, q, k, where,
  args...)`, with SQL.md's "Nearest's filter".
- **Thirteen planted bugs,** in `plant_plan_on.go`, whose `init` switches
  them on, so `plant_on.go` and `plant_ops_on.go` stay as they were:
  `query/nearest-ignores-filter`, `query/nearest-without-vec-check`,
  `query/nearest-k-is-the-limit`, `query/walk-join-drops-a-depth`,
  `query/walk-lookup-in-walk-order`, `query/null-not-in-walk-kept`,
  `query/record-every-time`, `query/record-of-any-table`,
  `query/once-terms-for-each-row`, `query/join-terms-after-lookup`,
  `query/and-not-folded`, `query/order-alias-first` and
  `query/filter-first-term-dropped`.
- **Tests:**
  - `plan_test.go`: `planCases`, 106 statements on the fixture with an
    empty table beside it, each with its shape and its answer, and 18 with
    the plan printed. `TestPlanShapes` is the plan-shape test. Beside it:
    subqueries worked out once, counted through a `store.Reader` that
    counts its calls; reads through a transaction; one argument for each
    `?` mark; the statement's moment for dates; the columns' names; a
    search's filter on 8 goroutines at once; and `TestNearestFilters`,

  - `plan_corpus_test.go`: every SELECT of the corpus through `Query`.
  - `plan_sqlite_test.go`, built with cgo only:

## Decisions settled

**The entry point** (`plan.go`):

- `Query(r store.Reader, s *Select, f *Frame) ([]string, Rows, error)`.
  `f.Args` fill the `?` marks, exactly one for each, and `f.Now` is the
  statement's moment. The Rows read `r` as they're pulled, so they last as
  long as the read, and the caller closes them.
- A Plan runs once, since the subqueries it works out once keep their
  answers.
- The planner's errors are `*Error`, and walk()'s refusals `*FuncError`, as
  the evaluator's are, so all of them are of kind "error" in a SELECT. An
  error from the store, such as a transaction that has ended, comes back
  as it is.

**The order of work,** which shows in which statements raise an error, as
SQLite's code generator orders it, each step checked against 0.x:

1. LIMIT, then OFFSET, through Q4's `Bounds`. A LIMIT of 0 runs nothing
   more.
2. The WHERE's terms that read no field, hold no subquery and call no
   `walk()`, each once, in turn, as `sqlite3WhereBegin` works them out
   before its loops. A date counts as constant, as SQLite's date functions
   do within a statement. Without a FROM, every term is one of these. When
   one is false or NULL, no row is read, and a walk in the FROM never runs.
   When one fails, the statement fails over an empty table too.
3. The rows, by the plan's shape:
   - **one row,** without a FROM;
   - **a scan:** the table's records in key order, or backwards for ORDER
     BY key DESC;
   - **a key lookup,** as SQLite looks records up in the key's index: the
     first term `key = e` where e reads no field, or else the first
     `key IN (...)` whose items read no field, `key IN ((SELECT ...))`, or
     `key IN` over a walk. The term is worked out once, when the rows
     start, even over an empty table. Only text names a key, and only a key
     of the table, so `key = 5` finds nothing. The records come in key
     order, or backwards for ORDER BY key DESC, each once, and the other
     terms meet only those;
   - **the nearest search,** below;
   - **a walk:** its steps in the walk's order;
   - **a walk join:** the walk is the outer loop, whichever source is
     written first, as in SQLite. Of the terms left, one that reads only
     the walk's columns, or no field at all, is worked out for each step
     before the table's record is looked up, as SQLite works it out at the
     walk's loop. A step to another table's record, or to no record, gives
     no row.
4. The other terms, in turn, through Q4's `Filter`.
5. Q4's tail: `Aggregate`, `Limit` and `Project`; `Sort`, or `TopK` under
   a LIMIT, then `Limit`; or `Limit` and `Project`.

Two more rules about ORDER BY's part in it:

- An ORDER BY whose first term is the table's key, by name, alias or
  number, over a scan or a key lookup, is the order the records come in,
  as the key's index gives it to SQLite. It sorts nothing and works out
  none of its terms, going up or down.
- Without a FROM, ORDER BY isn't worked out, apart from an aggregate
  query's aggregates, as SQLite takes one row as sorted. Its names and
  column numbers are checked all the same.

**Names,** as SQLite's `lookupName` finds them:

- For finding names, json_each has its hidden columns json and root
  besides SQL.md's eight, so they clash with a table's fields of those
  names. Only value may be used.
- Two sources may have one name. A name with that name before the dot then
  looks in both, and a column both have is ambiguous.
- In an aggregate query, a field outside an aggregate in ORDER BY is
  refused once its name is found, so a name that doesn't exist says so
  first.
- A join's ON is the table's key and the walk's key, or json_each's value,
  either way round. Anything else is outside the subset.

**ORDER BY's terms,** as `resolveOrderGroupBy` settles them: an alias,
then a column number as `sqlite3ExprIsInteger` reads it, after `foldAnds`,
then an expression. In an expression, a name finds a field first and an
alias after, and an alias stands for its column's expression, as
`resolveAlias` puts it there. So the expression is worked out again, with
its affinity.

**`x AND 0`:** `foldAnds` makes `x AND y` the integer 0 wherever SQLite's
parser does, in `sqlite3ExprAnd`: when either side is known to be false,
as Q1's `alwaysFalse` has it, and neither calls a function, LIKE
included, outside a subquery. It works on the whole tree before anything
is compiled. So a WHERE is split after it, and an IN list's items or an
ORDER BY term can become constant.

**The nearest search** is taken when:

- the FROM is one table, and there's no aggregate;
- ORDER BY's first term is `distance(vec, q)` going up, written out or by
  alias or number, where vec is the table's vector field and q reads no
  field, and a second term, if there is one, is the key going up;
- there's a LIMIT and a WHERE term `vec IS NOT NULL`;
- no WHERE term before `vec IS NOT NULL` can raise an error, as `mayFail`
  judges, and no result column can apart from those ORDER BY sorts by. The
  search works out WHERE's terms only for records with a vector, and the
  result columns only for the rows it gives, where 0.x's sort works out
  more.

Its filter is WHERE's other terms, in turn, once the terms worked out once
have passed, over a row of its own from a pool, since the store may call
it on several goroutines. Run works out q before the search, and sorts
instead, through `Scan`, `Filter` and `TopK`, when the search can't give
the sort's answer: when q is NULL, isn't a vector, holds only zeros, NaN
or infinity, or has another size than the table's vectors, when the LIMIT
is negative, or when k, LIMIT plus OFFSET, is past `store.MaxK`. Then
distance()'s errors come where 0.x's do: at the first row that passes
WHERE. When no row passes, there's no error. The search breaks ties by
key, as the stable sort over records in key order does, and its distances
have the bits of SQL's `distance()`.

The crux query is a walk join with `TopK`, as 0.x's SQLite runs it: the
walk is the outer loop, and only the records it reaches are read.

**Subqueries:** the one-record subquery and IN over a walk are worked out
once for each place they're written, the first time a row reaches them.
When no row reaches one, it isn't worked out. Its answer, an error
included, stands for the rest of the statement, as SQLite's `OP_Once` has
it. A key lookup's term is the exception, worked out before any row.
`walk()` as a function is worked out at each call, since 0.x registers it
as a function that isn't deterministic. The one-record subquery finds a
record only for text that starts with its table's name and a colon.

**walk()** checks its arguments in 0.x's order, with 0.x's messages: the
start is text, the depth an integer, the type NULL or text, and the
direction NULL or text that's out, in, both or empty in any case; then the
depth runs from 1 to 32. A start that isn't a record's key, or isn't a
valid key at all, reaches nothing.

**IN and NOT IN over a walk,** as the evaluator's `inWalk` has them: the
walk first, then x. IN is true when x is text equal to a key the walk
reached, NULL when x is NULL and the walk reached something, and false
otherwise. NOT IN is the opposite, and NULL stays NULL.

**Three of 0.x's plans that the planner doesn't follow,** which SQL.md's
"0.x's planner may check conditions in another order, or skip rows by
their key" covers. Each shows only in which error comes when one condition
fails and another rules its row out, or in the order of rows without ORDER
BY:

- When SQLite reads the key's index, as it does for a query that needs
  only the key, it works out the terms on the key alone before the others.
- In a join, `key IN` over a walk on the table's key makes SQLite read the
  table first, through the IN.
- SQLite's constant propagation can turn two terms on one field, such as
  `status = 'a' AND status = 'b'`, into a term that reads no field, which
  rules every row out before any is read, so a walk in the FROM never
  runs.

The random statements keep clear of them: a walk that may be refused gets
one other condition at most, and a filter's condition that can fail stands
alone.

**Where SQL.md is silent or unclear,** settled as 0.x's SQLite behaves and
checked against 0.x. The wording for SQL.md:

- "What gets worked out", the bullet on WHERE, in place of its last
  sentence: "AND's terms at the top of a WHERE are worked out in turn as
  written, without the rule for literals above, apart from those that use
  no field, no subquery and no `walk()`. Those are worked out first, once
  each and in turn, before any row is read, as SQLite's planner takes them
  out of its loop, and without a FROM every term is one of them. So
  `WHERE E AND 0` raises E's error when E is one of them, and reads no rows
  when it isn't, where `SELECT E AND 0` gives 0. When such a term is
  false or NULL, no row is read, and a walk in the FROM isn't worked out.
  When one raises an error, the statement fails even when the table has no
  records."
- "What gets worked out", a new bullet after it: "Before anything is
  worked out, `x AND y` becomes the integer 0 when either side is known to
  be false by the rule for literals above, an AND made 0 included, and
  neither side calls a function outside a subquery, as SQLite's parser
  makes it. So `WHERE (SELECT n FROM docs WHERE key = ?) AND 0` never
  works out its subquery, the list in `1 IN (1, n AND 0, E)` has no fields
  and is worked out whole, and `ORDER BY n AND 0` is the column number 0,
  which is out of range."
- "What gets worked out", another new bullet: "A WHERE term on the key
  that names the records to read, `key = e` where e uses no field, or else
  `key IN` with a list whose items use no field, over the one-record
  subquery or over a walk, is worked out once before any row is read, even
  when the table has no records, as SQLite looks the records up in the
  key's index. The first such equality is used, or else the first such IN,
  and the other terms meet only the records it names, in key order."
- "What gets worked out", another new bullet: "In a join, the walk comes
  first, whichever source is written first. A term that uses only the
  walk's columns, or uses no field and isn't worked out once, is worked out
  for each record the walk reaches, before the table's record is looked
  up, so it meets records of other tables too. The other terms are worked
  out for each row of the join."
- "What gets worked out", at the start of the bullet on ORDER BY: "An
  ORDER BY whose first term is the table's key, by name, alias or number,
  going up or down, is the order of the key's index, and the query is
  worked out as if it had no ORDER BY, with none of its terms worked out.
  That holds over a table. A join's rows are sorted."
- "ORDER BY", after rule 3: "In such an expression, an alias stands for
  its column's expression, which is worked out again, with its affinity:
  with `CAST(n AS INTEGER) AS c`, `ORDER BY c = '5'` compares as
  `CAST(n AS INTEGER) = '5'`, which is true where n is the text '5'. A
  query without a FROM has one row, and its ORDER BY isn't worked out,
  apart from an aggregate query's aggregates. Its names and numbers are
  checked all the same."
- "Sources, qualified names and clashes", in the bullet on json_each:
  "(key, value, type, atom, id, parent, fullkey and path, and the hidden
  json and root)". And a new bullet: "Two sources may have the same name. A
  name with that name before the dot then looks in both, and a column both
  have is ambiguous."
- "The one-record subquery", in place of "and the subquery is worked out
  once for each statement": "and the subquery is worked out once for each
  statement: the first time a row reaches it, or before any row when it
  names the records to read, as under "What gets worked out". A subquery
  no row reaches isn't worked out, and raises nothing." And in "Walks in
  IN", after "worked out once for each statement": "by the same rule".

## Notes for later tasks

- **Q6, writes:**
  - An UPDATE's or a DELETE's records can come from the planner. Build a
    `*Select` over the write's table with its WHERE, the result columns
    `key` and, for an UPDATE, the SET values, and `params` from the
    write's, then `Prepare` and `Run` it with the write's Frame. It gets
    the key lookups, the terms worked out once and the subqueries worked
    out once, as SQLite plans a write's WHERE with the same code. Copy each
    row, since a row is good only until the next `Next`, and read them all
    before the first write. Whether SQLite works out the SET values as it
    reads each row, or after its WHERE has run over them all, shows in
    which error comes first, and is Q6's to check against 0.x.
  - An INSERT's rows of values work as a Select without a FROM, one for
    each row.
  - In a write, a `*FuncError` is "invalid", as SQL.md's table has it, and
    the planner's `*Error` stays "error".
  - The 18 queries the corpus runs after a write need only the write:
    `planQuery` in `plan_corpus_test.go` gives their answers through
    `Query`.
- **G4, the driver:**
  - A SELECT is one call: `query.Query(r, sel, &query.Frame{Args: args,
    Now: now})`, with `r` the read's `store.Reader`, from the store's
    `Read` or a `store.Tx`, `args` made with `query.Arg`, exactly one for
    each `?` mark, and `now` read once for the statement. It gives the
    columns' names and the Rows.
  - The Rows' row is used again at each `Next`, so copy it, and hand a
    `KindVector` out as a copy of its bytes. Close the Rows before the read
    ends.
  - `*query.Error` and `*query.FuncError` are of kind "error" in a SELECT.
    Errors from the store come back as they are.
  - `Nearest` with a filter: as `plannedNearest` in `plan_test.go` makes
    it. 0.x's own checks come first, in 0.x's order: the table's name, the
    query vector, k, the table, and its vector size, where a table without
    one gives no hits before the filter is read at all. Then
    `ParseCondition`, then `NearestFilter`, whose `none` means no hits and
    no search, then the store's `Nearest` with the filter.
    `TestNearestFiltersAs0xHasThem` holds that order to 0.x.
- **A3's harness, for the crux query on random data through the driver:**
  - The crux query can join difftest's `statements` in 0.x's form,
    `SELECT d.key, d.title FROM json_each(walk(?, ?)) w JOIN docs d ON
    d.key = w.value WHERE d.status = ? AND d.vec IS NOT NULL ORDER BY
    distance(d.vec, ?) LIMIT ?`, with a start among the keys with links, a
    depth from 1 to 4, a status, a query vector from `g.vector("docs")` and
    a LIMIT. The Beta takes the same text, and `walk(?, ?) w` with `w.key`
    too.
  - Its rows compare in order. Here 15,000 crux queries gave 0.x's rows in
    0.x's order, ties included, since both engines keep the walk's order
    for rows that tie. If distances that differ in their last bits ever
    swap two rows, `sameUpToTies` in `plan_sqlite_test.go` compares them
    within `conformance.DistanceBound`, as A3.md's note asks.
  - A query vector binds as bytes, or as JSON text. A depth outside 1 to
    32, or a start that isn't text, is an error in both unless the LIMIT
    is 0, and a vector of another size is one when a row reaches
    distance(). Each is of kind "error", which the harness calls "other".
    A start with no record reaches nothing.
  - A condition added to it that can fail belongs on its own, since 0.x may
    check terms in another order, as SQL.md allows: three such orders are
    under "Decisions settled".
