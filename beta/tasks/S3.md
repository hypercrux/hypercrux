# S3: Change lists

Status: done, 8 October 2026.

## The contract

From BETA.md: change lists, emitted as a transaction runs, applied whole or
not at all, and a snapshot for compaction. Done when store A's change lists
applied to store B, and A's snapshot loaded into store C, both give back A.

## What was built

- **`beta/internal/store`**, in the new `changes.go` and `snapshot.go`, with
  changes to `write.go`, `tx.go` and `store.go`:
  - `Store.ApplyBatch(seq, changes)`, a batch the log has read, all of it or
    none, through a transaction of its own.
  - `Store.LoadBatch(seq, changes)`, a batch for a store that nobody reads
    yet, as on opening, made straight in the store.
  - Both give a `*errs.Damage` naming the batch when a change breaks the
    rules for the state it applies to.
  - `Store.Snapshot`, in place of P3's stub, in FORMAT.md's order for a
    compacted part. `Tx.Snapshot` is the same, with the transaction's
    changes in it.
  - `Apply` refuses a change that sets a field of `format.Change` its Op
    doesn't use, which `format.AppendBatch` refuses.
  - A put refuses text and bytes of more than 4,294,967,295 bytes, the
    format's limit, in `fits`.
  - `Tx.Apply` puts copies of a change's `Names` and `Fields` in its change
    list.
  - Seven planted bugs, in `plant_on.go` and `beta/plants.txt`:
    `store/batch-half-applied`, `store/unused-fields-taken`,
    `store/applied-slices-shared`, `store/snapshot-in-map-order`,
    `store/snapshot-fields-unsorted`, `store/snapshot-no-vectors` and
    `store/snapshot-size-left-out`.
- **Tests,** in `changes_test.go`, inside the package, and
  `snapshot_test.go`, outside it:
  - `TestChangeLists`, the closing test.
  - Applying whole: `TestApplyBatch`, `TestApplyBatchTakesItsTurn` and
    `TestLoadBatch`.
  - Emitting: `TestTheChangesOfEachWrite`, `TestApplyRefusesUnusedFields`,
    `TestAppliedChangesKeepTheirSlices` and `TestFits`.
  - The snapshot: `TestSnapshot`, `TestSnapshotOfTheFixtures`,
    `TestSnapshotStops` and `TestSnapshotSharesNothingThatChanges`.
  - S1's `TestWhatComesLater` and S2's `TestATransactionThatHasEnded` no
    longer expect the snapshot to panic. The second checks that an open
    transaction's snapshot holds its changes.

## The result

- **Store A's change lists applied to store B, and A's snapshot loaded into
  store C, both give back A.** `TestChangeLists` runs 12 seeds of 300
  transactions, or 3 of 150 with `-short`.
  - The transactions on A are S2's: puts, deletes, drops and creates, with
    the bad keys, names and values of S1's generator, statements taken back
    to their marks, commits whose write fails, and rollbacks.
  - Each change list a commit hands on goes through `format.AppendBatch`
    and `format.DecodeBatch`, and must come back the same. So does the list
    of a commit whose write fails.
  - B opens once a fifth of the transactions have run: it takes the batches
    committed by then with `LoadBatch`. Then it follows the log, taking each
    new batch with `ApplyBatch`, while two readers read it. Every state they
    see must be one that a commit on A left.
  - A quarter of B's batches come first with a change put in that breaks
    the rules, such as a table created twice or a put into a table that
    doesn't exist. The codec writes such a batch, since only the state
    shows what's wrong. B must report damage naming the batch and that
    change, and stay as it was.
  - Every 20 transactions, A's snapshot, taken inside `Read`, is checked
    against FORMAT.md's order and against the model. It's cut into batches
    of one to eight changes, each encoded and decoded, and an empty store C
    takes them, with `LoadBatch` or `ApplyBatch`. C, and B once it follows,
    must then agree with A on every table and key the workload can name,
    through `Table` and `Get`, on their snapshots, and on a dump of the
    whole store.
  - Seed 1 makes 170 commits that hand on a list, 28 whose write fails and
    61 rollbacks, and takes 315 statements back. B loads 34 batches on
    opening, applies 136, and finds the damage in 34 more. Its readers make
    about 1,500 reads each and see about 130 states. 15 snapshots go into a
    C.
  - It takes 0.7 seconds, 3.3 with `-race`, and 0.2 and 1.0 with `-short`.
    The store's tests take 4.5 seconds, 26 with `-race`, and 1.0 and 4.3
    with `-short`.
- **The snapshot matches P2's fixtures.** `file-new.hex`, loaded into a
  store without its links, gives a snapshot that, written as one batch of
  generation 2, is byte for byte the first batch of `file-compacted.hex`'s
  compacted part, which P2 wrote by hand from FORMAT.md. Loading the
  compacted part and the commit after it gives back the same store.
- **What the store emits is writable.** Every change list and snapshot in
  the closing test went through the codec, and `TestTheChangesOfEachWrite`
  pins the list of each kind of write. A change with a field its Op doesn't
  use is refused by `Apply` and by `AppendBatch` alike, for every Op and
  field.
- **Bugs planted by hand,** one at a time: 30 in the new code, of which 29
  made the tests fail. The other took the length check out of a put's text,
  which no test can reach, since it takes a value of 4 GB, so `TestFits`
  checks `fits` alone. An `ApplyBatch` that took no transaction was caught
  by Go itself: B's readers met its writes to the hash table, and the run
  stopped. `sh scripts/planted.sh` reports 28 of 28 caught, in about two
  minutes, 33 seconds of them for the seven new ones.
- `go vet ./...`, `go test -short ./...`, `go test -race` on `store`, and
  `sh scripts/check-beta.sh` pass.

## Decisions settled

**Emitting.**

- What a commit hands on is what the writes appended as they ran, in
  order: S1's `Put`, `Delete` and `Drop`, and `Tx.Apply`. They keep
  FORMAT.md's rules for what a writer emits. A put into a table that
  doesn't exist yet comes after a `CreateTable` with no fields and size 0.
  A put's fields are in byte order of name, spelt as the table spells them.
  A delete or a drop of something that isn't there fails before anything
  reaches the list. A transaction with no changes hands nothing on, so the
  log writes no batch.
- The store's checks now refuse everything `AppendBatch` refuses, so a
  commit never fails there. Two gaps were closed:
  - `Apply` refused nothing for a field of `format.Change` that its Op
    doesn't use, such as a delete with `Table` set, and `Tx.Apply` put the
    change in its list as it came. Now it's `errs.ErrInvalid`: "a delete
    change that sets Table, which it doesn't use".
  - Nothing held text and bytes to the format's u32 length. Now a value of
    more than 4,294,967,295 bytes is `errs.ErrInvalid`, in the store's
    words. 0.x's limit is lower: SQLite's 1,000,000,000 bytes, which
    go-sqlite3 builds with.
- `Tx.Apply` copies `Names` and `Fields` into its list, so a caller can use
  its slices again. The snapshot does, and an import may. Without the copy,
  a snapshot applied through a transaction would hand on the last record's
  fields in every put.

**Applying a batch whole.** Two calls, for the two places a batch arrives:

| Call | For | How | When a change breaks the rules |
|---|---|---|---|
| `ApplyBatch(seq, changes)` | a store others read: the writer catching up under the write lock, a follower (F6) | a transaction of its own: `Begin`, `apply` for each change, `Commit(nil)` | rolled back whole, and the store is as it was |
| `LoadBatch(seq, changes)` | a store nobody reads yet: opening (G1), and a reload's new store if F9 keeps it out of sight | the store's own `apply`, straight in, with no lock and no undo list | the changes before it stay, and the caller throws the store away, as an open that fails does |

- `LoadBatch` saves `Begin`'s call to `runtime.Stack`, the locks and the
  undo list on every batch, as S2's note proposed. It panics while a
  transaction is open.
- `ApplyBatch` keeps no change list, since the batch is in the file
  already. On the goroutine running the store's open transaction, `Begin`
  returns `errs.ErrInsideUpdate` at once, and `ApplyBatch` returns it
  unwrapped.
- **What a bad change returns:** a `*errs.Damage` with `Batch` set to the
  sequence number and the reason "change N: " followed by the store's
  error, counting from 1 as the codec does: "change 3: hypercrux: not
  found: no record table nosuch". It has no path, and an `Offset` of 0,
  which the log knows how to fill in. It wraps `errs.ErrDamaged` alone, so
  its kind is "error" whatever the store's error wraps. A batch with no
  changes is damage too, in the codec's words.
- Until S5, a batch with a link in it is damage, since `Apply` refuses
  links.

**The snapshot.**

- `Snapshot` builds its changes as they're ranged over, one at a time, so
  a caller ranges inside the `Read` or the transaction it came from.
  Ranging again starts again, and a caller that stops stops it.
- The tables come in byte order of name. Until S4 keeps each table's keys
  in order, the snapshot sorts them itself: one look at every record puts
  pointers to each table's records in a list of their own, and each list
  is sorted by key before the first change. That's the sort the brief
  allowed for now.
- A put's fields are the record's, with the vector as a value, sorted by
  name for each record. A record holds its fields in its table's order,
  which differs from byte order once a later put adds a field that sorts
  earlier, and the vector field can sort anywhere.
- A `CreateTable`'s `Names` are the table's own list, with its capacity cut
  at its length, as `Table` hands it out. A put's `Fields` slice is used
  again for the next put. Empty lists are nil, as the codec decodes them,
  so a decoded snapshot equals the one taken.
- Until V1, each put's vector is a copy, made with `value.Vector`.

**The planted bugs:** `store/batch-half-applied`, an `ApplyBatch` that
keeps the changes before the one that fails; `store/unused-fields-taken`,
an `Apply` that takes a change with a field its Op doesn't use;
`store/applied-slices-shared`, a `Tx.Apply` that keeps the caller's slices;
`store/snapshot-in-map-order`, records in the hash table's order;
`store/snapshot-fields-unsorted`, a put's fields in the table's order;
`store/snapshot-no-vectors`, puts without their vectors; and
`store/snapshot-size-left-out`, tables created with the vector size 0. Each
adds about 5 seconds to the planted job.

## Notes for later tasks

- **G1:** `Target.Apply` calls `LoadBatch` while `logfile.Open` reads the
  file, and `ApplyBatch` after it, for the batches `Lock` reads on. `Reset`
  swaps in `store.New()`. The log's `readOn` wraps an error from
  `Target.Apply` in a `*errs.Damage` of its own, and the store's error is
  one already, so the message would name the batch twice. The log can treat
  it as it treats the codec's damage, with `errors.As`, setting `Path` and
  adding the batch's offset to `Offset`. That's in `beta/internal/logfile`,
  which this task left alone.
- **F3:** a batch the check writes again and marks goes to the Target, and
  so through `ApplyBatch`. The same note about `readOn` applies.
- **F6:** a follower hands each marked batch to `ApplyBatch`, which waits
  for an `Update` under way.
- **F8:** compaction ranges over the snapshot inside `Read`, after the
  commit that set it off. To collect changes for `AppendBatch`, it clones
  each put's `Fields`, as `collect` and `cut` do in `changes_test.go`, or
  it encodes change by change. `TestSnapshotOfTheFixtures` is a golden test
  for it: compacting `file-new.hex` has to write `file-compacted.hex`'s
  compacted part.
- **F9:** a reload's new store can take `LoadBatch` while no reader can
  reach it. If readers can reach it, `ApplyBatch` is the call.
- **S4:** `byKey` in `snapshot.go` goes, and the snapshot walks each
  table's keys in order. `TestSnapshot` pins byte order of keys, with
  `users:10` before `users:9`.
- **S5:** the links go after the tables in the snapshot, where a comment
  marks the place, in byte order of the key they're from, then type, then
  the key they're to. `TestSnapshotOfTheFixtures` can then compare the
  compacted part's second batch too, and `withoutLinks` goes. `unused`
  already knows the fields Link and Unlink use.
- **V1:** the snapshot makes each put's vector with `value.Vector`, a copy
  of the slot.
- **G6:** an import can make its tables whole with `Tx.Apply` and a
  `CreateTable` carrying the field list and the vector size, and can use
  its buffers again, since `Tx.Apply` copies them.
- **R2:** the Beta takes text and bytes up to 4,294,967,295 bytes, where
  0.x refuses more than 1,000,000,000. Neither SQL.md nor S1 settles
  whether the Beta should keep 0.x's limit.
- **The coordinator:** `beta/README.md`'s line for `internal/store/` can
  read: "the in-memory copy, with its read API, `Reader`. So far it holds
  the records with their fields and each table's field list. Its writes
  give the change lists the log writes, `ApplyBatch` and `LoadBatch` take a
  batch the log has read whole or not at all, and `Snapshot` gives the
  copy as a compacted part. Reads share it through `Read`, and writes go
  through a transaction from `Begin`, which holds readers off from its
  first change until it ends, and puts the copy back with its undo list
  when it rolls back." `beta/plants.txt` has seven new lines for the store.
