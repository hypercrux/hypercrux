# S4: Key order and Scan

Status: done, 8 October 2026.

## The contract

From BETA.md: key order and `Scan`: each table's keys in byte order as they
come and go; `Scan` by prefix, after and limit. Done when the model test and
0.x's `Scan` cases pass.

## What was built

- **`beta/internal/store`**, in the new `order.go` and `scan.go`, with
  changes to `store.go`, `write.go`, `tx.go` and `snapshot.go`:
  - `keyOrder`: a table's keys in byte order, each with its record, in
    blocks of up to 256 keys. Each table holds one in `keys`. A put that
    makes a record adds its key, and a delete takes it out.
  - Two undo kinds, `undoNoKey` and `undoHadKey`: an entry before each
    change to a table's keys.
  - `Store.Scan`, in place of S1's stub, and `Tx.Scan`, which sees the
    transaction's changes. Both check the prefix by 0.x's rules, with 0.x's
    messages, and return the store's `cursor`.
  - The store's `epoch`, which moves on with every change to which tables
    there are and which keys they hold, so a cursor finds its place again
    after a change.
  - `drop` finds its table's records through the table's keys. The
    snapshot walks each table's keys, and `byKey` is gone.
  - Six planted bugs, in `plant_on.go` and `beta/plants.txt`:
    `store/order-not-undone`, `store/delete-keeps-key`,
    `store/join-out-of-order`, `store/after-included`,
    `store/cursor-keeps-its-place` and `store/prefix-table-unchecked`.
- **Tests,** in the new `order_test.go`, inside the package, and
  `scan_test.go`, outside it, with scans added to the tests of S1, S2 and
  S3:
  - The model test: `TestTheModel` with scans, and with cursors kept open

    `TestDropFindsItsOwnRecords`, each on 0.x and on the store, and scans

  - The blocks: `TestKeyOrder`, `TestKeyOrderJoins`, `TestUndoManyKeys`
    and `TestACursorThroughRollbacks`.
  - The timings: `TestKeyOrderTimings`, which prints them with `-v`.
  - `checkInvariants` checks each table's keys against the hash table, and

## The result

- **The model test passes with scans, through transactions and across
  rollbacks.**
  - `TestTheModel` runs 20 seeds of 4,000 steps, or 4 of 2,000 with
    `-short`. A tenth of the steps are scans. Their prefixes can end inside
    an id or inside a character, and one in twelve breaks the rules. Their
    after can be a key, a key of another table, a string inside or outside
    the prefix, or the prefix itself. A scan is pulled to its end, or
    stopped after a few records. A quarter of the scans made while no
    cursor is open stay open over the steps that follow. Such a cursor
    gives a record now and then, which must be the model's next after the
    last one it gave, whatever the steps between have done. A cursor that
    has given false must keep giving it. Seed 1 makes 360 scans that work
    and 35 that fail, and opens 79 cursors that stay open. They find their
    place again 59 times after a change, and all end, and the test checks
    798 times that an ended one stays ended.
  - `TestTransactions` runs S2's 12 seeds of 300 transactions. A third of
    the steps in a statement are reads through the transaction: gets,
    scans, and the opening of a cursor that stays open, one at a time. A
    step pulls a record from it half the time. It carries on across the
    transaction's changes and the statements it takes back, and must give
    nothing once the transaction ends. Two readers alongside scan every
    table in every read. Seed 1 makes 113 scans that work and 10 that fail,
    and opens 117 cursors. They find their place again 39 times, are open
    over 58 statements taken back, and are still open at 79 ends.
  - `TestChangeLists` compares scans of every table between A and B, and
    between A and C, and every dump of two stores compares their keys.
- **0.x's Scan cases pass, on 0.x and on the store side by side.**
  - `TestScan` is 0.x's TestScan and the suite's Scan, with 15 more cases,
    and 13 prefixes and limits that must fail with 0.x's errors in 0.x's
    order. Each case must give the same records from both, with the same
    fields and without vectors.
  - `TestScanInsideATransaction` scans inside 0.x's `Update` and through a
    `Tx` after the same changes, and both must give the same records. After
    the rollback, both give none of the changes.
  - `TestTheStoreAgrees0x` makes an eighth of its 3,000 random steps
    scans, with random prefixes, afters and limits, some of them negative.
    The two must agree on every record and every error message. 322 scans
    work, and 29 fail.
- **Inserts and deletes stay cheap at 100,000 keys.**
  `TestKeyOrderTimings` gave these on this two-core machine, with two
  other agents at work on it:

| 100,000 keys | Time | A key |
|---|---|---|
| In, in byte order, as a compacted file gives them | 2 ms | 20 ns |
| In, counting up: docs:0, docs:1 and so on | 23 to 33 ms | 230 to 330 ns |
| In, in a random order | 40 to 57 ms | 400 to 570 ns |
| Out, in a random order | 35 to 38 ms | 350 to 380 ns |
| A scan of them all | 0.15 ms | 1.5 ns |

At a million keys, in a run by hand, the same took 38 ms, 237 ms, 845 ms,
739 ms and 2.7 ms. The whole of `LoadBatch` for 100,000 records of two
fields takes 75 to 110 ms in byte order and 135 to 215 ms in a random
order, so the keys are a small part of opening.

- **Bugs planted by hand,** one at a time: 43 in the new code, of which 38
  made the tests fail. The first round let ten through, and five of those
  led to `TestKeyOrderJoins`, `TestACursorThroughRollbacks` and the check of
  each block's first key in `TestKeyOrder`. The other five change nothing:
  the cap of `blockMax` in `grow`, which capacities that double from 8
  never pass; a second `done = true` and the first `seek` in `scan`; and
  the epoch's moves in `newTable` and in undoing a new table, which a
  cursor can't see, since the keys' own moves come with them.
  `sh scripts/planted.sh` reports 41 of 41 caught, in about two and a half
  minutes, about 5 seconds of it for each new one.
- `go vet ./...`, `go test -short ./...`, `go test -race` on `store`, and
  `sh scripts/check-beta.sh` pass. The store's tests take 5.1 seconds, 28
  with `-race`, and 1.2 and 6.4 with `-short`.

## Decisions settled

**The structure: a list of blocks.** Each table's keys sit in blocks of up
to 256 keys in byte order, each key with its record. Every key in a block
comes before every key in the next.

- Finding a key's place is a binary search over the blocks' first keys,
  then one inside the block. An insert looks first in the block the last
  insert went into, which saves the search over the list when a program
  counts up.
- An insert moves at most 255 keys in its block. A full block splits into
  halves, which moves the blocks after it in the list: about one block for
  every 128 to 256 keys. A key after every key goes on the end, with no
  search, and starts a new block when the last one is full, so a compacted
  file loads into whole blocks.
- A delete moves the keys after it in its block. A block below 64 keys
  joins its smaller neighbour when the two hold 192 keys at most, and an
  empty block leaves the list.
- 64, 128 and 512 keys a block were no faster at 100,000 keys. A B-tree
  with more levels would move less at several million keys, where the list
  of blocks gets long. A million keys in a random order make about 5,600
  blocks, and a split moves up to that many block headers, 24 bytes each.
- Each key sits beside its record's pointer, 24 bytes a key, so a search
  reads the blocks and the keys' bytes and leaves the records alone. A
  table's first block starts at 8 keys and doubles, so a small table takes
  little memory. The places past a block's length are cleared, so a block
  never keeps a record alive.
- A random insert spends most of its time in cache misses on the keys it
  compares. Keeping each key's first 8 bytes in its entry would save most
  of them, if puts in a random order turn out to matter (T5).

**Undo.** Two kinds, made before each change to a table's keys, by `list`
and `unlist` in `write.go`:

| Kind | Made before | Holds | Put back by |
|---|---|---|---|
| `undoNoKey` | a key added to its table's keys | the table and the record | taking the key out |
| `undoHadKey` | a key taken out of its table's keys | the table and the record | putting the record's key back |

- Each is put back by key, so a block that split or joined since needn't be
  put back as it was. Only which keys a table holds counts.
- A delete is `remove`: `unhash`, with S2's `undoHadRecord`, then `unlist`.
  A put that makes a record makes S2's `undoNoRecord`, then calls `list`.
- A drop takes each of its records out of the hash table with `unhash`,
  and leaves the table's keys whole with the table, as S2's note said. So
  undoing a drop takes S2's kinds alone.

**Scan.**

- `Scan(prefix, after)` checks the prefix as 0.x does, in 0.x's order: a
  colon, then the table's name by `rules.Table`, with 0.x's messages. The
  rest of the prefix and all of after can be anything, as in 0.x.
- The records are the table's keys that start with the prefix and come
  after after, in byte order. That's 0.x's `key >= prefix AND key > after
  AND key < prefixEnd(prefix)` in SQLite's byte order. A table that doesn't
  exist gives none.
- The vector stays in the `Record`, where SQL needs it, and the public
  package leaves it out.

**Cursors.**

- A cursor keeps its place in its table's blocks, with the store's epoch
  when it found it. Every change to which tables there are and to which
  keys a table holds moves the epoch on, undone changes included. Then the
  cursor finds its place again: the first key after the last one it gave,
  in the table as it is now. So a cursor through a transaction carries on
  through the transaction's changes, gives no key twice, and sees a key
  added after its place. That's more than P3 promises for what a read hands
  out, which is good only until the transaction's next change.
- A cursor from `Read` is good until the read ends. Nothing changes the
  store during a read, so the epoch stays put.
- Once `Next` gives false, it always does. A cursor through a transaction
  gives false once the transaction has ended, as database/sql's rows do
  once they're closed, and doesn't read the store from then on.
- `Next` allocates nothing. Besides the record, it costs a look at the
  epoch and a comparison with the prefix.
- Only the goroutine that has the store moves the epoch: the transaction,
  under the copy's lock from its first change, or a goroutine that has the
  store to itself. Readers only read it, under the shared lock.

## Notes for later tasks

- **G2:** `DB.Scan` and `Tx.Scan` do what `scanGo` in `scan_test.go` does:
  - `Reader.Scan(prefix, after)` first, inside `Read` or through the
    transaction, which gives 0.x's errors for a bad prefix;
  - then 0.x's check of the limit, `fmt.Errorf("%w: limit %d", ErrInvalid,
    limit)` for a limit below 0, which 0.x checks after the prefix;
  - then at most limit records, or all of them for 0, each as `Fields`
    through `Table.Fields` and `Value.Go`, with the vector left out, and
    nil when there are none, as 0.x gives.

  The store needs nothing more for it.
- **Q4 and Q5:** a table's scan is `r.Scan(table+":", "")`, which gives the
  rows in key order, as SQL.md says. A cursor stays good while a statement
  writes, though SQL.md has a statement read everything before it writes.
- **Q6:** an UPDATE's or a DELETE's records, in key order, come from a scan
  collected before the writes.
- **S5:** `remove` is where a deleted record's links go, beside `unhash`
  and `unlist`. The loop over its table's keys in `drop` is where each
  dropped record's links go, both ways.
- **I1 and V2:** `LoadBatch` of 100,000 records of two fields, in byte
  order, takes 75 to 110 ms on this machine, of which the keys take about
  2. That's before vectors, the log and the codec, against an open target
  of 250 ms with 384-value vectors. The rest is S1's put path: the rules,
  the strings it copies, the hash table and the merge of fields.
- **T5:** a put in a random order spends 400 to 570 ns in the keys at
  100,000. If the target for puts needs that back, keep each key's first 8
  bytes after the table's name in its entry, and compare those first.
- **The coordinator:** `beta/README.md`'s line for `internal/store/` can
  read: "the in-memory copy, with its read API, `Reader`. So far it holds
  the records with their fields, each table's field list, and each table's
  keys in byte order, which `Scan` reads through a `Cursor`. Its writes
  give the change lists the log writes, `ApplyBatch` and `LoadBatch` take a
  batch the log has read whole or not at all, and `Snapshot` gives the copy
  as a compacted part. Reads share it through `Read`, and writes go through
  a transaction from `Begin`, which holds readers off from its first change
  until it ends, and puts the copy back with its undo list when it rolls
  back." `beta/plants.txt` has six new lines for the store.
