# F8: Compaction

Status: done, 8 October 2026.

## The contract

From BETA.md: compaction: `NAME.compact` with the database's permissions,
sync, rename, directory sync, cleanup on failure, writers waiting it out.
Done when T3 passes with a cut at every write and a failure at every call
during a compaction, and a copy taken mid-compaction opens.

## What was built

All of it is in `beta/internal/logfile`.

- **`compact.go`,** new. The comment at its top sets out the steps and the
  rules.
  - `Compact(snapshot iter.Seq[format.Change]) error`, the log's new call,
    for the holder of the write lock. `startCompact` makes and locks
    `NAME.compact`, `writeCompact` writes the compacted part and then the
    header, and `switchTo` moves the Log over to the new file. `dropCompact`
    is the end of a compaction that failed before the switch.
  - `Due() bool`, which says when a compaction is due.
  - `removeCompact`, which removes a leftover `NAME.compact`, and
    `compacting`, a waiter's look for a compaction under way. `hold` and
    `until` make a wait for the lock go on while one runs.
  - `changeSize`, the bytes a change takes in a batch, which the compacted
    part's batches are split by.
- **`log.go`:** `busy`, `retry` and `part` in `Log`. `takeMutex` and `flock`
  wait past their deadline while a compaction runs. `lockFile` removes a

- **`check.go`:** `checkLocked`, the check Open and Follow make holding the

## The result

- **T3 passes with a cut at every write and a failure at every call during a
  compaction, and a copy taken mid-compaction opens.**
  `TestCompactionPassesEveryPoint` runs the driver on a workload whose Setup
  makes 12 commits through the store, as the public package makes them:
  puts that write over five records in turn, vectors, links, deletes and a
  table made and dropped, so the file holds dead data. Run opens the
  database, makes the commit that sets the compaction off, compacts under the
  same lock from a read of the copy, and makes two commits more, which go
  into the compacted file. The compacted part's batches are set to 200
  bytes, so it takes seven. A state is the copy's snapshot as text, the same
  however a file holds the live data, and every open also requires that no
  `NAME.compact` is left beside the database.

  A run makes 57 calls, 18 of them the compaction's, and the power can be
  cut in 17 of them, 11 of them the compaction's: its file's creation, its
  seven batches, its header, the rename and the folder's sync.

  | Case | Calls a run | Seeds | Points | Opens at the last commit that succeeded | Opens at a later one |
  |---|---|---|---|---|---|
  | A cut at every write | 57 | 128 | 2,304 cuts | 3,714 | 894 |
  | A failure at every call | 57 | 24 | 1,368 failures | 2,736 | 0 |
  | A failure at every call, on a disk that dies | 57 | 24 | 1,368 failures | 2,448 | 288 |
  | Copies | 57 | 4 | 232 copies | 416 | 48 |
  | Copies read 100 bytes at a time | 57 | 4 | 232 copies | 152 | 312 |
  | A leftover `NAME.compact`, and a cut at every write | 62 | 32 | 608 cuts | 994 | 222 |
  | A leftover, and a failure at every call | 62 | 6 | 372 failures | 744 | 0 |

## Decisions settled

**The log's API** gains two calls.

| Call | What it does |
|---|---|
| `(*Log) Compact(snapshot iter.Seq[format.Change]) error` | holding the write lock, writes the snapshot into `NAME.compact`, renames it over the database and moves the Log over to it, still locked |
| `(*Log) Due() bool` | holding the write lock, whether a compaction is due; it makes no call |

- The snapshot is the store's `Snapshot`, from a `Read` taken after the last
  commit with the write lock held throughout, so it holds what the log has
  read. The Target is told nothing, since it holds that state already: no
  `Reset`, no batch.
- After Compact the Log holds the new file, locked, with the compacted part
  as its log, and the next Append under the same lock goes in after it,
  numbered on from the compacted part's last batch.

**The steps,** in this order:

1. An fstat of the database for its permissions and owner. `NAME.compact`
   is made with `O_EXCL` and the database's permissions exactly. When the
   name is taken, it's removed as a leftover would be, and made once more.
2. Its flock, tried up to 100 times a millisecond apart, since a waiter's
   look takes it for a moment when it can. Then a stat that the name still
   leads to the file, as newFile checks a `.new-` file's, and `Chown` when
   the owner or group differs, its refusal ignored.
3. The compacted part from offset 68. A batch ends at the change that would
   take its changes past a megabyte, and before the first link, as in P2's
   fixture; a change longer than that goes in a batch of its own. The puts'
   fields go into one slice for the batch, since the snapshot uses its own
   again. Then the header, written last: generation N+1, continuing from
   generation N, the last batch's number and checksum, taken from its
   marker, or 0 and 0, and the end of the compacted part.
4. One sync of the new file.
5. A stat of the path: when it names another file than the one compacted, a
   backup moved into place meanwhile, the compaction fails before the
   switch. This goes past the brief, at the cost of one call, so a restore
   made during a compaction is never replaced by the old data.
6. The rename. When it fails, a stat of the path says whether it took effect
   all the same; then it counts as the switch.
7. The folder's sync.
8. The switch: the Log takes the new file over, and closes the old one,
   which lets go of its lock. The new file's lock stays held as the write
   lock, and the caller's Unlock lets go of it and then the mutex.

**Failures.**

- Before the switch, from step 1 to step 6, `NAME.compact` is removed while
  it's still locked, then closed, and the Log carries on with the old file.
  When the removal fails, the next holder of the write lock removes the
  file. The error says the database carries on in its old file, and wraps
  the call's, and the removal's when that failed. A panic while the
  snapshot is ranged over removes it too, then carries on.
- After a failed compaction, the next isn't due until the file has grown by
  as much again as it had grown since its last compaction, so a disk without
  room for a second copy isn't written to the full after every commit.
  Compact itself always tries.
- After the rename, a failed folder sync moves the Log over to the new file
  all the same, closes the old one, and sets `stuck`, as `adopt` does, so the
  Log keeps the new file's lock until Close. Other writers on the old file
  get its lock, find the new file at the path, and wait for that one's lock
  until they time out.

**Leftovers.** The holder of the write lock removes a `NAME.compact` once
the inode check has passed: at every Lock, in `lockFile`, and in the checks
Open and Follow make holding the lock (`checkLocked`). With nothing there it
costs one call, an open that fails. It removes a regular file it can lock,
while the name still leads to the file it locked, as `removeLeftovers` does
for `.new-` files. A locked one stays: a compaction under way in a process
that locked an older file, or a waiter's look. Nobody without the lock
removes one. This is half of F9's contract, done here, since the brief
asked for it and its tests.

**Writers waiting it out.**

- In another process, a waiter finds `NAME.compact` locked. Each time flock
  finds the database locked, it opens `NAME.compact` and tries its lock, and
  when that's held, it notes the time (`hold`). With nothing there it costs
  one call a try, every 16 milliseconds at most.
- In this process, the compacting Log notes it itself: `busy` is the largest
  time there is while Compact runs, and Options.Wait after its end once it
  returns.
- A wait, for the mutex or for flock, ends at its deadline or at `busy`,
  whichever is later (`until`). So it ends Options.Wait after the
  compaction was last seen under way. That covers the moments after the
  rename, while the folder is synced and the locks go, and holds writers
  behind a compaction that stuck at its folder's sync no longer than behind
  a stuck commit. The mutex's waiters read `busy` too, so they wait while
  the mutex's holder waits for a compaction in another process.
- `confirm` waits through flock, so Open and Follow waiting to confirm damage
  wait a compaction out too, as F4.md said, and read the new file.

**When to compact.** `Due` measures the end of the log against the end of
the compacted part, or the header in generation 1: due at twice that, and
at least a megabyte past it. That's all the log knows of the live data
without a pass over the copy, and every process reads it from the file, so
they agree. A database whose commits write over its data compacts when it
holds about twice its live data, as BETA.md says. One that only grows
compacts each time it doubles too, which rewrites its data about once more
in all, pauses writers at each doubling, and has other processes reload. A
pass over the copy to measure the live data would cost about a third of a
compaction's processor time, since the store's snapshot copies every
vector, and no process could tell another what it found, so every process
would pay it at its first commit after each doubling, and the command on
every run. The megabyte keeps a small database from compacting every few
commits: one record written over again and again compacts once a megabyte.

**Not settled here:** how the public package reports a compaction that
fails after its commit, which is G5's (Notes).

## Notes for later tasks

- **G5, the public package's `Compact()` and the compaction after a commit:**
  - In `Update`, once `stx.Commit(db.write)` has returned nil, before the
    deferred Unlock, holding the lock still:

    ```go
    if db.log.Due() {
    	// The commit has succeeded, whatever the compaction does.
    	db.mem.Load().Read(func(r store.Reader) error {
    		return db.log.Compact(r.Snapshot())
    	})
    }
    ```

    The commit has succeeded whatever Compact returns, so Update should
    return nil. A compaction that failed before the switch leaves the
    database whole in its old file, and is tried again once `Due` says so.
    One that stuck makes every later write fail with `ErrStuck`. G5 may
    want to keep the error somewhere `check` can show it.
  - `Compact()`: `s.Outside()` first, as Update does, then `db.log.Lock()`,
    the same `Read` with `db.log.Compact`, and `Unlock`, returning
    Compact's error. A Compact under way in another goroutine of the process
    makes other writers wait.
  - The command's `compact` verb opens the database, calls `Compact()` and
    closes it.
  - The lock order holds: the log's mutex and flock, then the copy's lock
    held shared by `Read`. Readers in the process go on reading during the
    compaction, and Follow returns at once while the mutex is held.
  - `hypercrux.Update`'s comment on the wait could say that writers wait
    past 10 seconds while a compaction runs, until 10 seconds after it ends.
- **F9, reloading:** a compaction in another process gives `ErrReplaced`
  from Follow, as a backup moved into place does, and Lock moves to the new
  file by itself. The leftover `NAME.compact` after the inode check is done,
  in Lock and in `checkLocked`, so F9's contract needs only the reload. This
  process's own compaction needs no reload. `TestFollowersFindTheCompactedFile`
  is the shape for a follower's test.
- **F7, the file rules:** `NAME.compact` is `l.path` with `.compact` added, so
  a real path set in Open is the one it sits beside. Refusing a file with
  two names matters to compaction, which replaces one name only.
- **V2, and the store:** a compaction spends half its processor time in
  `format.AppendBatch`, most of it checking every vector value again, and a
  third in the snapshot copying each vector into a `value.Vector`. An
  encoder for changes the store has checked already, exported from `format`
  (F1.md's note on `appendChange`), and a snapshot that hands out a slot's
  bytes as they are, would take the rate towards BETA.md's estimate.
- **I1, and F9's closing test:** a many-process workload through
  compactions can compact on a schedule, or once `Due` says so after a
  commit, as `crashCommit` does.
- **R1, long runs:** `HYPERCRUX_CRASH_SEEDS` raises the closing test's seeds,
  and `HYPERCRUX_COMPACT_RECORDS` sets the rate test's size.
