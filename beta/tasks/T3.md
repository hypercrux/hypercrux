# T3: Crash-point driver

Status: done, 8 October 2026.

## The contract

From BETA.md: the crash-point driver: a cut at every write and a failure at
every call, then a reopen compared with the model, plus a copy taken
mid-commit. Done when it catches a planted "marker before sync" bug in a
toy log.

## What was built

All of it is in `beta/internal/crash`, a new package that imports `fault`
and `fsys` alone from the Beta. Its tests import `format`, `value` and
`logfile` as well.

- **`crash.go`:** the API. `Workload`, with a `Path`, an optional `Setup`,
  `Run` and `Reopen`; `Model`, with `Begin` and `Done`; `Options`; `Kind`,
  which is `Cuts`, `Failures` or `Copies`; `Point`, with `String` and
  `ParsePoint`; `Failure`, with its `Problem`; and `Report`.
- **`run.go`:** `Run`, which counts the calls, runs every point with every
  seed, and checks the database after each.
- **`proc.go`:** the thin `fsys.FS` of the driver's own over the disk's. A
  `proc` is a process whose open files the driver closes when it ends the
  process, as Linux does, and it counts its calls and lets a copy read
  just before each one. The `copier` reads the database's file through
  another open, from the start, a chunk at a time, as cp does.
- **`plant_off.go` and `plant_on.go`:** one planted bug,
  `crash/marker-before-sync`, in the toy log, with its line in
  `beta/plants.txt`.
- **Tests:**
  - `toy_test.go`: the toy log, a header-free sequence of batches in the
    codec's encoding, each written, synced and then marked, with recovery
    enough to read it back. Its workloads: a new log, a log opened again
    after `Setup`, and a log compacted midway, with the new file renamed
    over the old.
  - `crash_test.go`: the closing test in two halves; the driver catching a
    skipped sync, a marker ahead of its batch, workloads that change and a
    workload that fails; a process ending with its files; a copy's chunks;
    points read back; and the two variables.
  - `real_test.go`: copies of the real log, F2's, taken mid-commit.

## The result

- **The driver catches the planted "marker before sync" bug.**
  `TestTheDriverCatchesMarkerBeforeSync` writes each marker before its
  batch's sync, in a toy log of 16 commits:
  - With cuts alone, the cut in WriteAt 2, the first marker's write, with
    seed 39 kept the marker and tore the batch. The toy log opened as
    damaged, with a whole marker past the end of the log.
  - With failures alone, the failed sync in call 5 with seed 0 left the
    batch and its marker in the page cache. A process opened the database
    at commit 1, and after a cut it opened at commit 0, so a commit a
    reader had seen was lost.
  - Replayed alone from its point and seed, each failure came back word for
    word.
  - With the plant switched on, `TestTheToyLogPassesEveryPoint` fails in
    the four of its five cases that cut or fail calls, and
    `sh scripts/planted.sh` reports 22 of 22. In an ordinary build `plant`
    is the constant "", so the bug is built in only with the
    `hypercrux_planted` tag.
- **The toy log without the bug passes every point.**
  `TestTheToyLogPassesEveryPoint` runs five cases with 12 seeds each, 3
  with `-short`: 5,886 points and 11,772 opens. Every open found the last
  commit that succeeded or one begun after it, and both came up often.

  | Case | Calls a run | Cuts | Failures | Copies | Opens at the last commit that succeeded | Opens at a later one |
  |---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
  | A new log of 16 commits | 51 | 420 | 612 | 612 | 1,272 | 2,016 |
  | 8 commits more, after `Setup`'s 8 | 30 | 216 | 360 | 372 | 886 | 1,010 |
  | Copies read 100 bytes at a time, from 2 seeds | 51 | | | 102 | 16 | 188 |
  | 8 commits, a compaction, then 8 more | 80 | 660 | 960 | 960 | 2,792 | 2,368 |
  | Every call failing from the one picked on | 51 | | 612 | | 456 | 768 |

  The cuts come in every write and every call on names, and once after the
  last call. The failures come at every call, and the copies from every
  call on and once the workload had ended. 26 copy points came before the
  file existed.
- **How often a point finds a bug,** out of 300 seeds at a point, in the
  16-commit toy log:
  - marker before sync, by a cut in a marker's write: 6 to 18 seeds, 2 to 6
    per cent, always as damage;
  - marker before sync, by a failed sync: 229 to 248 seeds, 76 to 83 per
    cent, mostly as a lost commit;
  - a skipped sync, by a cut in a write or after the last call: 220 to 262
    seeds.

  Cuts find the first rarely, and with mostly the same seeds at every
  point, since the disk draws nothing from its seed before the cut.
- **Copies:** a copy taken between a marker written ahead of its batch and
  the batch's write opens as damaged. A copy that opened the toy log before
  the compaction's rename read the old file to its end, and opened at a
  state the model allows. Of the real log, 90 copies taken mid-commit from
  the database's creation on all opened at an allowed state, with 16 copy
  points, over 2 seeds, before the file was renamed into place.
- **The tests catch a wrong driver.** Of 17 bugs put into the driver by
  hand one at a time, the tests caught all 17. Among them: an ended process
  keeping its files open, no second open after a cut, a copy's states taken
  from its end, every state since the start allowed, failures made into
  cuts, one cut point short, the copy left unsynced, and every seed the
  same. Two were missed at first, a cut point whose cut never came and a
  rule left in place for the check, and the tests that catch them were
  added. The toy log's recovery syncing a batch without writing it again,
  the case FORMAT.md writes a batch again for, was caught too, by a failed
  sync and then a cut.
- **Workloads that can't be driven are reported:** one that makes a commit
  fewer after its first run, one that swaps a write for a stat, and one
  that fails with nothing done to it.
- **F2's log today:** with cuts, the driver finds the last commit lost at a
  cut after the run, since its marker isn't synced and nothing writes it
  again. That's F3's to fix, so the real log's test runs copies alone for
  now.
- `go vet ./...`, `go test -short ./...`, `go test -race` on
  `beta/internal/crash` with and without `-short`, `sh scripts/planted.sh`
  and `sh scripts/check-beta.sh` pass. The package's tests take about 0.9
  seconds, 0.2 with `-short`, 6 under `-race` and 1.8 under `-race -short`.

## Decisions settled

**The API.** A test gives `Run` a `Workload` and `Options`, and gets back a
`Report`, or a `*Failure` for the first point that fails.

- `Workload.Run(sys, m)` makes the commits through `sys`, and calls
  `m.Begin(state)` before each one and `m.Done()` once it has succeeded.
  `Reopen(sys)` opens the database and returns its state. A state is a
  string the test chooses, compared as a string, so the driver needs
  nothing from the engine.
- `Setup`, when it's given, runs first on every new disk with no faults,
  and its state counts as committed. Without it, the state before the
  first commit is what `Reopen` finds on a new disk.
- `Options.Kinds` picks the kinds of point, all three by default. `Seeds`
  and `Seed` set the seeds each point runs with. `CutIn` names the kinds of
  call to cut in. `Err` and `Times` set a failure's error and how many
  calls it lasts. `Chunk` sets a copy's reads, and `Replay` runs one point
  alone.
- A `Failure` names its point and its `Problem`: `NotOpened`,
  `NotAllowed`, `Lost`, `RunFailed` or `Diverged`. Its reason numbers
  commits from 1 in the order they began, and calls the starting state
  commit 0's.

**What the model allows.** After a cut or a failure, the state of the last
commit that succeeded, or of any commit begun after it: the one under way,
or one that failed, since a failed commit's outcome is unknown. A workload
may carry on after a failed commit, and gives later commits the states
they'd have without it, since a log makes a failed batch unusable before
it lets go of the lock, or else refuses every write after it.

**The check.** Each run starts on a new disk, `fault.New(seed)`, with the
database's folder and whatever `Setup` leaves. Once `Run` returns, the
driver clears the rule and ends the workload's process by closing every
file it left open, so its flocks go and the page cache keeps its writes.
Then:

- After a cut, it opens the database on the disk the cut left. After a
  failure, and after a run with nothing done to it, it opens it as a
  process started once the workload's had ended, with no cut.
- Then it cuts the power again and opens the database once more. That
  state has to be allowed too, and no earlier than the first open's. A
  reader applies a batch only once it's on the drive, so a commit a reader
  has seen has to last, and so does whatever the first open did to recover
  the file. This second open is what finds a commit lost after a failed
  sync, and a recovery that marks a batch it hasn't made last.
- Either open failing is a failure, since the database has to open after
  every point.

**The points,** in this order, each with every seed before the next:

- Cuts: one after the last call, then one in each call of the kinds in
  `CutIn`. By default those are the calls that change what a cut can leave
  on the disk, data or names: `WriteAt`, `Truncate`, `Create`, `Rename`,
  `RenameNoReplace`, `Remove` and `SyncDir`. A cut in a write or a truncate
  comes once the call has reached the page cache, and in any other call
  before it, so a cut in a `SyncDir` comes after the names it would make
  last (T2). A cut in a `Sync` would come straight after the write before
  it, where there's a cut already, so it's left out.
- Failures: each call in turn, with `syscall.EIO` unless `Err` says
  otherwise.
- Copies: from each call on, and once the workload has ended.

**Counting and determinism.** One run with no fault counts the calls with
the disk's `Calls`, from after `Setup`, and a rule counts from when it's
set, so the numbers match. A cut or a failure that never came, a clean run
that makes another number of calls, and a copy that was to start past the
last call are failures with the problem `Diverged`, since the workload
hasn't made the same calls in every run. So a workload makes its file
calls one at a time, from one goroutine.

**The copy mid-commit.** The workload's file system is the driver's `proc`
over the disk's. For a copy point, just before the workload's call N, the
copier opens the database's path through another process on the same disk
and reads the file from its start, `Chunk` bytes at a time: one read just
before each of the workload's calls from N on, and the rest once the
workload has ended, until a read comes back short. That's how cp reads a
file: it opens it once and reads through it, and a file renamed or removed
meanwhile goes on being read. It reads what the page cache holds.

- The default chunk is 128 KiB, cp's buffer, so a small file is copied
  between two calls. A smaller chunk spreads a copy over several.
- The copy may hold the states from the last commit that had succeeded
  when it began to the last one begun before it ended.
- It goes onto a new disk of its own, synced with its folder, and is
  checked as above.
- Copies come only in runs with nothing else done to them. A copy can
  catch the end of the log while a failed commit cuts it and writes it
  again, which FORMAT.md's "Copies" says reads as damage.
- A copy point before the file exists takes no copy, and the report counts
  it.

**Seeds and long runs.** Every point runs with `Seeds` seeds from `Seed`,
1 by default, and `HYPERCRUX_CRASH_SEEDS` overrides it for R1. The disk
draws nothing from its seed before the cut, so a seed makes the same draws
at every point. More of what a cut can do turns up with more seeds, and
more points mostly find the same seeds again. The tests size themselves
with `-short`.

**Replays.** A failure names its point and seed as `Point.String` writes
them, such as `cut WriteAt 2 seed 39`. `Options.Replay`, or
`HYPERCRUX_CRASH_POINT` when that's empty, runs that point alone. The
variable reaches every `Run` in a test binary, so a replay runs one test
alone, as the failure's message says.

**The toy log,** in the tests:

- A header-free sequence of batches, each made by `format.AppendBatch`,
  then synced, then followed by its marker from `format.AppendMarker`,
  with a fixed ID and generation 1. Its file is created and its folder
  synced, so names lost at a cut (T2) can't take it.
- Opening reads the marked batches. Past the end of the log, a whole
  marker naming the next batch or a later one is damage, and opening fails,
  which is how the planted bug shows. A batch that counts at the end
  without its marker is written again, synced and marked, since a marker
  isn't synced and a cut can take the marker of a commit that succeeded.
- Opening it for more commits then cuts off what lies past the last
  marker and syncs the cut.
- The planted bug writes the marker before the sync. The tests also set the
  toy log's bug themselves, to show the driver catching it in an ordinary
  build, along with two more: the sync left out, and the marker written
  before its batch.

## Notes for later tasks

- **F3, recovery:** in `logfile`'s tests, a `Workload` whose `Run` opens
  the log on the `sys` it's given, and for each commit calls `m.Begin` with
  the state after it, then `Lock`, then `Append`, then `m.Done` once
  `Append` returns nil, then `Unlock`. `Reopen` opens the log with a
  recorder, which F3's check of the end of the log runs in, closes it, and
  returns what the recorder was handed. `realWorkload` in
  `crash/real_test.go` has that shape, and `Options{Kinds: crash.Cuts}` is
  F3's closing test. Two things to know:
  - The log takes its database ID and `.new-` names from `crypto/rand`, so
    every marker's bytes differ from run to run. A torn sector's split falls
    between the first and last bytes that differ, so the disk's draws can
    differ too, and a seed may not replay exactly. A way for tests to fix
    the ID, an option or a variable for the source, makes it replay.
  - A cut finds `logfile/marker-before-sync` in a few per cent of seeds, so
    F3's test wants a hundred seeds or more for cuts, or failures too,
    which find it at once.
- **F5, failed commits:** `Options{Kinds: crash.Failures}`, and again with
  `Times: -1` for a disk that dies, where the cut back fails and the handle
  sticks. A workload may carry on after a failed commit, as `Model` says.
  The driver ends the workload's process by closing its files, which is how
  a stuck handle lets go of its lock. The check that a stuck handle blocks
  other writers while it's open is F5's own.
- **F8, compaction:** `Setup` builds the database up to the commit that
  sets compaction off, so the points fall in the compaction. The default
  `CutIn` cuts in `Create`, `Rename` and `SyncDir` already.
  `toyCompactWorkload` has the shape: a copy that opened the database
  before the rename reads the old file to its end, which is why a copy's
  states start from the last commit that had succeeded when it began.
- **T2, names:** the toy log syncs its folder once it has created the
  file, and so does the copy's disk, so name loss at a cut leaves both
  where they are. If T2's cut draws more from the seed, the tests' seeds
  give other outcomes, and the tests that catch bugs keep a wide margin: 256
  seeds for the rare one.
- **T4 and F6:** the driver drives one process at a time. Readers beside a
  writer need T4's harness, since goroutines making file calls side by side
  don't replay (T1.md).
- **R1, long runs:** `HYPERCRUX_CRASH_SEEDS` raises the seeds of every
  point in every test that uses the driver.
- **The real log's copies test** is in `crash`'s tests and uses
  `logfile`'s API, so a task that changes that API updates it. F3 can move
  it into `logfile`'s tests beside its own.
- **Shared files:** `beta/plants.txt` has the new line. `beta/README.md`
  needs an entry for the package, and the board needs T3 marked done; the
  report gives the wording. P3.md's table of packages could gain a row for
  `beta/internal/crash`. No interface changed.
