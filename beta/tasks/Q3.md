# Q3: Parser

Status: done, 8 October 2026.

## The contract

From BETA.md: the parser: the whole subset, with the walk forms, the
one-record subquery, `INSERT`, `UPDATE`, `DELETE` and parameters. Done when
every corpus statement parses or is refused as marked, and a fuzz of the
parser runs clean.

## What was built

All of it is in `beta/internal/query`, beside P3's `Rows`, which is as it
was.

- **`lex.go`,** the lexer: SQLite's `sqlite3GetToken`, token for token. It
  knows SQLite's tokens that the subset leaves out, such as hex numbers,
  `?1`, `:name` and backquoted names, so each is refused with a message of
  its own.
- **`parse.go`,** the statements: `Parse`, `ParseCondition` for `Nearest`'s
  filter, the `Error` type, and SELECT, INSERT, UPDATE and DELETE with every
  clause and form of SQL.md's grammar.
- **`expr.go`,** the expressions: a parser that climbs SQL.md's nine levels,
  with BETWEEN's AND, LIKE's ESCAPE, the signs, the one-record subquery,
  walks in IN, CAST and calls.
- **`check.go`,** the rules beside the grammar that need no database:
  functions and their arguments, where fields and aggregates may go, what a
  write names, and SQLite's limit on how tall expressions grow.
- **`tree.go`,** the tree, and **`print.go`,** which prints a tree back as
  SQL. **`words.go`** holds the reserved words, the words SQLite reads as
  keywords in some places, the functions, SQLite's own function names and
  SQLite's limits.
- **Eleven planted bugs,** in `plant_on.go` and `beta/plants.txt`:
  `query/and-or-one-level`, `query/between-takes-comparisons`,
  `query/sign-takes-operators`, `query/plus-kept`, `query/left-as-alias`,
  `query/params-from-one`, `query/text-without-comments`,
  `query/aggregate-in-where`, `query/record-key-takes-or`,
  `query/is-not-printed-bare` and `query/slash-star-comment`.
- **Tests:**
  - `corpus_test.go`: `TestTheCorpusParsesAsMarked`, the closing test;
    `TestTreesPrintBack`, on every piece of SQL in the corpus;
    `TestColumnNamesMatchTheCorpus`; and
    `TestTheCorpusFormsAndQueriesAfterParse`.
  - `parse_test.go`: `TestPrecedence`, `TestStatements`,
    `TestNamesAndKeywords`, `TestParams`, `TestLiterals`, `TestResultText`,
    `TestPlaces`, `TestRefusals` (288 cases),
    `TestTheLimitsHoldAtTheEdge`, `TestConditions` and
    `TestTheTreeIsWhatTheTestsSay`.
  - `lex_test.go`: `TestTokens`, `TestTheTextEndsAtNUL` and `TestUnquote`.
  - `fuzz_test.go`: `FuzzParse`, seeded from the corpus.
  - `sqlite_test.go`, built with cgo only: the parser against SQLite itself,
    through 0.x. `TestTreesAreSQLitesOnTheCorpus`,
    `TestTreesAreSQLitesOnRandomExpressions` and
    `TestKeywordsReadAsSQLiteReadsThem`.
  - `dump_test.go`: a tree written as a short S-expression, without its
    places in the text, which the tests compare.

## The result

- **Every corpus case parses or is refused as its mark says.** Of the 3,911
  cases:

  | Cases | How many | What the test holds them to |
  |---|---|---|
  | Marked in | 3,227 | they parse |
  | Marked in, a syntax error in 0.x too | 19 | refused with the same kind of message: incomplete input, a syntax error or an unrecognized token |
  | Marked in, refused here | 3 | refused, as 0.x refuses them: `abs(1, 2)`, `unknownfunc(1)`, and an INSERT without `key` |
  | Marked out | 51 | refused, with a message that says what isn't taken |
  | Marked out, for a later stage | 4 | they parse: `json_each`'s `key` column, `sqlite_schema`, `hc_links` and `rowid`, which need the tables to find |
  | Dates on fixed dates, marked out | 607 | they parse, and each holds a `date()` or `datetime()` on something other than `'now'`, which Q2 refuses |

  The 608th date case, `date(NULL, '+1 day')`, is refused here, since
  `date()` takes text literals only. The 19 syntax errors include the
  expressions where `--` begins a comment, and an OR in BETWEEN's lower
  bound. The test names the cases it treats differently and holds each to
  its reason, so the lists stay true.
- **Printing a tree back gives the same tree,** for all 3,868 pieces of SQL
  in the corpus that parse (statements, 0.x's forms and the queries after
  writes), for the strict printing too, and in the fuzz.
- **The trees are SQLite's.** The cgo tests print every tree strictly, with
  every operator's operands in parentheses, so SQLite reads exactly the
  parser's tree, and run it through 0.x on the corpus's data:
  - 3,819 corpus cases give 0.x's recorded answers, printed strictly and as
    `String` prints them, apart from the names of columns;
  - 20,000 random expressions, half of them flat runs of operators over
    small values, give the same answer as written and printed strictly
    (19,454 taken), and every one the parser refuses as a syntax error 0.x
    refuses too;
  - each of SQLite's 147 keywords, and seven other names such as TRUE and
    ROWID, in 47 places where the parser takes a bare name: 8,242 statements
    the parser takes, each of which SQLite runs, with the columns named as
    the parser says.

  Broken by hand, with AND at OR's level, the random expressions failed
  where the corpus didn't: only 6 of its cases mix AND and OR, and their
  values happen to agree. The flat runs are there for that.
- **Column names:** 3,912 columns in the corpus are named as 0.x named them,
  by alias, by field, or by the column's text.
- **The fuzz ran clean:** `FuzzParse` for 12 minutes with one worker, on a
  machine two other agents were using: 6.9 million runs and 469 new
  inputs of interest, with nothing found, so `testdata/fuzz` holds nothing.
  Shorter runs while the parser was being written found nothing either;
  `TestTreesPrintBack` found the one round trip bug before any fuzzing did
  (below).
- **The planted bugs are caught,** all eleven, one at a time with
  `-failfast` as `scripts/planted.sh` runs them. The precedence bugs are
  caught by `TestPrecedence`, by the random expressions against SQLite and
  by the round trip; `query/plus-kept` by `TestPrecedence` alone, since
  SQLite reads `-(+1)` as `-1` itself, and only the tree shows it.
- **Bugs found on the way,** each now covered by a test:
  - `x IS (NOT y)` printed as `x IS NOT y`, which reads as IS NOT
    (`TestTreesPrintBack` on the corpus).
  - Printing every compound operand in parentheses made a long chain such as
    `1 + 1 + ... + 1` nest as deep as it was long, past the nesting limit, so
    the printer now puts in only the parentheses the grammar needs.
  - The printer printed the right operand of IS twice when it was a NOT, so
    printing took twice as long at each level of `x IS (NOT ...)`.
- `sh scripts/check-beta.sh`, `go vet ./...`, `go test -short ./...`, `go
  test -race` on the package, and the package's tests with `CGO_ENABLED=0`,
  which leave out `sqlite_test.go`, pass.

## Decisions settled

**The tree** (`tree.go`):

- A statement is a `*Select`, `*Insert`, `*Update` or `*Delete`, and
  `Params()` gives its count of `?` marks. An expression is a `*Literal`,
  `*Param`, `*Column`, `*Call`, `*Cast`, `*Unary`, `*Binary`, `*Between`,
  `*Like`, `*In` or `*Record`.
- Parentheses leave no node, as in SQLite, whose parser drops them. So
  `(x)` is `x`, and the rules SQL.md gives "with or without parentheses"
  need no unwrapping: a sign before a literal, `ORDER BY (1)`, and a result
  column that is a field.
- A sign in front of a plus sign's expression takes its place as the tree is
  built, as SQLite's parser does: `-+x` and `-(+x)` give the tree of `-x`,
  and `+(+x)` that of `+x`. A minus sign is never part of a number: `-1` is
  a `*Unary` over the literal `1`.
- A literal keeps its text exactly as written, and `Data` holds what text
  and bytes literals stand for. Numbers are left for Q1 to read.
- `=` and `==` are one operator, `OpEq`, and so are `!=` and `<>`, `OpNe`.
  `x IS NULL` is `OpIs` with a NULL literal.
- A name is an `Ident`: its text without quotes, whether it was quoted, and
  `Folded()`, with ASCII letters in lower case, as names match.
- Every node has `At`, the byte where it starts, and `*Binary` has `OpAt`
  for its operator. `Param.Index` numbers the marks from 0 in the order of
  the text.
- A result column keeps `Text`, by SQL.md's rule: from its first character
  to just before the next token, comments kept, trimmed of SQLite's spaces.
- `Select.Aggregate` says whether the result columns use an aggregate, and
  `Call.Aggregate()` whether a call is one.

**What the parser checks, and what it leaves.** It checks everything that
needs no database: the grammar, the function names and their numbers of
arguments, `*` only in `count(*)`, `date()` and `datetime()` on text literals
only, where fields and aggregates may go, a field beside an aggregate in the
result columns, each field once in an INSERT or UPDATE, `key` in every
INSERT, the counts of values, `*` over a join or json_each, the join of one
walk and one table, and SQLite's limits. It leaves to later stages anything
that needs the tables: whether tables and fields exist, what each name
finds, the sides of a join's ON, ORDER BY's three kinds of term, fields in
an aggregate query's ORDER BY, the values of LIMIT and OFFSET, and what
`date()`'s literals say.

**Refusals.** An `*Error` has the byte where the trouble starts and a
message, and it's of kind "error". Syntax errors and the errors SQLite
gives for functions keep SQLite's messages, such as `near "SELEC": syntax
error`, `incomplete input` and `wrong number of arguments to function
abs()`. Everything outside the subset reads "X is outside the SQL subset",
with SQL.md's reason or the way the subset has where there is one: "LIMIT m,
n is outside the SQL subset: write LIMIT n OFFSET m". The checks beside the
grammar run once the whole statement has parsed, as SQLite's do, so a syntax
error anywhere comes first.

**Names where SQLite reads keywords.** The reserved words are SQL.md's. The
keyword test against SQLite found the other words SQLite reads as keywords
in some of the places the Beta takes a bare name, and the parser refuses
each there:

- a bare alias, of a result column or a source, can't be CROSS, FULL,
  INNER, LEFT, NATURAL, OUTER, RIGHT or INDEXED;
- right after an operand, LIKE is the operator, and GLOB, REGEXP and MATCH
  are refused as SQLite's operators;
- `with` can't be a bare name in an expression, since SQLite reads WITH
  after a parenthesis as the start of a subquery, and a tree with one in it
  couldn't always be printed back;
- TRUE and FALSE can't be bare names in an expression, and WINDOW with a
  name and AS after it starts SQLite's WINDOW clause.

**What 0.x's SQLite does where SQL.md is silent,** settled the same way, and
checked against the corpus and SQLite:

- The text ends at its first NUL byte, since SQLite reads it as a C string.
  `ParseCondition` refuses a NUL, since 0.x puts the condition in
  parentheses at the end of its own query, and a NUL cuts off the closing
  one: "incomplete input".
- A vertical tab can't start a run of spaces, and can be in one after its
  first character. A UTF-8 byte order mark at the start of a token is a
  space. A `/*` that ends the text is a slash and a star.
- `count()`, which SQLite takes for `count(*)`, and `date()` and
  `datetime()` without arguments, which SQLite takes as `'now'`, are outside
  the subset.
- SQLite's limits hold: 2,000 result columns, ORDER BY terms and SET fields,
  1,000 arguments to a function, 32,766 `?` marks, and expressions at most
  1,000 tall, as SQLite counts them, adding up through subqueries. Where
  SQLite turns a node into two, such as NOT LIKE, the parser counts two, so
  a tree it takes is never one SQLite refuses.
- Expressions nested more than 250 deep are refused. SQLite's parser stack
  holds 2,500 entries, and in every shape measured a level of nesting took
  at most five of them and a subquery about ten, so a subquery counts as ten
  levels. The limit keeps a statement the Beta takes inside SQLite's, and a
  hostile one from running the stack out.

**Printing** (`print.go`). `String` puts in only the parentheses the grammar
needs, from the levels and from what follows each operand, since a NOT runs
on through level 4. So the SQL never nests deeper than the text the tree
came from. A strict printer, unexported, puts every operator's operands in
parentheses, for the tests against SQLite.

**The tests against SQLite** are in a file of their own with the build tag
`cgo`, since they reach 0.x and go-sqlite3. The rest of the package's tests
build without cgo, like the package.

## Notes for later tasks

- **Q1, values and functions:** take the tree as SQLite's, already built:
  - the sign rule is applied, so a `*Unary` with `OpNeg` over a `*Literal`
    of kind `LitInt` or `LitReal` is "a minus sign in front of a number
    literal", which negates the literal as written; parentheses between
    them have gone already;
  - a `*Unary` with `OpPlus` stays in the tree, since a plus sign takes
    away affinity, and a `*Cast`'s `Type` is in upper case;
  - the AND and OR rule for integer literals that fit in 32 bits takes a
    bare literal only, with no sign in front, as Q1 found against 0.x:
    `-0 AND x` works out x;
  - `Call.Func()` is the function's name in lower case, and the parser has
    checked it and its count of arguments against SQL.md;
  - the expression cases are `SELECT <expression> AS v`, so a test takes
    `Results[0].Expr` and fills `Param.Index` from the case's arguments.
- **Q2, dates:** `date()` and `datetime()` reach Q2 with text literals only,
  in `Literal.Data`. Q2 refuses a first argument other than `'now'`, in any
  case, and a modifier outside SQL.md's pattern, before anything runs, with
  a message such as "date() on '2026-10-07' is outside the SQL subset". The
  fixed-date cases parse, so Q2's test can take their calls from the tree.
- **Q4 and Q5, operators and planner:** names are all theirs to find:
  tables, fields, `rowid`, `oid` and `_rowid_`, json_each's columns other
  than `value`, a double-quoted name that isn't a field, and an ambiguous
  name in a join. So are ORDER BY's three kinds of term, a field in an
  aggregate query's ORDER BY that isn't an alias, a column number out of
  range, and the sides of a join's ON. A result column's name is its alias,
  then the field's name as the table spells it when its `Expr` is a
  `*Column`, then its `Text`. `Source.Name()` gives the name a source is
  known by.
- **Q6, writes:** an INSERT names each field once, `key` among them, and
  each row has one value for each field, with no fields in the values. An
  UPDATE sets each field once. Whether the fields exist, and a key that
  changes, are Q6's.
- **G3 and G4, the driver and slice 3:** `Params()` is the driver's
  `NumInput`, and the check for fewer arguments or more is the driver's.
  `ParseCondition` is `Nearest`'s filter: a nil `Expr` for one that's only
  white space, and its own count of marks. Every `*Error` is of kind
  "error".
- **G5, the command:** it keeps its own check for one statement, with exit
  code 2, as P5.md says; `Parse` refuses a second statement too.
- **R1, long runs:** `go test -fuzz ^FuzzParse$ ./beta/internal/query`. The
  tests against SQLite take about two seconds; `-short` runs 4,000 random
  expressions in place of 20,000.
