# A4: SQL corpus

Status: next. Planned at 3 hours. It needs no other task.

## The contract

From BETA.md: statements inside and outside the subset, a few thousand
generated expressions and date cases, with answers recorded through 0.x.
Done when it replays clean on 0.x twice.

## What it's for

- P5 marks every statement in the corpus as in or out of the subset.
- Q1 must match the corpus's expression answers exactly.
- Q2 must match its date cases, month ends included.
- Q3 must parse every statement marked in, and refuse every one marked out.
- Q4's aggregates must match the corpus's answers.

## Where it goes

Into `beta/sqlcorpus`, Linux only, with an untagged `doc.go`. The corpus
itself goes in `beta/sqlcorpus/testdata`, as JSON lines. A test records the
answers through 0.x, and replays them and compares.

## To settle, and write down in this file

- **The data:** a small fixed database built by code through the
  conformance API, so the Beta can build the same one. Tables `docs`,
  `people` and `t_1`, with fields of every type, vectors and links, so the
  statements have something to find.
- **Statements,** a few hundred, written by hand and from templates. Each
  carries a first guess, in or out, for P5 to settle:
  - in: everything in BETA.md's "Queries and SQL": expressions and aliases,
    `WHERE`, `ORDER BY`, `LIMIT`, `OFFSET` and aggregates over the whole
    result. Also the walk forms, including `json_each(walk(...))` and
    `IN (SELECT value FROM json_each(walk(...)))`, the one-record
    subquery, `distance` and `vector`, `IN` lists, `BETWEEN`, `LIKE` and
    `IS NULL`, and single-table `INSERT`, `UPDATE` and `DELETE`;
  - out: what BETA.md leaves for later, such as `GROUP BY`, `HAVING`,
    other joins and subqueries, `CASE`, `WITH`, window functions, and
    `date` on a stored date.
- **Generated expressions,** a few thousand: random trees over literals of
  every type, run as `SELECT <expression>` through 0.x.
  - The literals: integers, reals with -0 and the extremes, text with
    Unicode, blobs, and NULL.
  - The operators: arithmetic and `%`, comparisons, `AND`, `OR`, `NOT`,
    `||`, `IS`, `IN`, `BETWEEN` and `LIKE`.
  - The functions are a candidate list for P5: `abs`, `length`, `lower`,
    `upper`, `substr`, `trim`, `round`, `coalesce`, `ifnull`, `typeof`,
    `instr`, `replace`, and the two-argument `min` and `max`.
  - Each answer keeps its type and its exact bits.
- **Dates:** `'now'` moves, so the cases start from fixed dates, such as
  `date('2026-01-31', '+1 month')`, which SQLite answers itself. Q2's
  closing test runs the Beta's date arithmetic on the same starting dates. A
  few statements on `'now'` are compared against SQLite at the moment they
  run.
- **The format:** one JSON object a line, with the statement, its arguments,
  the in-or-out guess, and the answer. The answer is the columns and the
  rows, or an error's kind. Values use the tagged form from
  `beta/difftest` (`difftest.Value`), so floats keep their bits.
- **Recording and replaying:** an environment variable, say
  `HYPERCRUX_CORPUS_RECORD=1`, writes the answers. Without it, the test
  replays them and compares. "Replays clean twice" means recording once,
  then two replays with no differences.
- **Error kinds** as in A3: ok, not found, invalid or other.

## Notes from earlier tasks

- A1.md: P5 also settles the scalar `walk()` form, which 0.x's tests use.
