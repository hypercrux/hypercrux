# V1: Vector arrays and Nearest

Status: done, 8 October 2026.

## The contract

From BETA.md: vector arrays and `Nearest`: arrays without pointers, float64
norms, free slots, the size set by the first vector and reset by `Drop`,
top k with ties broken by key, a filter hook. Done when the model test
passes and results match a brute-force search.

## What was built

- **`beta/internal/store`**, in the new `vectors.go`, with changes to
  `store.go`, `write.go`, `tx.go`, `snapshot.go` and `read.go`:
  - `vectors`, each table's vector array: the values in blocks of float32s,
    each slot's norm in a `[]float64`, each slot's record in a `[]*record`,
    with nil for a free slot, and the free list.
  - A record holds its slot's number, `slot`, where it held a vector of its
    own, and `Record.Vec` is the slot itself.
  - The writes: `setVector`, which a put calls for the vector field, and the
    small functions under it, `takeSlot`, `fillSlot` and `freeSlot`, each
    with its undo entry first, as `changing` requires. A delete frees its
    record's slot, and `Commit` puts the slots a transaction freed on their
    free lists (`release`, in `tx.go`).
  - Four undo kinds: `undoNewSlot`, `undoTookSlot`, `undoFreedSlot` and
    `undoVector`. `undoRecord` keeps a record's slot where it kept its
    vector, and `undoSize` empties the array when it takes a table's size
    back to 0.
  - `checkVector`, the check of a put's vector, which decodes it into a
    buffer that every put uses again and checks it with the sum its norm
    needs anyway.
  - `Nearest`, in place of S1's stub, and `MaxK`, 10,000. `Tx.Nearest`
    calls it, so a search through a transaction sees the transaction's
    changes.
  - The snapshot copies each vector out of its slot with `value.Vector`.
  - Eight planted bugs, in `plant_on.go` and `beta/plants.txt`:
    `store/freed-slot-taken-at-once`, `store/overwrite-not-kept`,
    `store/norm-not-undone`, `store/deleted-vector-found`,
    `store/ties-by-slot`, `store/filter-after-distance`,
    `store/top-k-first-seen` and `store/nearest-table-first`.
- **Tests,** in the new `vectors_test.go`, inside the package, and
  `nearest_test.go`, outside it, with searches added to the tests of S1 to
  S5:
  - The model test: `TestTheModel` and `TestTransactions` with searches,

  - Brute force: `TestNearestAgainstBruteForce`, on random and awkward

  - 0.x's cases side by side: `TestNearestIsExact`,
    `TestNearestChecksIn0xsOrder`, `TestNearestTiesComeByKey`,
    `TestNearestInsideATransaction` and `TestNearestAfterDeletesAndDrops`,
    each on 0.x and on the store, and searches among the random steps of
    `TestTheStoreAgrees0x`.
  - S3's agreement test: `sameStores` in `TestChangeLists` compares a search

  - The arrays: `TestBlockShifts`, `TestTheArrayGrows`, `TestSlotsComeBack`,

## The result

- **The model test passes with vectors, through transactions and across
  rollbacks.**
  - `TestTheModel` runs S1's 20 seeds of 4,000 steps, or 4 of 2,000 with
    `-short`, with a search beside one step in seven. The model searches
    plainly: every record of the table with a vector, the ones the filter
    lets through, each distance from vecmath's `Dot`, `Norm` and `Distance`,
    one sort by distance and then key, and the first k. The store must give
    the same hits, bit for bit and in the same order, the same kind of
    error, nil where the model gives nil, and the same number of calls to
    the filter.
  - The searches' queries are random, or a vector a record of the table
    holds, and now and then a query, a table or a k that breaks the rules, a
    table that isn't there, or a query of another size. The filters let
    every record through, none, the keys that end in an odd byte, or the
    records whose field n holds a whole number, or fail at their first call.
    Each Record a filter is handed must be the model's record, with a
    vector of the table's size. One put in six that gives a vector gives one
    another record of the table holds already, so searches meet ties.
  - In seed 1, 314 searches work: 174 find hits, 40 of those with ties, and
    76 are of a table with no vector size. 144 break the rules, 72 are of a
    table that isn't there, and the filter stops 40.
  - `compareAll` searches every vector of every table against the model, so
    every comparison with the model in S1's to S5's tests checks the arrays
    too, after each transaction and each statement taken back.
  - `TestTransactions` searches through the transaction at the end of half
    the statements, and after half the statements taken back. In seed 1,
    444 searches work, 188 with hits and 35 with ties. The two readers
    alongside search every table with a vector size in every fourth read,
    and must find each vector that a scan of the table gives, as a search
    written plainly finds it. They made 2,910 and 3,446 reads.
  - `checkVectors` checks each array's shift and blocks, that a slot in use
    holds a vector that keeps the rules, with the norm vecmath gives it, bit
    for bit, and a record of the table that names the slot, and that a free
    slot is on the free list once, or else freed by the open transaction
    and waiting for its commit, and that nothing is kept past the last slot.
  - Seed 1's counts of links, unlinks, reads of links, walks, deletes and
    drops in `TestTheModel`, `TestTransactions` and `TestChangeLists` are the
    ones S5.md records, so the earlier steps run as they did.
- **Results match a brute-force search.** `TestNearestAgainstBruteForce`
  runs 25 rounds, or 2 with `-short`, of 12 sizes from 1 to 32,768 values,
  the last in several blocks of 8. Each round puts up to 64 vectors of a
  size, takes a sixth away by deletes and a sixth by puts of null, and puts
  more, which take the free slots again. The vectors are random, near
  float32's smallest values, near its largest, both at once, one ulp from
  the vector before, the same as it, opposite it, or one big value among
  small ones. The queries are random, a stored vector or its opposite, tiny
  or huge, with k from 1 to past the records there are, and a third with a
  filter on the key. 2,400 searches must each match a search written
  plainly with vecmath bit for bit, and each hit's distance must agree
  within 1e-9 with the vector's distance in plain float64 arithmetic,
  adding in order. They met 6,643 ties between neighbouring hits.
- **0.x's Nearest cases pass, on 0.x and on the store side by side.**
  - `TestNearestIsExact` is 0.x's TestNearestIsExact and the suite's: 2,000
    vectors of 32 values, 20 searches with no filter and 20 with one that
    lets a third through, `"group" = ?` in 0.x and a Filter in the store, and
    the errors those tests check.
  - `TestNearestChecksIn0xsOrder` has every check failing, in 0.x's order:
    the table's name, the query, k, the table, then the query's size, each
    with 0.x's message, and nil for a table with no vector size, an empty
    list for one whose vectors have all gone, and a k past the records
    there are.
  - `TestNearestTiesComeByKey` puts one vector into records in an order
    other than their keys', so their slots come in another order, with its
    double, its opposite and vectors across it. Both engines give the keys
    in the same order.
  - `TestNearestInsideATransaction`, from 0.x's TestUpdateIsAllOrNothing,
    searches inside 0.x's `Update` and through a `Tx` after the same
    changes, and after the rollback.
  - `TestNearestAfterDeletesAndDrops`, from TestTableNamesLikeHyperCruxsOwn
    and TestDropAndAdoptAfterSchemaChanges: tables with 0.x's own words for
    names, and a table dropped and made again with another size.
  - Both engines must give the same error with the same message, or hits
    that agree within `conformance.DistanceBound`, in the same order but
    where two distances fall within the bound, and nil for nil.
    `TestTheStoreAgrees0x` makes a search beside one step in six, with
    choices of its own, and a search of every vector of every table every
    100 steps. 339 searches work, 251 of them with hits, 63 of those through
    a filter on the key, which is `key < ?` in 0.x. 130 break the rules and
    43 are of a table that isn't there.
- **S3's agreement test with vectors.** `TestChangeLists` already put
  vectors through the codec, `ApplyBatch`, `LoadBatch` and the snapshot.
  Now `sameStores` also compares a search of every table, with queries of

- **Timings,** from `TestNearestTimings` with `-v`, over three runs on this
  two-core machine, with two other agents at work on it. Each search is the
  middle of five:

| | Time | Target |
|---|---|---|
| 10 nearest among 100,000 vectors of 384 values | 33 to 39 ms | 65 ms |
| The same, a tenth passing a filter on a field | 7 to 9 ms | 12 ms |
| 10 nearest among 10,000 vectors of 1,536 values | 13 to 15 ms | 26 ms |
| `LoadBatch` of 100,000 records with a field and a 384-value vector, in batches of a megabyte | 306 to 379 ms | 250 ms for the whole open |

A record with a whole number and a vector of 384 values takes 1,796 bytes
in all, from the heap's size before and after, and the array 23.8 bytes a
vector beside its values. These are the store's own times: the public

- **Opening.** In a quick check with every batch built before the timing
  began, the same 100,000 records load in 262 to 324 ms, and in 60 to 66
  ms without their vectors, so the vectors cost about 200 to 260 ms. On
  this machine, touching 154 MB of memory fresh from the system takes 65 to
  83 ms on its own, which any layout pays. In a profile, decoding the
  vectors' bits took about 80 ms when they weren't in the processor's
  cache, where 384 values take 155 ns when they are, their norms about 30
  ms and copying them into their slots about 20. The rest is the collector
  and the put path.
- **Bugs planted by hand,** one at a time: 24 in the new code. 22 made the
  tests fail at once. The two that got through led to new checks: a search
  that skipped a table's last block, which no test searched in full, since
  the brute-force test's vectors fitted in one block, so it took vectors of
  32,768 values and `TestNearestTimings` a search of every vector; and a
  rollback of a new slot that left the record's pointer past the end of the
  slice, which `checkVectors` now looks for. `store/filter-after-distance`
  first got past `TestNearestFilter`, whose k was past the records there
  are, so it searches for 3 too. The store's 39 planted bugs, switched on
  one at a time as `scripts/planted.sh` does, are all caught, in 62 seconds
  with `-failfast`: each new one in 3 seconds or less.
- `go vet ./...`, `go test -short ./...`, `go test -race` on `store`, and
  `sh scripts/check-beta.sh` pass. The store's tests take 11 seconds, 56
  with `-race`, and 3.4 and 15 with `-short`.

## Decisions settled

**The arrays.**

- A table's vectors are in blocks of float32s, each slot's values back to
  back and the slots one after another, so a search streams through each
  block in order and the collector never looks inside them. Slot i is in
  block i >> shift, at value (i & (1<<shift - 1)) * size.
- A full block holds the most slots, as a power of two, whose values fit
  in a megabyte: 512 of 384 values, 128 of 1,536, 4 of 65,536. The first
  block starts with room for one slot and doubles until it's full; every
  block after it is made full when the one before fills.
- BETA.md has one block. The blocks are that block cut into pieces of a
  fixed size, so the array grows without copying itself: in a quick check,
  one block grown by append took 1.1 seconds to take 100,000 vectors of 384
  values and reached 620 MB of memory for their 154 MB, and blocks of 512
  slots took 80 ms and 153 MB. A search of 100,000 vectors goes through 196
  blocks.
- Beside each slot's values: its norm, a float64, and its record, a
  pointer, which is nil for a free slot. That nil is the mark, so the one
  read a search makes for each slot tells it whether the slot is free and
  which record to hand the filter.
- The blocks never shrink, and nothing moves vectors to close gaps. A slot
  a rollback takes back leaves its room for the next vector, a free slot is
  taken again by the next record that needs one, and a reload after a
  compaction builds the arrays afresh. The array goes with its table.
- Memory besides the values: 16 bytes a vector for its norm and its record's
  pointer, plus the room those slices keep for growing and the room left in
  each table's last block, 23.8 bytes a vector in all at 100,000. A record
  holds its slot's number in 8 bytes, where it held a slice of 24, so every
  record takes 104 bytes, in Go's 112-byte size, as S5's note expected.

**The writes.**

- A put that gives a record its first vector takes the free slot freed
  last, or else a new slot after the last. A put that changes a vector
  writes over the old values in the slot, and inside a transaction its undo
  entry keeps a copy of the old values and the old norm. A put of null and
  a delete free the slot and leave its values. A drop changes no slot: the
  array goes with the table, so a table made again with the same name
  starts with size 0 and an empty array, as S1's note asked.
- A slot freed outside a transaction joins the free list at once. Inside
  one it joins at the commit, from the transaction's undo entries, so no
  vector the transaction puts can take it, and undoing the free gives it
  back to its record with nothing to copy, as S2's note asked.
- The table's first vector, or a CreateTable with a size, sets the size
  and the array's shift together. Undoing it empties the array, after
  every slot taken since has been undone, so the next first vector can have
  another size.
- The norm is worked out at the write, the square root of the vector's dot
  product with itself, by vecmath, and kept beside the values. A read never
  works one out.
- Every change to an array goes through `changing` first, so the copy's
  lock is held and the undo list has its entry:

| Kind | Made before | Holds | Put back by |
|---|---|---|---|
| `undoNewSlot` | a slot made after the last | the table and the number of slots it had | cutting the norms and records back, and clearing the records past the end |
| `undoTookSlot` | a free slot taken | the table and the slot | marking the slot free, and putting it back on the free list |
| `undoFreedSlot` | a slot freed | the table, the slot and its record | giving the slot back to its record |
| `undoVector` | a vector written over | the table, the slot, a copy of its values and its norm | copying them back |

- `undoRecord` keeps the record's slot, and `undoSize` empties the array
  when it puts a size of 0 back. An undo entry is 120 bytes, from 112.

**Checking a vector.** A put's vector is decoded into the store's buffer,
which every put uses again, and its dot product with itself is the whole
check: the sum is finite only when every value is, since 65,536 of
float32's largest values squared come to about 7.6e81, and above zero once
one value isn't zero, since float32's smallest squared is 2^-298. The norm
needs that sum anyway, so a vector that keeps the rules costs a decode and
a dot product, and `rules.Vector`, which took 712 ns for 384 values, runs
only to give 0.x's message for one that doesn't. `TestTheVectorCheck` holds
the check to `rules.Vector`'s on 20,000 vectors of random bits and awkward
values. The values reach their slot with one copy, and no vector costs an
allocation of its own.

**Opening.** `LoadBatch` and `ApplyBatch` go through the same put as the
API, so vectors go into the arrays with their norms as they load. The
store decodes with its own loop, which took 155 ns for 384 values in the
processor's cache, where `value.AppendVector` took 470. Copying the bits as
they are through package unsafe took 30 ns, since amd64 and arm64 hold a
float32 in those same 4 bytes, and would save about 12 ms an open of
100,000 vectors whose bits are in the cache, and more when they aren't. The
loop needs no rule about the processor, so it stays until I1 shows the open
target needs the time.

**Nearest.**

- The checks are 0.x's, in its order, with its messages: the table's name,
  the query, k from 1 to 10,000, the table, then the query's size. A table
  with no vector size gives nil and no error. Otherwise the hits are a list
  of their own, which is empty when nothing gets through, and isn't nil, as
  0.x gives it.
- The search widens the query once, works out its norm once, and goes
  through the blocks in order. It skips each free slot, calls the filter
  for the record, and works out the distance for each record the filter
  lets through, with `Wide.Dot` and `Distance`, so the bits are the same on
  amd64 and arm64. The closest k so far are in a heap with the furthest on
  top, so a vector further than that costs one comparison. A sort by
  distance and then key gives the order.
- Ties go by key. Distances are never NaN, since every vector and query is
  finite with a norm above zero, and keys in a table differ.
- The filter is called once for each record with a vector, in the order of
  their slots, before its distance, with the Record `Get` gives, its Vec
  the slot. An error from it ends the search, and comes back as it is.
- Through a transaction the search sees the transaction's changes, since
  they're in the copy, and slots it freed are free to it.

**Record.Vec is the slot,** with its capacity cut at its length. A later
put of another vector writes over it, which Reader allows, since what a
read hands out is good until the read ends, or inside a transaction until
its next change. S1's `TestReadsShareNothingThatChanges` had pinned more,
from when a put replaced a record's vector slice whole; it now checks that
the vector is the slot and that appending to it never reaches the next
slot. `read.go`'s comment on `Vec` says so.

**The planted bugs:** `store/freed-slot-taken-at-once`, a slot freed in a
transaction going on the free list at once; `store/overwrite-not-kept`, a
vector written over with no copy of the old values for the undo;
`store/norm-not-undone`, the undo putting the values back and leaving the
new norm; `store/deleted-vector-found`, a delete leaving its slot in use;
`store/ties-by-slot`, ties broken by slot; `store/filter-after-distance`,
the filter called only for records near enough to join the closest k;
`store/top-k-first-seen`, a search keeping the first k vectors it meets;
and `store/nearest-table-first`, the table looked for before k is checked.

## Notes for later tasks

- **G2:** `DB.Nearest` and `Tx.Nearest` without a filter call
  `Reader.Nearest` inside `Read` or through the transaction, with
  `[]float32(q)` and a nil Filter, and give 0.x's errors as they are.
  `store.Hit` matches 0.x's `Hit` field for field. The store gives nil for
  a table with no vector size and an empty list otherwise, as 0.x does, so
  the public package converts nil to nil. `MaxK` can be `store.MaxK`. The
  hits' keys are the records' own strings, which never change, so they
  can be handed out.
- **Q5:** a WHERE clause becomes a `Filter`, called for each record with a
  vector, so `vec IS NOT NULL` holds for every record it sees. A record's
  Vec is its slot, good until the read ends, so `value.Vector(r.Vec)`
  copies it for SQL. SQL's `distance()` on a stored vector can work out the
  norm with `vecmath.Norm`; the store keeps each one in its array, if a
  later task wants a way to read it.
- **I1 and V2:** `LoadBatch` of 100,000 records with 384-value vectors took
  306 to 379 ms here, against a target of 250 ms for the whole open, and 60
  to 66 ms without the vectors. Of the vectors' 200 to 260 ms, about 70 is
  touching 154 MB of fresh memory, which a vector block read on opening
  would pay too, up to 80 decoding their bits, about 30 their norms and 20
  copying. On the way to the store, each vector's bits are copied by the
  read and again by the codec, which makes one string of each batch. So on
  this machine `LoadBatch` alone is past the target. What V1 left to try:
  decoding through package unsafe, about 12 ms when the bits are in the
  cache, and the codec handing vectors over as `[]float32`.
- **T5:** these are the store's own times. The public package adds `Read`'s
  lock and the `stat` call, and 0.1's searches start from `vectorTable`'s
  vectors, with `grp = ?` for the filtered one, as `TestNearestTimings`
  does.
- **F8 and F9:** the snapshot gives each vector as a `value.Vector` copy of
  its slot, so compaction writes vectors as ordinary fields. A store that
  loads the compacted part gets arrays with no free slots.
- **G7:** `check`'s count of vectors is the slots in use, one for each
  record with a vector. `checkVectors` in `model_test.go` is a start on the
  store's own check of the arrays.
- **R2:** an array never shrinks while a process runs. Deletes leave free
  slots that a search passes at the cost of reading a pointer, until puts
  take them again or a reload builds the array afresh.
- **The coordinator:**
  - `beta/README.md`'s line for `internal/store/` can read: "the in-memory
    copy, with its read API, `Reader`. It holds the records with their
    fields, each table's field list, each table's keys in byte order, which
    `Scan` reads through a `Cursor`, each record's links both ways, which
    `Neighbours` reads and `Walk` follows, and each table's vectors in an
    array of their own, which `Nearest` searches. Its writes give the change
    lists the log writes, `ApplyBatch` and `LoadBatch` take a batch the log
    has read whole or not at all, and `Snapshot` gives the copy as a
    compacted part. Reads share it through `Read`, and writes go through a
    transaction from `Begin`, which holds readers off from its first change
    until it ends, and puts the copy back with its undo list when it rolls
    back." `beta/plants.txt` has eight new lines for the store.
  - `value.AppendVector`'s comment in `beta/internal/value/value.go` says
    V1 uses it to put a vector into its table's array. The store decodes
    with a loop of its own instead, three times as fast, so the comment
    could lose that clause, or `AppendVector` could take the loop.
  - The store's tests now import `beta/conformance` for `CompareHits` and
    `DistanceBound`, which has no other package of the Beta's under it, so
    there's no cycle.
