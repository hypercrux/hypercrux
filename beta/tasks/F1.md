# F1: Codec

Status: done, 7 October 2026.

## The contract

From BETA.md: the codec, batches, markers and headers encoded as pure
functions. Done when the golden fixtures match byte for byte, random round
trips pass, and a 30-minute fuzz of the decoder runs clean.

## What was built

All of it is in `beta/internal/format`, beside P3's `Change`. Every function
is pure, with no file in sight.

- **`header.go`:** `Header`, `AppendHeader` and `DecodeHeader`, with
  `Version` and `HeaderSize`.
- **`batch.go`:** `AppendBatch`, `DecodeBatch`, `BatchLength` and `Batch`,
  with `BatchHeadSize`, `MinBatchSize` and `ErrDoesNotCount`.
- **`marker.go`:** `Marker`, `AppendMarker` and `DecodeMarker`, with
  `MarkerSize`.
- **`rules.go`:** FORMAT.md's rules for a change on its own, which the
  encoder and the decoder share.
- **Tests:**
  - `codec_test.go`: every fixture decoded and encoded again, the two small
    databases rewritten whole, batch by batch and marker by marker, and each
    batch's changes compared with `fixtures_test.go`'s reader. Besides
    those: 29 header cases, each of FORMAT.md's checks failing in its order;
    markers, with every bit of one changed; each of the five checks for a
    batch that counts, every truncation of a batch and every one-bit change
    to it; 70 changes that break one rule each, which `DecodeBatch` reports
    as damage and `AppendBatch` refuses in the same words; 23 kinds of
    broken syntax; 16 things only the encoder refuses; changes at the
    limits (65,535 fields, a vector of 65,536 values, a 1,024-byte key, an
    800-byte link type); and decoded changes owning their slices, apart from
    the caller's bytes.
  - `random_test.go`: random round trips; random changes that break a rule
    now and then, against `fixtures_test.go`'s reader; random bytes changed
    in random batches.
  - `fuzz_test.go`: `FuzzDecodeBatch`, `FuzzDecodeHeader` and
    `FuzzDecodeMarker`, seeded from the fixtures.
- **`fixtures_test.go`:** `readFixture` takes a `testing.TB`, so the fuzz
  targets can read the fixtures. The reader is otherwise as P2 wrote it.
- `doc.go`, and the comment on P3's `TestChangesCarryTheFixtures`, say where
  the codec is.

## The result

- **The golden fixtures match byte for byte.** All 12 decode and encode back
  to their bytes. The codec reads the same changes, lengths and checksums in
  them as `fixtures_test.go`'s reader, which uses no codec.
- **Random round trips pass.** 3,000 random change lists (300 with
  `-short`) give decode(encode(c)) == c and encode(decode(b)) == b, with
  awkward values throughout: -0, subnormals and the extremes as reals and
  vector values, the i64 extremes, empty and zero-holding text and bytes,
  text and bytes past 65,535 bytes, every length of UTF-8 sequence up to
  U+10FFFF, names and keys at their limits, generations and sequence
  numbers up to 2^64 - 1. `fixtures_test.go`'s reader reads every one the
  same.
- **The rules agree with the fixtures' reader.** In 6,000 batches of
  changes written without checks, the codec takes and refuses exactly what
  that reader does: 1,402 taken, 4,594 refused, and 4 too short to count.
  `AppendBatch` refuses exactly what `DecodeBatch` takes for damage. 20,000
  batches with random bytes changed are damage or read back to the same
  bytes when their checksum fits, and don't count when it doesn't.
- **The fuzz targets ran clean for 30 seconds each** here, with one worker:
  `FuzzDecodeBatch` at about 17,000 inputs a second, `FuzzDecodeHeader` at
  23,000 and `FuzzDecodeMarker` at 18,000.
- **The 30-minute fuzz of `FuzzDecodeBatch`:** for the coordinator, once
  the round's agents have finished:
  `go test -run '^$' -fuzz '^FuzzDecodeBatch$' -fuzztime 30m ./beta/internal/format`.
- **The tests catch broken codecs.** Of 45 bugs put into the codec one at a
  time, such as text left unchecked for UTF-8, -0 counted as a value that
  isn't zero, the length written without the checksum, or a checksum that
  fails reported as damage, the tests caught 44. The other changes nothing,
  since an empty vector is refused as all zeros anyway.
- `go vet ./...`, `go test -short ./...`, `go test -race -short
  ./beta/internal/format` and `sh scripts/check-beta.sh` pass. The package's
  full tests take about 4 seconds.

## Decisions settled

**The functions.**

| Part | Encode | Decode |
|---|---|---|
| Header | `AppendHeader(dst, Header) ([]byte, error)` | `DecodeHeader(b, size) (Header, error)` |
| Batch | `AppendBatch(dst, gen, seq, []Change) ([]byte, uint32, error)`, giving the checksum for the marker | `DecodeBatch(b, gen, seq) (Batch, error)`, and `BatchLength(head, gen, seq, room) (int64, error)` for a reader that reads the head first |
| Marker | `AppendMarker(dst, id, gen, Marker) []byte` | `DecodeMarker(b, id, gen) (Marker, bool)` |

- The encoders append, as `strconv` and `encoding/binary` do, so the log can
  use one buffer again for every commit. When one refuses, `dst` comes back
  as it was.
- `DecodeBatch` and `DecodeHeader` take the bytes from the start of what
  they read. `DecodeBatch` treats the end of `b` as the end of the file, and
  leaves the marker and what follows alone. `DecodeHeader` takes the file's
  size, for the check that the compacted part ends within the file.
- `DecodeBatch` takes the generation and the sequence number that comes
  next, so all five checks for a batch that counts are in one place.
- `DecodeMarker` reports whole or not, with no error, since a marker that
  isn't whole is never damage on its own. What a whole marker in the wrong
  place means is the log's to decide.

**The line between a batch that counts and damage.** When any of the five
checks fails, the error wraps `format.ErrDoesNotCount`, and nothing else
does. It doesn't wrap `errs.ErrDamaged`, since that's where the log ends,
and only a marker further on can show damage there. It's a sentinel of
`format`'s own, outside P3's `errs`, because it's a signal for the log and
never reaches a caller of the public package.

**Content errors wrap `errs.ErrDamaged`.** A batch that counts holds what its
writer wrote, so changes in it that aren't well formed, or that break the
rules, can't come from a crash. They come back as a `*errs.Damage` naming the
batch by its sequence number, with the offset in the batch where the bad
change starts, or where the syntax failed, and a reason such as `change 2:
the field name "KEY", which breaks the rules for field names`. The log sets
the path and adds the batch's offset in the file. A damaged header is a
`*errs.Damage` too. A file too short for a header or with the wrong magic
number gives `errs.ErrNotDatabase`, SQLite's header `errs.ErrZeroX`, and
another version `errs.ErrFormatVersion`, in FORMAT.md's order.

**What the decoder checks:** the syntax, and FORMAT.md's rules for a change
on its own. Names (tables, keys, fields and link types), with their lengths;
a vector size of at most 65,536, with a vector field when it isn't 0; no two
field names in a change that match regardless of case; a put's fields in
byte order; the vector field holding a vector or null, and no other field a
vector; reals and vector values finite; text valid UTF-8; vectors of 1 to
65,536 values that aren't all zero. What depends on the state, such as a
put going into a table that exists or a vector having its table's size, is
the store's.

**One set of rules for both directions.** The decoder reads a change's syntax
into a `Change`, then checks it with the same function `AppendBatch` uses,
so the two refuse the same changes, in the same words. The encoder also
refuses what the syntax can't write: more than 65,535 fields, text or bytes
over 4 GB, an unknown `Op`, a generation or sequence number of 0, and a batch
with no changes. And it refuses a change that sets a field of `Change` its
`Op` doesn't use, which keeps decode(encode(c)) equal to c. So `AppendBatch`
never writes a batch a reader would take for damage, even if the store lets
a bad change through: the commit fails with `errs.ErrInvalid` instead, before
anything reaches the file.

**Memory.** `DecodeBatch` checks the head and the checksum on the caller's
bytes, then copies the batch's changes into one string, so the caller can use
its buffer again. Every string in the changes is a slice of that one: keys,
names, link types, text and bytes, and each vector's bits, through
`value.VectorBits`. All the puts' fields share one array, and all the create
changes' field names another, so a batch costs a few allocations however
many changes it holds. Each change's slices have their capacity cut at their
end, so appending to one never reaches the next. Holding any string from a
batch holds the whole batch, its vectors included, so the store copies what
it keeps (see the notes for S1).

**The fixtures' reader stays independent.** `fixtures_test.go` keeps its own
reader, written from FORMAT.md alone. It's now the judge in three more
places: the round trips, the test of random changes that break rules, and
all three fuzz targets, which check that the codec takes and refuses exactly
what it does. Switching it to the decoder would have left the codec checked
against itself.

**The fuzz targets** each check the bytes as they come, then again with the
checksum made to fit, so the fuzzer gets past the checksum to the changes. On
every input, the codec has to agree with the fixtures' reader, keep the line
between a batch that doesn't count and damage, and encode what it read back
to the same bytes. `FuzzDecodeBatch` is the main one.

## Notes for later tasks

- **F2, the log:** opening calls `DecodeHeader` on the first 68 bytes with
  the file's size; an empty file is the log's case before that. Then,
  from offset 68: `DecodeBatch(data[off:], h.Gen, next)`. An error wrapping
  `ErrDoesNotCount` ends the log. A `*errs.Damage` gets its path and the
  batch's offset added and is reported. Otherwise the batch is marked when
  `DecodeMarker(data[off+bt.Length:], h.ID, h.Gen)` is whole and equals
  `Marker{bt.Seq, bt.Sum}`. A commit is `AppendBatch`, write, sync, then
  `AppendMarker` with the checksum `AppendBatch` returned. A new database's
  header is `AppendHeader(nil, Header{ID: id, Gen: 1})`.
- **F3, recovery:** "a batch that counts" is a `DecodeBatch` that doesn't
  fail with `ErrDoesNotCount`. Its damage cases arrive as `*errs.Damage`.
- **F4, damage:** looking past the end of the log can call `DecodeMarker` at
  every offset, since it checks the 4-byte magic number before the check
  value. A long tail would go faster with `bytes.Index` on the magic number,
  which `format` could export then.
- **F6, following:** `BatchLength` on a batch's first 28 bytes, with `room`
  the file's size less the batch's offset, gives where its marker starts.
  Then `DecodeMarker`, and `DecodeBatch` once the batch is read.
- **F8, compaction:** the snapshot uses a put's `Fields` slice again for the
  next put, so collecting changes for `AppendBatch` means cloning each put's
  fields. Encoding change by change would avoid that: `appendChange` and the
  framing in `AppendBatch` are the pieces to export. The header is
  `Header{ID, Gen: N+1, FromGen: N, FromSeq, FromSum, CompactedEnd}`, with
  `CompactedEnd` 68 when there are no tables. `AppendHeader` can't check the
  file's size, since the header is written last.
- **S1, the rules:** the store's rules must refuse everything `rules.go`
  refuses, or a commit fails at `AppendBatch` with `errs.ErrInvalid`. That's
  safe, since nothing reaches the file, but the error then has the codec's
  words where 0.x's are wanted. One set of checks could serve both later:
  `rules.go`'s are unexported for now (`isTableName`, `isKey`,
  `isFieldName`, `isLinkType`, `valueProblem`).
- **S1, S3 and G1, memory:** keys, names, text and bytes in a decoded change
  are slices of the batch's string. The store copies the ones it keeps with
  `strings.Clone`, or every batch stays in memory, vectors and all, for as
  long as one of its records lives.
- **S3, change lists:** `AppendBatch` holds emitted changes to `Change`'s
  comment: a put's fields in byte order, nothing set that the `Op` doesn't
  use, and at least one change in a batch.
- **G7, check:** a `*errs.Damage` from the codec names the batch and an
  offset from the batch's start.
- **CI:** the three fuzz targets join the `fuzz` job by name, at about 40
  seconds each with the build, so the job grows by about two minutes.
- **R1, long runs:** the header and marker targets can run for hours too,
  but there's little in them to find. `FuzzDecodeBatch` is the one to run
  long. A planted bug in the codec, such as text left unchecked for UTF-8,
  would be caught, as the 45 bugs above show.
- **T5 and I1:** nothing here was timed, since benchmarks weren't run on the
  shared machine. Opening 100,000 records in 250 ms counts on the decoder's
  speed: one copy of each batch, a CRC32C, and one pass over each vector's
  values for the finite and zero checks.
