# F5: Failed commits

Status: done, 8 October 2026.

## The contract

From BETA.md: failed commits: a cut back to just after the last marker, or
else the lock kept and writes refused. Done when T3 passes with a failure
at every call, and a handle stuck that way blocks other writers until it's
closed.

## What was built

All of it is in `beta/internal/logfile`, apart from one test in
`beta/hypercrux`.

- **`failed.go`,** new. The comment at its top sets out the rules.
  - `failed`, for a commit that fails in `Append`: the batch's write, the
    sync or the marker's write. It cuts the file back (`cutBack`). When
    that works, the error wraps the call's and says the batch was cut back
    out of the file. When it fails too, it sets `stuck`, and the error
    wraps the call's, the cut back's and `errs.ErrStuck`, and says the
    commit's outcome is unknown.
  - `cutBack`: the last marker written again in place, unless the log has
    no batch yet, a sync, the cut to just after the last marker, and the
    cut's sync.
  - `unfinished`, the error for a write, a sync or a cut that fails in the
    check of the end of the log, which leaves the end for the next check.
- **`log.go`:** `tail` is gone, and with it `Append`'s refusal after a
  failed commit. `Append` returns `stuck` once it's set. The comments on
  `Open`, `Lock`, `Unlock` and `Append`, and the one at the top, say what
  happens now.
- **`check.go`:** the skip in `catchUp` is gone. Step 3 of the check is
  `cutBack`, and `markLeft`'s failures go through `unfinished`.
- **`read.go`** and **`create.go`** lose `tail`, and **`doc.go`** mentions
  failed commits.
- **Five planted bugs,** in `plant_on.go` and `beta/plants.txt`:
  `logfile/failed-left-uncut`, `logfile/cut-unsynced`,
  `logfile/cut-without-marker`, `logfile/stuck-lets-go` and
  `logfile/check-cuts-what-failed`.
- **Tests:**
  - `crash_test.go`: `TestTheRealLogAfterAFailedCall`, the closing test's
    first half, now on workloads that carry on after a failed commit
    (`carryOn`), in five cases, two of them on a disk that dies.
    `newWorkload` and `againWorkload` take `carry`, `runCommits` is their
    Run, and `stateOf` gives the state of any list of commits.
  - `failed_test.go`, new: `TestAStuckHandleBlocksOtherProcesses`, the
    closing test's second half, `TestAStuckHandleKeepsTheLock`,
    `TestACutBackLastsAPowerCut` and `TestAStuckLogsBatchIsMadeToLast`.
  - `log_test.go`: `TestAFailedCommitIsCutBack`, in place of
    `TestAFailedCommitStopsAppends`.
  - `check_test.go`: `TestAFailedCheckLeavesTheEndForTheNext`, in place of
    `TestAFailedCheckIsHandledLikeAFailedCommit`.
  - `damage_test.go`: a case in `TestAReaderReadsAgainUnderTheLock` where a
    reader races a failed commit's cut, and `signal`.
  - `helpers_test.go`: `spy`, which a test's `spyFile`s share, and which
    can fail any write, sync or truncate (`failCalls`, `failFrom`), and
    `closeAtEnd`.
  - `lock_test.go`: the helper role `stick`.
  - `beta/hypercrux/file_test.go`: `TestUpdateRollsBackWhenTheCommitFails`
    grows, where G1.md's note said.

## The result

- **T3 passes with a failure at every call.** `TestTheRealLogAfterAFailedCall`
  fails each call in turn, from 24 seeds: 7,368 failures and 14,736 opens.

  | Workload | Calls a run | Failures | Opens at the last commit that succeeded | Opens at a later one |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | A new database, 8 commits | 76 | 1,824 | 3,648 | 0 |
  | A batch left without its marker, then 4 commits | 49 | 1,176 | 2,352 | 0 |
  | A torn marker and zeros, then 4 commits | 53 | 1,272 | 2,544 | 0 |
  | A new database, on a disk that dies | 76 | 1,824 | 2,880 | 768 |
  | A torn marker and zeros, on a disk that dies | 53 | 1,272 | 2,160 | 384 |

  Each workload carries on after a failed commit, and gives each commit
  after it the state it has without the failed one. With one call failing,
  every failed commit was cut back, and no open found one, before the
  power cut or after it; the test requires that. Where Setup left a batch
  without its marker, a failure in Open's check left the batch for the
  workload's second Open, which marked it. On a disk that dies,
  every call fails from the one picked on, so a failed commit's cut back
  fails too, the Log sticks, and every commit after it fails at Lock. The
  later opens come from there: the next process's check marked the stuck
  Log's batch when it counted. With `-short`, the test makes 6 commits from
  4 seeds.
- **A handle stuck that way blocks other writers until it's closed.**
  `TestAStuckHandleBlocksOtherProcesses`: a helper process commits while
  every sync fails, so the commit and its cut back fail, and its Log is
  stuck. While the helper keeps the Log open, this process's Lock waits its
  200 ms and times out, twice. Once the helper closes the Log, with its
  process still running, or once the process is killed, this process
  commits, and its check marks the helper's failed batch first.
  `TestAStuckHandleKeepsTheLock` does the same between two Logs in one
  process, in six cases: the commit's sync failing, then each of the cut
  back's four calls, and a failed batch write and a failed marker write
  each followed by a failed cut back. Append's error wraps both calls'
  errors and `errs.ErrStuck`. A second Append under the lock, and every
  Lock, give `errs.ErrStuck`, Lock at once. The other Log's Lock times out
  until Close, then its check marks the failed batch where the cut back
  stopped before the cut, and finds a whole log otherwise.
- **The cut back.** `TestAFailedCommitIsCutBack`: with the batch's write,
  the sync and the marker's write failing in turn, Append writes the last
  marker again, syncs, cuts and syncs, and only cuts and syncs in a log
  with no batch yet. The file then holds what it held before, and the next
  Append, under the same lock, goes in where the failed one was.
  `TestACutBackLastsAPowerCut`: on the fault layer's disk, from 32 seeds
  for each of the three calls, a power cut straight after Append's error
  leaves the three commits before it.
- **A failure in the check.** `TestAFailedCheckLeavesTheEndForTheNext`:
  each of the check's calls fails in turn, three while it marks a batch and
  four while it cuts half of one. Lock fails and lets go, the file is as it
  was, or cut when only the cut's sync failed, and the next Lock marks or
  cuts and then commits.
- **F3's reason on a stuck Log's batch.** `TestAStuckLogsBatchIsMadeToLast`:
  every sync fails from a commit on, and the marker before it lies across
  the edge of two sectors. Once the stuck Log is closed, the next Log's
  check marks the failed batch, writing it again with the marker before
  it, and commits after it, and a power cut leaves both, from 32 seeds.
- **A reader races a failed commit's cut,** as F4.md's note asked, in
  `TestAReaderReadsAgainUnderTheLock`. A real Log's commit fails at its
  sync while a reader opens the file, which reads the failed batch, whole
  and unmarked. By the reader's try at the lock, the writer has cut it back
  and committed two shorter batches, and the first one's marker lies
  inside what the reader read. The reader waits for the lock, reads again
  holding it, and applies both. With `logfile/damage-unconfirmed` planted,
  it reports "a whole marker at offset 176 names batch 2" instead.
- **The public package.** `TestUpdateRollsBackWhenTheCommitFails`: with the

  reads go on, a later Update fails with `ErrStuck` even once the disk

  handle is closed, the other's next Update finds the failed commit marked.
- **F3's closing test** still passes. The three workloads whose Open cuts

## Decisions settled

**The cut back** (`cutBack`), holding the lock, from the end of the log as
the Log has it:

1. The last marker is written again in place, from the bytes the Log holds,
   unless the log has no batch yet. Before the first batch comes the
   header, synced when the database was made and never written again.
2. A sync.
3. The cut to just after the last marker.
4. The cut's sync.

- The marker is written again for F3.md's reason: a failed sync can mark
  its page clean without it reaching the drive, while reads go on seeing
  it, and then no later sync writes it. It's written whichever call
  failed, so every cut back makes the same four calls.
- The sync before the cut goes past the brief. With it, a writer that stops
  partway, because a call fails or its process dies, leaves the end it was
  cutting, which the next check finds and cuts again, or the last marker on
  the drive. A cut synced once could leave the marker off the drive with
  nothing after it to show it. If the Log stuck there and was closed,
  another writer's next commit could be lost at a power cut, or leave the
  file reading as damaged, since that commit's marker names a batch past
  the end of the log. The extra sync only costs anything after a failure or
  a crash.
- Step 3 of the check is the same cut back, so it writes the last marker
  again too. That covers a writer that died after a failed sync, or stuck
  and was closed, with half a batch left after the marker.
- Once a cut back has worked, the failed batch is gone for good, which
  `TestACutBackLastsAPowerCut` pins. So for a caller, an error from Append
  means the commit isn't in the file, unless it wraps `errs.ErrStuck`. A
  power cut during the cut back can leave it, as T3's model allows, but
  then no caller is left to get the error.

**The handle that sticks.** When any call of the cut back fails, `stuck` is
set: an error that wraps `errs.ErrStuck` and the cut back's error.
Append's own error wraps the commit's error too, and says the outcome is
unknown. From then on `Append` and `Lock` return `stuck`, and `Lock` does
so without waiting for flock. `Unlock` lets go of the mutex and keeps
flock, so other Logs and processes wait, and time out. `Close` clears
`stuck` and closes the file, which lets go of flock, and so does the end of
the process. The next writer's check deals with what's left as with what a
crash leaves: the failed batch is marked when it counts, since its commit's
outcome is unknown, and cut otherwise.

**A failure in the check** leaves the end of the log for the next check, and
the lock goes, as it does for any error in `Lock`. That settles the choice
the brief left, between that and keeping the lock. Each step of the check
writes only what the file holds already, so it can be made again, and the
end it leaves always shows what's left to do:

- a failure in writing a batch again, its sync or its marker leaves the
  batch unmarked, and the next check writes it again and marks it;
- a failure in the cut back's marker, its sync or the cut leaves the end
  it was cutting, which the next check cuts again;
- a failure in the cut's sync leaves the cut made, with the last marker
  synced before it.

So a batch whose commit may have succeeded is never cut, and the Log needn't
stick. `Open` fails when its check does, as F3 had it, and the next Open or
Lock checks again.

**What went:** `tail`, the skip in `catchUp`, and `Append`'s refusal. Once a
Log's failed commit is cut back, nothing of it is left past the end of the
log, so what a check finds there is another writer's, or what a failed
check left.

**The workloads that carry on** (`carryOn`) begin each commit with the state
of the commits that succeeded before it plus its own, and after an error
let go and go on to the next. A failed Open is tried once more. With one
call failing, the closing test requires that no open finds a failed commit,
which is stricter than T3's model, since every failed commit is cut back
before the workload goes on.

## Notes for later tasks

- **F6, following:** a follower can find a failed commit's batch cut back
  and written over while it reads, as the race in
  `TestAReaderReadsAgainUnderTheLock` shows for Open: a whole marker inside
  what it took for the end of the file. `confirm` deals with it, and that
  case is the shape for a follower's test. A stuck Log's process can still
  read; whether it follows other processes' commits while it's stuck is
  F6's to settle.
- **F8, compaction:** a failed folder sync after the rename sticks the Log
  as `adopt` does, by setting `stuck`; the cut back isn't involved, since
  the commit that set the compaction off has succeeded.
- **G1 and the public package:** `Update`'s comment says a power cut could
  still leave a failed commit in the file. Since F5, that's so only when
  the error wraps `ErrStuck`. It could read: "When the commit itself fails,
  the database in memory goes back to how it was before fn, and the commit
  is cut back out of the file for good. If the file can't be put back, the
  error wraps ErrStuck and the commit's outcome is unknown, and every later
  write through db fails with an error that wraps ErrStuck until db is
  closed, while other handles and processes wait for the write lock."
- **I1:** a stuck handle in one process holds up writers in every other
  process until it's closed or its process ends, which the many-process
  suites can count on.
- **R1, long runs:** `HYPERCRUX_CRASH_SEEDS` raises the closing test's
  seeds, and `TestACutBackLastsAPowerCut` and
  `TestAStuckLogsBatchIsMadeToLast` take theirs from the numbers at their
  top.
