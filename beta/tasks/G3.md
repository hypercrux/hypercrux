# G3: The database/sql driver

Status: done, 8 October 2026.

## The contract

From BETA.md: the private database/sql driver behind `Query`, `QueryRow`,
`Exec` and `SQL()`, with the transaction carried in the context, results
copied out and `Begin` refused. Done when every value and destination type
scans as it does in 0.x, and `SQL().Begin` returns an error.

## What was built

- **`beta/hypercrux`**, the public package:
  - `driver.go`, new: the driver. A connector and connections that hold
    nothing of their own, prepared statements, rows copied out, `Exec`'s
    result, the conversion of arguments, and `engine`, the interface that
    runs a statement once the driver has it ready, with `notYetEngine` in
    its place until G4.
  - `sqlapi.go`: `Exec`, `Query` and `QueryRow` on `DB` and `Tx` go through
    the driver. `stubSQL` has gone.
  - `hypercrux.go` and `glue.go`: `DB` holds its own `*sql.DB`, made in
    `Open` and closed by `Close`, and its engine. `SQL()` gives the handle,
    and its comment says what errors come through it.
  - `doc.go` says what works now.
  - Seven planted bugs, in `plant_on.go` and `beta/plants.txt`:
    `hypercrux/blob-shared`, `hypercrux/time-layout`,
    `hypercrux/tx-dropped`, `hypercrux/row-kept`,
    `hypercrux/rows-pulled-late`, `hypercrux/write-kept` and
    `hypercrux/sql-wraps-invalid`.
- **Tests,** in `beta/hypercrux`:
  - `export_test.go`: `Engine` and `UseEngine`, the hook through which a
    test hands one DB's driver an engine of its own, which reads and writes
    the store as G4's will.
  - `sql_test.go`, new: `TestValuesScanAs0xs`, `TestArgumentsAre0xs`,
    `TestSQLBeginIsRefused`, `TestATxsStatementsRunInItsUpdate`,
    `TestAPoolOfOneHoldsUpNoStatement`, `TestSQLErrorsHave0xsKinds`,
    `TestExecGivesItsCounts`, `TestRowsAreCopiedOut` and
    `TestSQLCallsAfterTheEnd`.
  - `stubs_test.go`: `TestTheStubsSayWhatsMissing` makes the SQL stubs on an
    open database, through every way into the driver, and loses its lines
    for `SQL().Ping` and `SQL().Begin`, which work and refuse now.

## The result

- **Every value and destination type scans as it does in 0.x.**
  `TestValuesScanAs0xs` scans 114 values into 60 destinations, through
  `Rows.Scan` and `Row.Scan`: 13,680 scans on each engine. The Beta's value
  comes from the test's engine through the driver, and 0.x's from `SELECT ?
  AS v` with an argument that makes the same value.
  - The values: NULL; 26 integers at the ends of every integer type; 30
    reals with -0, subnormals, the infinities, float32's ends and past
    them; 42 texts that read as numbers or booleans or nearly do, with zero
    bytes, bytes that aren't UTF-8 and 1,000 bytes; 9 bytes values, empty
    among them; and 6 vectors, one of 384 values.
  - The destinations: every type database/sql's conversions name, the
    `sql.Null` types and `sql.Null[T]` of eight types, pointers to pointers,
    a named type of each kind, `json.RawMessage`, each package's `Vector`, a
    `Scanner`, three types database/sql refuses, a destination that isn't a
    pointer and a nil pointer.
  - Every scan gives the same on both, bit for bit, nil apart from empty,
    or the same error word for word, column name included. 5,446 gave a
    value and 8,234 were refused, and every kind of value has hundreds of
    each.
- **`SQL().Begin` returns an error,** by every way database/sql has to
  begin: `Begin`, `BeginTx` with options and without, and `Conn.BeginTx`.
  It reads "hypercrux: SQL().Begin is an unsupported operation in the Beta:
  a transaction goes through Update" and wraps `errors.ErrUnsupported`.
  Each time the connection goes back to the pool, so a pool of one carries
  on. 0.x's `Begin` starts a transaction, which the test checks too.
- **Arguments in.** `TestArgumentsAre0xs` gives both engines 94 Go values
  from SQL.md's table, at their edges. For the 80 that 0.x takes, the value
  the Beta's driver hands its engine is the value 0.x stores for it, in a
  field through UPDATE and back through `SELECT ?`, which agree with each
  other. The round trip through the Beta's driver gives 0.x's too. Among
  them: NaN of three payloads and through `sql.NullFloat64` as NULL, a
  time as go-sqlite3's text in its own zone, a zone with seconds and a
  monotonic reading among them, a nil `[]byte` as NULL and an empty one as
  empty bytes, a `Vector` as its bytes and a nil `*Vector` as NULL. The 14
  that 0.x refuses, the Beta refuses with the same message, which
  database/sql writes for both, before its engine sees them, apart from a
  decimal, which each refuses in words of its own. A named argument is
  refused, and so is a count of arguments that isn't the statement's count
  of marks, through prepared statements too.
- **The transaction in the context,** as far as G3 can go before G4 runs
  SQL. In `TestATxsStatementsRunInItsUpdate` the test's engine reads a
  field from the `store.Reader` the driver hands it and writes it in the
  `*store.Tx`. A `Tx`'s `Query` and `QueryRow` see the Update's changes,
  `Exec`'s writes commit with the Update, or go when it rolls back, and a
  reopen reads them back. A statement that fails takes back its own
  changes only, and the Update carries on. Through the database, inside
  the Update, `Query`, `QueryRow`, `Exec` and `SQL()`'s `Query` and `Exec`
  fail with `ErrInsideUpdate` as the Go API's calls do: a read after the
  first change, a write before and after. A read on another goroutine waits
  for the commit and sees it. A `Tx` once its Update has returned gives
  `ErrClosed`.
- **Errors of 0.x's kinds.** `TestSQLErrorsHave0xsKinds` sends a broken rule
  through ten ways in, on both engines, the Beta's engine giving the error
  G4's will. Through `Exec` on a database and a transaction, both give it
  wrapping `ErrInvalid`, with the same text. Through `SQL()`'s `Exec`,
  `Query`, `QueryRow` and a prepared statement, both give it wrapping
  nothing, with the same text, 0.x's there. Through `Query` and `QueryRow`
  the Beta's is invalid, as SQL.md says its kind follows the statement,
  and 0.x's a plain error. Four syntax errors give SQLite's words on both,
  of kind "error", every way.
- **Rows copied out.** In `TestRowsAreCopiedOut`, writing into the
  `sql.RawBytes` a scan gave changes neither the store's bytes nor the
  vector the engine gave. Rows the engine hands over in one buffer come out
  each as it was. The engine's rows are read to the end and closed before
  `Query` returns, so a `Put` goes ahead while rows are open, and open rows
  go on giving what the database held when the query ran, even after later
  commits and `Close`. A transaction's rows do the same once its Update has
  ended. An error from the engine's rows, or a row of the wrong width,
  gives no rows.
- **A pool of one.** `TestAPoolOfOneHoldsUpNoStatement` sets
  `SQL().SetMaxOpenConns(1)` and holds that connection with open rows. A
  `tx.Exec` for each row of a `tx.Query`, which 0.x runs on its
  transaction's connection, runs on both, and a `db.Query` while another's
  rows are open runs on the Beta.
- **The planted bugs are caught,** each by the test written for it, and
  more: `blob-shared` by `TestRowsAreCopiedOut`, which sees the store's
  bytes change; `time-layout` by `TestArgumentsAre0xs`; `tx-dropped` by
  five tests; `row-kept` and `rows-pulled-late` by `TestRowsAreCopiedOut`;
  `write-kept` by `TestATxsStatementsRunInItsUpdate`; and `sql-wraps-invalid`
  by `TestSQLErrorsHave0xsKinds`. The package's 16 planted bugs, run one at
  a time with `-failfast` as `scripts/planted.sh` runs them, are all caught,
  in 147 seconds.
- `go vet ./...`, `go test -short ./...`, `go test -race` on `beta/hypercrux`
  and `betax`, and `sh scripts/check-beta.sh` pass. The public package's
  tests take 9.5 seconds, 2.4 with `-short` and 24 with `-race`. The new
  tests take half a second of that.

## Decisions settled

**Two handles.** Each `DB` has its own `*sql.DB`, which `SQL()` gives, made
in `Open` over connections bound to the DB and closed by `Close`. The
methods go through `calls`, one handle for the package, made on first use,
whose connections are bound to no database: each statement's context
carries a `via` naming its `DB`, and its `Tx` when the call is through one.

- A program that sets `SQL().SetMaxOpenConns(1)`, as some do with 0.x,
  can't make a transaction's statements wait for each other. 0.x runs a
  transaction on its one connection, so a `tx.Exec` for each row of an
  open `tx.Query` works there. Over one pool for both, it would wait for
  ever in the Beta, since each statement takes a connection of its own.
- `QueryRow` on a closed database, or an ended transaction, still gets a
  connection, through which `failedRow` hands back `ErrClosed` in a
  `*sql.Row`, which only database/sql can make. So the SQL calls give
  `ErrClosed` before they look at the statement or its arguments, as every
  other call does.
- Neither handle limits its connections, since a connection is a small
  struct. 0.x limits its `SQL()` to 8.
- `SQL()` on a closed database gives the handle it had, closed. On a `DB`
  nothing opened it gives a handle closed from the start, so a caller that
  sets it up doesn't panic. Every call through either gives database/sql's
  "sql: database is closed", as 0.x's does after `Close`.

**A statement's way through the driver.** database/sql converts each
argument with the connection's `CheckNamedValue`. The driver parses the
text with `query.Parse`, refuses an argument with a name, takes exactly as
many arguments as `?` marks, and runs the statement where its `via` puts
it:

| Statement | Through the database | Through a transaction |
|---|---|---|
| SELECT | one read inside the copy's `Read` | the copy's transaction |
| INSERT, UPDATE, DELETE | an `Update` of its own | the copy's transaction, marked first |

- The statement is parsed and its arguments taken before it runs, since
  the driver can't tell a read from a write before it parses. So through
  the database inside an `Update`, a statement that doesn't parse gives its
  syntax error, and one that does gives `ErrInsideUpdate`.
- A write in a transaction is all or nothing (SQL.md, "Writes"). The driver
  marks the transaction before it, and when the write fails takes it back
  to the mark with `RollbackTo`, and the transaction carries on. So the
  engine needn't. Through the database a failed write fails its own
  `Update`, which rolls back.
- `Exec` of a SELECT runs it, drops its rows and gives `RowsAffected` 0.
  `Query` of a write runs it and gives no columns, nil as in 0.x, and no
  rows. `LastInsertId` returns an error that wraps `errors.ErrUnsupported`,
  since records have no row numbers.
- Too few arguments or too many: "hypercrux: the statement takes 2
  arguments and was given 1". A prepared statement's `NumInput` is
  `Params()`, so database/sql checks each call against it, in its own
  words, which are 0.x's too there: "sql: expected 1 arguments, got 2".
- A named argument, from `sql.Named`, is refused: "hypercrux: the argument
  named x has no mark to fill", since the subset has `?` marks only. 0.x
  leaves the mark it falls on NULL.

**Arguments** (`argument`): database/sql's own conversion first,
`driver.DefaultParameterConverter`, which takes a `driver.Valuer`, pointers
and named types, and refuses other types, and then go-sqlite3's binding, as
SQL.md's table says. NaN becomes NULL, and a `time.Time` becomes text in
go-sqlite3's layout, `2006-01-02 15:04:05.999999999-07:00`, in its own zone.
database/sql's conversion turns a `uint` of 2^63 or more into a negative
integer, where it refuses a `uint64` that size. 0.x does the same, and the
Beta with it, though SQL.md's table doesn't say so.

**Values out** (`out`), as go-sqlite3 gives them: nil, an `int64`, a
`float64`, a `string` for text, and a new `[]byte` for bytes and for a
vector, whose bytes are its values' bits (P3). Text shares its string,
which never changes, as `Get` does. Bytes are copied each time, since
database/sql hands a `[]byte` straight on to a `sql.RawBytes` or a
`Scanner`, where the caller may write into it. A real that is NaN, which
SQL never has, goes out as NULL.

**Rows** (`drain`): the engine gives a `query.Rows`, and the driver copies
every row's values out of the engine's buffer into one slice, then closes
the rows, all before the read ends. So the engine's operators may use one
buffer for every row and read the store as they go.

**Errors.** Through the methods, an error is as the engine gives it, so its
kind follows the statement, as SQL.md says. Through `SQL()`, an error wraps
neither `ErrInvalid` nor `ErrNotFound`, so every one is of kind "error", as
in 0.x, where that handle gives go-sqlite3's errors as they are, even in an
`Exec` (`plain`). A rule broken by a write reads as 0.x's does there:
"hypercrux: keys in table docs are ...", without the "invalid: " that
`Exec`'s error has. The Beta's own errors, such as `ErrInsideUpdate`,
`ErrClosed` and a stub's `errors.ErrUnsupported`, keep their values through
`SQL()`, since each is of kind "error" already.

**The stub until G4.** A statement that parses, with the right count of
arguments, gives "hypercrux: SQL is an unsupported operation in the Beta
until task G4", from `notYetEngine`. A write through the database takes the
write lock first, and writes nothing.

**The test hook** is `UseEngine(db, Engine{Query, Write})`, in
`export_test.go`, which sets one database's engine, so no test can touch
another's.

**The planted bugs:** `hypercrux/blob-shared`, bytes and vectors handed to
database/sql without a copy, so writing into a `sql.RawBytes` writes into
the store; `hypercrux/time-layout`, a time bound in RFC 3339's layout;
`hypercrux/tx-dropped`, a `Tx`'s statements run through the database;
`hypercrux/row-kept`, each row kept as the engine's own slice;
`hypercrux/rows-pulled-late`, rows read from the engine as database/sql
asks for them, after the read has ended; `hypercrux/write-kept`, a failed
write's changes kept in the transaction; and `hypercrux/sql-wraps-invalid`,
an error through `SQL()` that still wraps `ErrInvalid`.

## Notes for later tasks

- **G4:**
  - Write the engine: a type with the two methods of `engine` in
    `driver.go`, over Q5's planner and Q6's writes, in place of
    `notYetEngine{}` in `open`, in `glue.go`. The driver hands it the parsed
    statement, its arguments as values in the order of `Param.Index`, and a
    `store.Reader` for a SELECT, a read of the copy or the transaction, or
    the `*store.Tx` for a write. The column names are the engine's to give.
  - The driver reads, copies and closes every row before the read ends,
    so operators may use their buffers again and read the store as they
    go. It marks the transaction before each write and takes it back when
    the write fails, so the engine needn't.
  - The engine's errors reach the methods as they are, so each must have
    its statement's kind: "invalid" for a rule a write breaks, "error"
    otherwise. Through `SQL()` the driver takes off `ErrInvalid` and
    `ErrNotFound`.
  - Each call to the engine is one statement, so reading 'now' once a call
    reads it once a statement.
  - Take the SQL lines out of `TestTheStubsSayWhatsMissing`. The tests in
    `sql_test.go` set their own engines, so they test the driver alone and
    can stay as they are.
  - `Nearest` with a filter can take its arguments through `argument`, as a
    statement's are taken. 0.x's go through database/sql, which wraps a
    refusal as "sql: converting argument $1 type: ...".
  - Column types: the driver's rows don't implement database/sql's methods
    for them, so `ColumnTypes` gives "" for each type's name, `any` for its
    scan type, and no answer for nullable. 0.x gives "TEXT" and
    `sql.NullString` for `key`, "" and `*any` for other columns and for
    expressions, and nullable for each. The engine can say which column is
    `key`, and `rows` can add the methods.
  - The methods' handle, `calls`, keeps database/sql's default of 2 idle
    connections. If timing SQL shows connections opened and dropped under
    many goroutines, `SetMaxIdleConns` on it costs nothing.
