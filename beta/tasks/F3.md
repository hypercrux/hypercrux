# F3: Crash recovery

Status: done, 8 October 2026.

## The contract

From BETA.md: crash recovery: the tail check, a complete batch rewritten,
synced and marked, a torn marker rewritten, a cut from the first bad batch.
Done when T3 passes with a cut at every write.

## What was built

All of it is in `beta/internal/logfile`.

- **`check.go`**, new: the check of the end of the log, FORMAT.md's steps 2
  and 3, with the place for step 1 left to F4.
  - `catchUp` reads on to the end of the log, holding the write lock, and
    then checks the end (`checkEnd`). `lockFile` calls it where F2 set
    `tail`.
  - `markLeft` is step 2. When the first batch after the end counts, and no
    whole marker naming another batch follows it, it writes the batch again
    in place with the marker before it, syncs, writes the batch's marker,
    and hands the batch to the Target.
  - `checkEnd` then cuts the file back to just after the last marker and
    syncs the cut. In a compacted file whose log ends before the compacted
    part does, it reports damage and cuts nothing.
  - `tryCheck` is Open's try at the lock without waiting. When it gets
    flock, it makes Lock's checks (the inode, then that what was read is
    still in the file), reads on, checks the end, and lets go. It's the
    call F6's reader makes when it finds the writer gone.
- **`log.go`:** Open calls `tryCheck` at the end of its loop. `Options.ID`
  sets the database ID of a database the Log creates. `failed` is the one
  place for a failed write or sync, in a commit or in the check, and keeps
  F2's handling. The comment at the top says where F4, F5 and F6 go now.
- **`read.go`:** `placed` and `refused`. A `*errs.Damage` from the Target is
  now the log's error, with the path set and the batch's offset added, as
  the codec's is.
- **`create.go`:** `newFile` takes `Options.ID` when it's set.
- **Three planted bugs,** in `plant_on.go` and `beta/plants.txt`:
  `logfile/cut-what-counts`, `logfile/sync-without-rewrite` and
  `logfile/no-marker-again`.
- **Tests:**
  - `check_test.go`, new. `endCases` is 23 ends of a log, after FORMAT.md's
    table. `TestReadingStopsAtTheEndOfTheLog` reads them, and
    `TestTheCheckOfTheEndOfTheLog` checks them through Open and through
    Lock. Beside those: a helper process that dies mid-commit, the Target's
    damage, a failed check, commits landing between Open's read and its
    try at the lock, and a compacted part.
  - `crash_test.go`, new: T3's driver on the real log. The closing test,
    `TestTheRealLogPassesACutAtEveryWrite`; `TestTheRealLogAfterAFailedCall`;
    `TestCopiesOfTheRealLog`, moved here from
    `beta/internal/crash/real_test.go`, which is gone; and
    `TestACrashPointReplays`.
  - F2's tests that changed. `TestReadingStopsAtTheEndOfTheLog` holds the
    lock through another open file, so Open reads without checking.
    `TestAFailedCommitStopsAppends` ends with another Log marking the failed
    batch, and the first appending again. `TestDamageInABatchThatCounts`
    has a Lock half. `TestUnlockLetsGoOfFlockFirst` leaves out the unlock at
    the end of Open's check.
  - The helper processes in `lock_test.go` have a role `die`.

## The result

- **T3 passes with a cut at every write.**
  `TestTheRealLogPassesACutAtEveryWrite` runs the driver with
  `Kinds: crash.Cuts` and the default calls to cut in, on six workloads:
  4,352 cuts and 8,704 opens. Every open found the last commit that
  succeeded or one begun after it, and both came up in every workload.

  | Workload | Calls a run | Cut points | Seeds | Cuts | Opens at the last commit that succeeded | Opens at a later one |
  |---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
  | A new database, 8 commits | 76 | 21 | 128 | 2,688 | 2,998 | 2,378 |
  | Opened again after 4 commits, then 4 more | 43 | 9 | 32 | 288 | 284 | 292 |
  | A batch left without its marker | 48 | 11 | 32 | 352 | 414 | 290 |
  | A torn marker, then zeros | 50 | 12 | 32 | 384 | 460 | 308 |
  | A batch cut short | 47 | 10 | 32 | 320 | 344 | 296 |
  | 1,000 zeros | 47 | 10 | 32 | 320 | 352 | 288 |

  In the last four, Setup leaves the end of the log as a writer that died
  there would, synced, and the workload's own Open checks it, so the cuts
  come in the check's two writes and in its cut as well. With `-short` the
  test makes 6 commits, from 8 seeds and 4.
- **A failure at every call.** `TestTheRealLogAfterAFailedCall` fails each
  call in turn, in workloads that stop at their first error: 1,824 failures
  on a new database and 1,200 after a torn marker, from 24 seeds. Once the
  workload's process had ended, the database opened at the last commit that
  succeeded or the one that failed, and after a cut there or later. Among
  those points is the case FORMAT.md writes a batch again for: a failed
  sync, then an open that finds the batch whole in the page cache, then a
  cut.
- **Copies.** 180 copies of the real log taken mid-commit, read whole or 100
  bytes at a time, from 2 seeds, opened at allowed states. 32 copy points
  came before the file existed.
- **FORMAT.md's table.** Of the 23 ends in `endCases`, Open and Lock alike:
  - leave a whole log, and a header alone, as they are;
  - cut off a few bytes, zeros, a batch's head, a batch cut short and a
    batch that fails its checksum;
  - write again and mark a batch that counts when what follows it is
    nothing, a torn marker, a marker one byte short, a marker with a bit
    changed, a marker from another database, a torn marker and zeros, the
    start of another batch, or a second batch that counts, and the first
    batch of a log with nothing after it, then cut off whatever followed;
  - report damage, and change nothing, for a batch that counts followed by a
    whole marker naming another batch: the next batch's checksum, the batch

  - cut, until F4, four ends that F4's look past the end of the log reports
    as damage.

  Each file the check leaves opens again unchanged, and takes the next
  commit after its last batch. Held by another open file, the lock keeps
  Open from checking, and every file stays as it was.
- **A crash point replays.** With `Options.ID` set, a six-commit workload
  run twice with the power cut in the same write, for each write and 4
  seeds, left the same file byte for byte. With random IDs, the files
  differed.
- **The planted bugs are caught.**

- **Bugs put in by hand,** one at a time: of 19, the tests caught 17. Among
  them: no sync after the rewrite; the marked batch kept from the Target;
  the log not moved on past it; the marker left unwritten; the rewrite
  covering half the batch; a failed write's bytes checked by its own Log;
  no reading on before the check; Open never checking; a whole marker
  naming another batch taken for a torn one; nothing cut; a read error
  taken for the end of the file; the compacted part cut into; the Target's
  damage wrapped again; `Options.ID` ignored. Two of those, the marker
  taken for a torn one and nothing cut, were planted bugs at first.
  `TestTheCheckOfTheEndOfTheLog` catches both, and they're left out of
  `plants.txt` to keep the planted job short. Open's try without the check

  - the cut left unsynced. The next check cuts again whatever a power cut

    starts a sector, at 1 position in 512.

## Decisions settled

**The check,** holding the write lock, once the log has been read to its
end:

1. **Step 1 is F4's,** at the top of `checkEnd`. One part of it is there
   already: in a compacted file whose log ends before the compacted part
   does, the check reports damage and changes nothing, since FORMAT.md says
   nothing before the end of the compacted part is ever cut. The look past
   the end of the log for markers is left to F4.
2. **A batch that counts after the end** is written again in place, with
   the marker before it, then synced, then marked, then handed to the
   Target. A batch that doesn't count is left for the cut.
   - The marker before it is written again too. It was written after the
     last sync that worked, so after a failed sync its page can be marked
     clean without reaching the drive, as the batch's can. If a power cut
     then took it, the next check would find the batch before it unmarked,
     mark that, and cut this batch off, which a reader may have seen.
     `logfile/no-marker-again` shows it happening. FORMAT.md doesn't say
     this yet; Notes has wording.
   - The batch and the marker before it go in one write, from the bytes
     the file holds. When there's no batch before, the header comes before
     it, and it's synced at creation and never written again.
   - A whole marker naming another batch, by sequence number or checksum,
     is damage, and nothing is written. Any other bytes where the marker
     goes, or none, count as a torn marker.
   - Changes that are malformed, or break the rules for a change on its
     own, are the codec's damage, found before anything is written.
   - The Target gets the batch once it's marked, as every reader gets
     batches, so nothing in memory ever holds a batch that isn't on the
     drive. That puts the rules for the state after the marker: a batch
     that counts but breaks them is marked, then refused, and reported as
     damage, as every open after it reports it. Such a batch can't come
     from a crash or from this build's writer, whose store checks every
     change first. The other order would have the Target hold a batch that
     a failed sync can leave off the drive, with no way to take it back.
   - A batch whose marker is whole and right can't be found here, since
     reading on under the lock applies it. It would be written again and
     marked, which changes nothing.
3. **The cut** comes only when bytes are left after the last marker, and
   then it's synced. When the marker the check writes ends the file,
   nothing is cut, and the marker needs no sync of its own.

**Who runs it.**

- **The holder of the write lock:** Lock, in `lockFile`, after the inode
  check, the check that what was read is still there, and the leftovers.
  A batch the check marks reaches the Target before the transaction runs.
- **Opening,** when it gets the lock without waiting: `tryCheck`, at the
  end of Open's loop, then flock is let go. It checks nothing in an empty
  file, or when another file has taken the path since Open opened it, or
  nothing is there: the next Lock deals with those. Damage it finds fails
  Open, and so does any other error, a failed write or sync included.
- **A reader that finds the writer gone** is F6's, and calls `tryCheck`
  holding the mutex.

**Until F4,** the check cuts what doesn't count, whatever lies beyond it:
a marked batch that fails its checksum, a marked batch with the wrong
sequence number or generation, and a whole marker past zeros. Those four
are F4's in `endCases`, marked `f4`. F2.md's note gave "a marker naming the
batch before" to F4 as well; it's a batch that counts followed by a whole
marker naming another, so it's step 2's damage, and the check reports it.

**A failure in the check.** A failed write, sync, marker or cut goes through
`failed`, as a failed commit's does, and keeps F2's handling until F5: it
sets `tail`, nothing is cut, and Lock lets go of flock and fails with an
error saying the outcome is unknown. Then `catchUp` leaves those bytes
alone in this Log: while the log hasn't moved past where it ended when the
failure came, Append refuses with an error wrapping `errors.ErrUnsupported`,
and no check runs. Another Log's check deals with them as with anything a
crash leaves, cutting them, or marking a batch that counts, since a failed
commit's outcome is unknown. Once the log has moved on, the first Log
checks and appends again. Without this, a Log could mark its own failed
batch after its store had put the changes back.

**A fixed database ID.** `Options.ID`, all zeros by default, which means 16
random bytes. It's for tests: the crash tests set it, so a seed draws the
same tears in every run. The `.new-` names stay random, since the disk
draws nothing from names.

**The damage named once.** `refused` takes a `*errs.Damage` from the Target
with `errors.As`, sets its path and adds the batch's offset, and returns
the Target's error; any other error from the Target is wrapped in damage of
the log's own, as before. The codec's damage goes through the same
`placed`.

**The crash tests' workloads.** Each commit is `m.Begin`, Lock, Append,
`m.Done` once Append has returned, then Unlock, as T3.md set out. The state
is a line for each batch the Target was handed. The ends a writer leaves
when it dies are written by Setup and synced, so no cut can take them, and
Setup's state holds a batch that counts, since the check always marks it.
Commit i's batch holds 1 to 1,300 letters, as in the toy log, so batches
and markers fall across sectors in many ways. Copies run only on the plain
workload: one taken while the check cuts and writes the end again can hold
two versions, which FORMAT.md's "Copies" says reads as damage once F4 is
in.

**Why the closing test misses `logfile/marker-before-sync`.** The bug can
leave a whole marker after a batch a cut tore. Until F4 the check cuts
both, which the model allows for a commit under way, so in 24,600 cuts from
600 seeds no cut caught it. With a rough look past the end of the log put
into the check by hand for a trial, the closing test caught it at
`cut WriteAt 6 seed 22`, and five of its six workloads within 7 seeds, and
without the bug the trial raised no damage. So a new database gets 128
seeds, ready for F4, and the other workloads 32.

## Notes for later tasks

- **F4, damage:** step 1 goes at the top of `checkEnd`, before `markLeft`
  writes anything. When it lands, the four `f4` cases in `endCases` become
  damage, and the closing test catches `logfile/marker-before-sync` by
  cuts too; the trial above suggests it does by seed 22. The compacted-part
  rule is in `checkEnd` already, for holders of the lock; readers need it
  too. Copies of a file whose end a check cuts and writes again can then
  read as damage, which is why the copies test runs on the plain workload
  alone.
- **F5, failed commits:** `failed` is the one place for Append's failures
  and the check's. What changes:
  - the cut back to just after the last marker, and its sync, and `stuck`
    when that fails. The skip in `catchUp` and the refusal in Append go
    then, and `TestAFailedCommitStopsAppends` and
    `TestAFailedCheckIsHandledLikeAFailedCommit` change with them;
  - write the last marker again before syncing the cut, as `markLeft` does.
    A failed sync can leave its page off the drive while reads see it, so
    a later commit's sync wouldn't make it last, and a power cut after
    that commit would lose it, and the next check would cut the commit off;
  - a failure while the check writes a batch again: "handled like a failed
    commit" would cut a batch whose commit may have succeeded, since its
    sync worked before a power cut took its marker. Keeping the lock, or
    leaving the batch for the next check, keeps it. F5 settles which, and
    FORMAT.md follows;
  - `TestTheRealLogAfterAFailedCall` is the place to add a workload that
    carries on after a failed commit, and `Times: -1`.
- **F6, following:** `tryCheck` is the reader's try at the lock without
  waiting, made holding the mutex when reading on stops short of the end
  of the file.
- **G1:** Open can now hand the Target one batch beyond the marked ones,
  the one its check marks, and can fail with the check's damage or an I/O
  error. Lock can hand the Target a batch the check marked before the
  transaction runs. A `*errs.Damage` from `ApplyBatch` is the log's error
  as it comes, with the path and offset filled in. After a failed commit,
  a reopen can find the commit's changes: a failed sync leaves a batch that
  counts, and the reopen's check marks it, since the outcome is unknown.
  A test that reopens after a failed commit has to allow both until F5.
- **T4 and I1:** a writer killed mid-commit leaves what
  `TestAWriterThatDiedLeavesItsBatchForTheNext` makes by hand, and the next
  holder of the lock marks or cuts it.
- **R1, long runs:** `HYPERCRUX_CRASH_SEEDS` raises every workload's seeds
  in both driver tests.
- **The coordinator:**
