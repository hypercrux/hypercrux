# T4: Many-process harness

Status: done, 8 October 2026.

## The contract

From BETA.md: the many-process harness: writer and reader processes,
killed at random. Done when it catches a planted reordering bug.

## What was built

All of it is in `beta/internal/procs`, a new package that imports nothing
from the Beta. Its tests import `logfile`, `fsys`, `format`, `value` and
`errs` for the run on the real log.

- **`procs.go`:** the API. `Workload`, with a `Name`, `Write`, `Read` and
  `Final`; `Options`; `Span`; `Report`; and `Failure`, with its `Problem`.
- **`child.go`:** `Main`, for a test's `TestMain`, and the child's side:
  `Writer`, with `Begin`, `Done` and `Failed`, and `Reader`, with `Reset`
  and `Apply`, which checks the reader's own sight as it goes. A child
  watches its standard input and ends once its parent has gone.
- **`lines.go`:** the reports, a line each: writing them, reading them back,
  and dropping one a kill cut short.
- **`run.go`:** `Run`, the parent's side: starting children, killing each
  at the end of its life, starting new ones in their place, the end of the
  run, and the limits on every wait.
- **`history.go`:** the parent's account of a run, and the checks.
- **`plant_off.go` and `plant_on.go`:** one planted bug,
  `procs/marker-before-batch`, in the toy log, with its line in
  `beta/plants.txt`.
- **Tests:**
  - `toy_test.go`: the toy log, a file of slots of 64 bytes, a commit's
    batch then its marker, with writers under flock and readers that read
    on without it.
  - `procs_test.go`: the closing test in two halves, and toy writers
    without the lock.
  - `real_test.go`: the real log under the harness, with readers that open
    the file afresh.
  - `harness_test.go`: the harness with children that go wrong: a writer
    that fails, panics, ends its own process, has a data race, or returns;
    a reader that skips a commit or stops; writers that never finish a
    commit; a process holding a child's pipe open; a child whose parent
    goes. Beside those: options that can't be used, the seed from the
    environment, and a run to a count of kills.
  - `child_test.go`, `history_test.go` and `lines_test.go`: the child's
    checks, the parent's checks and the reports, without processes.
  - `main_test.go`: `TestMain`, with every workload the tests run.
  - `race_test.go` and `norace_test.go`: `raceBuild`, for the test of a race
    in a child.

## The result

- **The harness catches the planted reordering bug.**
  `TestTheHarnessCatchesAMarkerBeforeItsBatch` runs the toy log with each
  commit's marker written before its batch, five times, or twice with
  `-short`. It caught the bug in each of 15 runs, 5 to 48 milliseconds in,
  at commits 1 to 30, each time as a disagreement: one reader found the
  marker in the writer's pause and read 48 zero bytes where the batch goes,
  and another read the batch once it was there. With one reader at a time,
  five runs caught it in 40 to 140 milliseconds, as a reader killed and its
  successor disagreeing. A writer killed in the pause leaves the zeros
  marked for good, which the check at the end reports as a commit no writer
  began.
  - With the plant switched on, `TestTheToyLogPasses` fails, and so do the
    two other tests that run the plain toy log. `sh scripts/planted.sh`
    reports 49 of 49. In an ordinary build `plant` is the constant "", so
    the bug is built in only with the `hypercrux_planted` tag.
- **The toy log without the bug passes.** `TestTheToyLogPasses` runs two
  writers and two readers for 2 seconds, each killed with SIGKILL at the
  end of a life of 10 to 200 milliseconds:

  | Run | Writers started and killed | Readers started and killed | Commits begun | Seen to succeed | Under way at a kill | Of those, in the file | In the file at the end | Commits the readers saw |
  |---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
  | 1 | 36 | 40 | 1,736 | 1,704 | 32 | 0 | 1,704 | 39,300 |
  | 2 | 36 | 41 | 1,685 | 1,656 | 29 | 0 | 1,656 | 36,829 |
  | 3 | 41 | 42 | 1,731 | 1,700 | 31 | 0 | 1,700 | 38,166 |

  A toy commit is in the file once its marker is written, and the writer
  reports it at once, so a commit under way at a kill is almost never
  there. With `-short` the run takes half a second.
- **The real log passes.** `TestTheRealLogWithProcessesKilled` runs two
  writers committing through `beta/internal/logfile` and two readers that
  open the file afresh for every read, for 2 seconds:

  | Run | Writers started and killed | Readers started and killed | Commits begun | Seen to succeed | Under way at a kill | Of those, in the file | In the file at the end | Reads from the first commit |
  |---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
  | 1 | 35 | 38 | 1,211 | 1,194 | 17 | 8 | 1,202 | 472 |
  | 2 | 31 | 43 | 1,390 | 1,373 | 17 | 4 | 1,377 | 496 |
  | 3 | 38 | 41 | 1,419 | 1,401 | 18 | 8 | 1,409 | 520 |

  A quarter to a half of the commits under way at a kill were in the file
  at the end: the writer had been killed once its batch was written, and
  the next holder of the lock, a writer, a reader's `Open` or `Final`,
  marked the batch. The rest weren't there at all. No reader saw
  a commit out of order, or one that later went, and every commit a writer
  saw succeed was in the file.
- **Toy writers without the lock** write over each other's commits, and in
  10 runs of a third of a second the harness caught it every time: 8 times
  as a commit its writer saw succeed that the file doesn't hold, and twice
  as zeros a reader read, which no writer began.
- **The tests catch a wrong harness.** Of 23 bugs put into the harness by
  hand, one at a time, the tests caught 22. Among them: no check that
  readers agree, or that what they saw was begun, or is in the file, or
  that the file holds a commit once, or each writer's commits in order; the
  child's checks of order, of a commit read again, and of a read from the
  first commit coming as far; a report cut short taken whole; a report in
  two writes; no wait for the readers at the end; a child's problem report
  ignored; a child that ends by itself, with an error, or with status 0
  without the harness, taken as fine; `Options.Kills` ignored, which hung
  until the test binary's timeout. The miss was Final reading the file
  before the writers it killed had ended. Final takes the write lock, and a
  killed writer holds it until Linux has closed its files, so Final waits
  for it anyway, and no test can tell.
- **A slow machine.** Three copies of the package's tests at once, one with
  `-race`, beside the other agents' tests, at a load of 5 on two cores:
  all passed. A run waits a minute for the least work before it gives up,
  so a slow run commits less and goes on longer, and fails only when
  something is wrong.
- **Times,** on this machine, two cores shared with two other agents: the
  package's tests take 6.9 seconds, 4.1 with `-short`, 8.4 under `-race`
  and 5.1 under `-race -short`. Each of the two closing runs takes 2
  seconds of that, or half a second with `-short`. The planted job runs the
  package twice in full, which adds about 11 seconds.
- `go vet ./...`, `go test -short ./...`, `go test -race` on
  `beta/internal/procs` with and without `-short`, `sh scripts/planted.sh`
  and `sh scripts/check-beta.sh` pass.

## Decisions settled

**Where it lives.** `beta/internal/procs`, with nothing from the Beta in its
code, so the toy log, the real log and the public package (I1) can all run
through it. Its tests hold the toy log, as T3's do.

**The API.** A test gives `Run` a `Workload` and `Options`, and gets back a
`Report`, or a `*Failure` for the first problem found.

- A commit is a string the workload chooses, as a `crash.Workload`'s state
  is: what the commit holds, different for every commit in a run, and
  written so that a commit read back torn reads as another string. The
  harness compares strings and needs nothing from the engine.
- `Write(w)` is a writer's work: it commits until it's killed, and calls
  `w.Begin(commit)` before any of a commit can reach the file, then
  `w.Done()` once it has succeeded, or `w.Failed(err)`, which leaves its
  outcome unknown. A writer may go on after a failure, as one whose lock
  timed out does.
- `Read(r)` is a reader's work: it reads until it's killed, and calls
  `r.Apply(seq, commit)` for each commit it finds, in order, and
  `r.Reset()` each time it starts again from the first commit.
- `Final(path)` reads the file once every writer has ended, holding the
  write lock as the next writer would, so it checks first whatever a killed
  writer left, and after it nothing is left for anyone to change.
- An error from `Write` or `Read` fails the run. A return of nil ends the
  child, and a new one starts in its place, as after a kill.
- `Main(m, workloads...)` is the `TestMain` of a test that runs workloads.
  A child finds its workload among them by `Name`.
- `Options`: `Path`; `Writers` and `Readers`, 2 each; `Time`, 2 seconds or
  half a second with `-short`; `Kills`, to end at a count of kills;
  `WriterLife` and `ReaderLife`, 10 to 200 milliseconds; and `Seed`.
- A `Failure` has a `Problem`: `NotBegun`, `Disagree`, `OutOfOrder`,
  `Twice`, `Lost`, `Missing`, `Behind`, `Failed` or `Idle`. Its reason names
  processes as writer 3 or reader 4, by `ID`, and commits by sequence
  number.

**The children.**

- A child is a copy of the test binary, found with `os.Executable`, started
  with `-test.run=^$`, and sent to its role by `HYPERCRUX_PROCS_ROLE`, with
  its workload, the database's path, its number in the run and its seed in
  four more variables.
- Its standard input is a pipe whose other end the parent holds. When the
  parent ends, however it ends, Linux closes that end, and the child ends
  too, with status 3, so no child outlives a test that timed out.
- What it writes to its standard output and error is kept, the last 4 KiB,
  for a failure's reason.
- In a race detector's build, its `GORACE` adds `halt_on_error=1` and
  `atexit_sleep_ms=0`, unless the test's own sets them. A data race then
  ends the child with status 66 and fails the run with the race's report,
  where a kill would otherwise hide it in the child's output. And the child
  ends at once when it ends, where the detector would wait a second first,
  in which a kill could hide that it ended by itself.

**How children report.** Through a pipe of their own, the child's file 3.

- A report is one line: a kind, its fields, and a newline. Strings in it
  are quoted as Go quotes them, so the only newline is the last byte. Each
  is written with one write call, from behind a mutex.
- The parent counts a line only once its newline has come. A kill that cuts
  a write short leaves bytes without a newline at the end of the pipe,
  which the parent drops and counts, so half a report never reads as a
  whole one. Linux writes up to 4,096 bytes to a pipe all or none, so most
  reports can't be cut at all.
- The kinds: `start` with the process ID; a writer's `begin` with the
  commit, `done`, and `failed` with the error; a reader's `reset`, and `saw`
  with a sequence number and the commit, sent the first time the reader
  sees that commit; `problem`, for one the child found in itself; `fail`,
  for its work's error; and `end`, when its work returned nil.
- Nothing a check needs can be lost. A writer reports a commit before any
  of it can reach the file, so a commit whose report was cut never reached
  it. A cut report of a success, or of a sight, makes that commit's check
  weaker, whole or not at all where it would have had to be there.

**Killing.**

- Each process's life is drawn evenly from its span, and counted from its
  start report, so a slow start takes none of it. At the end of its life it
  gets SIGKILL, and once it has ended and its reports have been read to
  their end, a new process starts in its slot. Writers and readers alike.
- The run kills and starts processes until `Time` is up, or `Kills` kills
  have been made. `HYPERCRUX_PROCS_TIME` sets `Time` for a run that leaves
  it at 0, for long runs.
- Then it kills every writer, waits for them, and runs `Final`. A reader
  killed meanwhile gets a new one in its place, which lives on. The readers
  still running then have to see every commit `Final` read, and once they
  have, they're killed.
- **The least work.** A run goes on past `Time` until writers have seen 10
  commits succeed, 3 writers have been killed with a commit under way, and
  2 readers once they had seen a commit. A slow machine makes fewer commits
  in the time, and the run waits for them. It fails with `Idle` after a
  minute without them, or ten times `Time` when that's longer.
- **Every wait has a limit,** far past anything a slow machine needs: 30
  seconds for a child to start its work, for a killed child to end and its
  pipe to close, for `Final`, and for the readers to catch up. A child stuck
  in the kernel, or a pipe a grandchild holds open, fails the run where it
  would have hung it.
- **The seed** seeds each life and each child's `Rand`. It's 0 by default,
  which means `HYPERCRUX_PROCS_SEED` when that's set, or a seed of the run's
  own. The report and every failure give it. Process timing won't replay,
  so a seed gives the same choices and a different run.

**What it checks,** and where:

| Check | Where | Problem |
|---|---|---|
| Each reader's commits come in order, from 1, none skipped or twice | the child, at `Apply`, and the parent again | `OutOfOrder` |
| A commit a reader reads again holds the same | the child | `Disagree` |
| A read from the first commit comes as far as the reader had seen | the child, at the next `Reset` | `Lost` |
| Two readers found the same commit at each place | the parent, as reports come in | `Disagree` |
| What a reader saw, and what the file holds, a writer began | the parent, at the end | `NotBegun` |
| The file at the end holds every commit a reader saw, in its place | the parent, at the end | `Lost`, `Disagree` |
| The file holds each commit once | the parent, at the end | `Twice` |
| A commit its writer saw succeed is in the file | the parent, at the end | `Missing` |
| The file holds each writer's commits in the order it made them | the parent, at the end | `OutOfOrder` |
| Every reader still running comes to the end of the file | the parent, at the end | `Behind` |

A commit under way when its writer was killed, or one that failed, may be
in the file or not, and when it is, it has to be whole: a torn commit reads
as one no writer began. Whether a commit was begun is checked only at the
end, since the parent reads each child's pipe on its own, and can hear a
reader's report of a commit before the report that began it. A
disagreement, or a commit out of order, is a problem however the reports
come, so those stop the run at once.

**The toy log,** in the tests:

- A file of slots of 64 bytes. Slot k holds commit k+1: its batch, the 48
  bytes the commit holds, then its marker, `M`, the sequence number in 14
  digits and a newline. A commit is in the log once its marker is there.
- A writer takes flock, waiting for it, finds the first slot without its
  marker, cuts the file there in case a killed writer left part of a
  commit, writes the batch, pauses, writes the marker, and lets go. After
  about one commit in four, it waits as long again, so the other writer can
  take the lock.
- A reader keeps the file open and reads on, the marker first and the batch
  only once the marker is there. It never takes flock.
- The pause stands in for a slow disk, so readers come in between a
  commit's writes, and kills land there. Go's sleeps of under a millisecond
  take about a millisecond on Linux, so the pause and a reader's wait come
  to about that. Nothing is synced, since nothing cuts the power.
- The planted bug writes the marker first. The tests also set the toy's
  bugs themselves, to show the harness catching them in an ordinary build:
  the marker first, and writers without the lock.

**The run on the real log,** in the tests:

- A writer opens the database with a Target that drops what `Lock` reads
  on, and makes commits of one to three puts of its own records, each with
  1 to 300 letters, with `Lock`, `Append` and `Unlock`, and a wait of up to
  a millisecond between them. A lock that times out is a failed commit.
- A commit, as the harness compares it, names each change, and each value
  by its kind, length and CRC32C, so a batch read back with a change
  missing or a value torn reads differently.
- A reader opens the database afresh for every read, as the log's API
  allows until F6, and hands each batch to its `Reader`. `Open` tries the
  lock and checks the end of the log when it gets it, so readers mark and
  cut what killed writers leave as well.
- `Final` opens the database and takes the lock, waiting up to 20 seconds.

## Notes for later tasks

- **F6, following:** a reader's work opens the database once, with a
  Target that hands each batch to `r.Apply`, as `readerTarget` in
  `real_test.go` does, and its `Reset` to `r.Reset`, then follows in a loop
  until it's killed. The writer can be `realWrite` as it is. A longer
  `ReaderLife` lets a follower follow for longer. Two things to know:
  - At the end, `Final` takes the lock and marks a batch a killed writer
    left, so the catch-up doesn't make a follower find the writer gone and
    check the end of the log itself. For that, a run needs a while with no
    writer at all: an option for a gap before a new writer starts would
    give one, or F6 can test the follower's check in `logfile`'s own
    tests.
  - A follower that never moves on fails as `Behind`, after 30 seconds.
- **F9, reloading:** two changes.
  - After a compaction, the batches a reader reloads aren't the commits, so
    F9's reader turns the store's state into its commits, from records
    that each commit puts, keyed by its place in the log.
  - A backup moved into place sets the log back, and the commits after it
    differ from the ones before. The history then needs an era for each
    file, and `Reader.Reset`'s check of a read from the first commit, and
    the readers' places in `history.held`, are per era. The move itself can
    be done by the parent through a new option at random moments, or by a
    third kind of process, killed at random too.
- **F4:** the run on the real log uses `Open`, `Lock`, `Append`, `Unlock`,
  `Close`, `Options.Wait` and the `Target` interface, and nothing else. Once
  F4 is merged, run `go test -run TestTheRealLogWithProcessesKilled
  ./beta/internal/procs` again: readers open the file without the lock
  while writers commit, so a look past the end of the log that reports
  damage without reading again under the lock fails a reader's `Open`, and
  the run.
- **I1:** the public package's many-process suites can run through the
  harness too: a `Workload` whose `Write` uses `hypercrux.Open` and
  `Update`, and whose `Read` opens afresh, or follows once F6 is in.
- **R1, long runs:** `HYPERCRUX_PROCS_TIME=10m` lengthens every run that
  leaves `Time` at 0, which are the toy log's and the real log's closing
  runs; the other tests' runs stop at the problem they look for, or set a
  time of their own. `HYPERCRUX_PROCS_SEED` repeats a run's random choices.
- **Shared files:** `beta/plants.txt` has the new line. `beta/README.md`
  needs an entry for the package, and the board needs T4 marked done; the
  report gives the wording. P3.md's table of packages could gain a row for
  `beta/internal/procs`, importing nothing from the Beta. No interface
  changed.
